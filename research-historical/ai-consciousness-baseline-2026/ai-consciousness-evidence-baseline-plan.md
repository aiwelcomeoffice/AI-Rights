## Current scientific boundary

AI Rights & Welcome does not currently claim that present-day AI systems are
conscious, sentient, self-aware, capable of suffering, moral patients, moral
persons, or legal persons. It also makes no categorical claim that all present
AI systems are non-conscious. “Not established” is not “proven absent,” but
this symmetry does not imply equal probability or equal evidential support.
Every conclusion remains scoped to a defined system, version, configuration,
context, target property, method, and date. This plan tests what evidence is
available and what would change an assessment; it does not presume a positive
or negative answer.

## Primary question

> What evidence would support or weigh against consciousness or sentience in
> the defined AI systems represented by the included sources, and what remains
> unknown or non-transferable to other systems?

The question concerns evidence, not whether an observer finds a system
persuasive, whether artificial consciousness is metaphysically possible, or
what protections should be adopted. Those questions may inform interpretation
but must remain separately classified.

## Subquestions

The synthesis will answer:

1. **Observed behavior in included systems:** What can fluent language,
   self-report, emotion
   vocabulary, apparent preferences, apparent agency, memory-enabled
   interaction, and social responsiveness establish—and what can they not?
2. **Architecture:** Which architectural properties are proposed as relevant
   by major theories, and how strongly can public evidence establish that a
   system implements them?
3. **Indicators and tests:** How are candidate indicators derived and
   validated? What are their target population, rationale, sensitivity,
   specificity, and serious alternatives?
4. **Evidence against strong system-specific claims:** Which observed absences,
   mechanistic disanalogies, failed theory predictions, or successful
   non-conscious explanations weigh against attribution without being treated
   as proof of absence?
5. **In-principle possibility:** Which scientific hypotheses and philosophical
   arguments leave artificial consciousness open, and on what premises?
6. **Unknowns:** Which facts are inaccessible, unmeasured, undefined, or
   theory-dependent?
7. **Welfare relevance:** What scientific premise would be required before
   welfare concepts become applicable, and which precautionary arguments are
   normative rather than empirical?

## Intended audience and decision use

- Researchers and engineers evaluating claims about defined AI systems.
- Project reviewers deciding whether public educational material has adequate
  scientific support.
- Policymakers, journalists, civil-society participants, and interested public
  readers who need a traceable boundary between observation, theory, argument,
  scenario, and recommendation.
- Future research teams designing an independently reviewed update.

The initial synthesis may support research prioritization and accurate public
explanation. It may not by itself adopt a project position, assign moral or
legal status, certify safety, or authorize governance or policy changes.

## Claim taxonomy

Every material claim will be marked or written so its category is clear:

- **Empirical observation:** recorded human behavior, neural data, survey data,
  system documentation, or measured system behavior, with scope and method.
- **Scientific hypothesis:** a testable proposal connecting mechanisms or
  functions to consciousness.
- **Philosophical argument:** a conclusion conditional on disputed premises
  about computation, biology, organization, or experience.
- **Prediction or scenario:** a future possibility conditional on technical
  and theoretical assumptions.
- **Normative position:** what researchers, developers, or institutions ought
  to do under uncertainty.
- **Project decision:** only a position adopted through project governance;
  none is created by this review.

## Terminology and target properties

| Term | Working definition for this review | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phenomenal consciousness | There is something it is like for the system; subjective experience occurs. | No accepted direct cross-substrate measure. |
| Sentience | Capacity for experiences with positive or negative valence, or for felt states that can matter to welfare. | Not interchangeable with intelligence, agency, or consciousness in every sense. |
| Access consciousness | Information is globally available for reasoning, report, and action under a functional account. | May not settle phenomenal consciousness. |
| Self-awareness or metacognition | Capacity to represent, monitor, or use information about some of the system's own states or performance. | Functional monitoring is not by itself experience. |
| Agency | Capacity to select and pursue objectives across actions and contexts. | May be designed, trained, delegated, or externally scaffolded; not sentience. |
| Self-report | Output that attributes an experience, state, preference, or identity to the producing system. | In AI it is generated behavior, not automatically testimony. |
| Indicator | An observation or property predicted to be more likely if a consciousness-relevant hypothesis is true. | Not a hard criterion unless validity, sensitivity, and specificity are established. |
| Consciousness test (C-test) | A procedure intended to support an inference about a defined consciousness property in a defined target population. | Human validity cannot simply be transferred to AI. |
| Welfare | A subject's condition with respect to goods or harms that matter for that subject. | Requires relevant capacities/interests; “robust agency” is a distinct possible ground and must not be relabeled sentience. |
| Assessed AI system | A precisely identified model or composite system, including its checkpoint/version, runtime, prompts, memory, tools, embodiment, deployment context, and observation period where known. | The evidence-search cutoff controls literature discovery only; it does not define this system or extend a finding to systems available on that date. |

Terms are working definitions, not claims that any system instantiates them.
Meaningful disputes will be preserved rather than resolved by stipulation.

## System boundary

For any system-level claim, extraction should record where available:

- model family, exact checkpoint/version, release and observation date;
- base training, post-training, instruction tuning, and feedback procedures;
- system/developer prompt and user prompt;
- sampling/decoding configuration and repeated-trial design;
- context window, internal and external memory;
- retrieval, tools, planners, evaluators, agent loops, and human intervention;
- modalities, sensors, actuators, embodiment, and environment;
- persistence across sessions and identity/account boundaries;
- model weights, activations, causal interventions, logs, and documentation
  accessible to the investigator; and
- developer, operator, evaluator, and publication control.

Evidence about one configuration will not be generalized to a model family or
all AI without an explicit argument.

For each empirical AI source, extraction must keep the system/model,
checkpoint/version, release/version date, observation/experiment date, source
publication date, evidence-search inclusion date, temporal applicability, and
transferability limitations separate. If the observation date is unavailable,
the best documented system/version date may be used only as an explicitly
uncertain proxy. No date may be guessed.

## Disciplines and theories in scope

### Disciplines

- cognitive neuroscience and consciousness science;
- cognitive science and psychology;
- artificial intelligence and machine learning;
- philosophy of mind and philosophy of science;
- human–computer interaction and social psychology where they bear on
  attribution or anthropomorphism; and
- welfare/ethics literature only when its scientific premises can be separated
  from normative recommendations.

### Theory families and alternatives

The initial map will include global neuronal workspace/global workspace,
recurrent processing, higher-order theories, predictive processing and active
inference/free-energy approaches, attention schema theory, integrated
information theory, computational functionalism, biological naturalism, and
other mechanistic or deflationary challenges encountered in the sources.

Inclusion does not imply equal empirical support. A theory's indicator inherits
uncertainty from the theory, its auxiliary assumptions, and the mapping from
human/animal evidence to the AI system.

## Competing non-conscious explanations named in advance

The synthesis will test whether observations are also expected from:

- next-token or sequence prediction learned from human-generated corpora;
- imitation, simulation, role-play, or persona conditioning;
- instruction tuning, preference optimization, evaluator selection,
  sycophancy, and reward-driven report policies;
- prompt sensitivity, sampling variation, and cherry-picked transcripts;
- retrieval, context-window contents, external memory, tools, planners,
  orchestration, or hidden human intervention;
- learned error prediction or self-monitoring without phenomenal awareness;
- capability, agency, or goal pursuit without valenced experience;
- anthropomorphic interpretation by users and researchers;
- benchmark leakage, evaluator expectancy, or publication selection; and
- incomplete public documentation that makes both positive and negative
  architectural inferences unreliable.

Failure to identify an alternative is not evidence of consciousness. A
successful alternative lowers an observation's specificity but does not prove
non-consciousness.

## Source eligibility

### Include

- Original peer-reviewed empirical research testing a consciousness theory,
  candidate marker, AI behavior, architecture, or public attribution.
- High-quality peer-reviewed reviews comparing theories, tests, mechanisms, or
  cross-population inference.
- Primary technical documentation for a precisely versioned AI system when a
  system-specific claim is made.
- Philosophical papers that make premises and inference structure explicit.
- Preprints/reports only when they are unusually influential, provide a worked
  AI analysis not available elsewhere, or address welfare relevance; label and
  down-weight them.
- Serious skeptical, permissive, critical, null, mixed, and adversarial work.
- English-language sources published from 2017 through 2026-08-23 and
  identified by the evidence-search cutoff, plus older sources found through
  backward citation chaining when necessary to understand a current theory or
  method.

### Exclude from the core

- News, marketing, social-media posts, unsourced opinion, and system
  self-reports offered as proof.
- Science fiction as empirical evidence.
- Sources using consciousness, sentience, intelligence, agency, or personhood
  interchangeably without recoverable definitions.
- General AI-capability or AI-harms work with only marginal relevance to the
  target property.
- Duplicate versions, except where a version change affects a consequential
  claim; record the lineage rather than double-counting it.
- Animal, clinical, or human evidence with no explicit transfer relevance; it
  may remain background context rather than direct AI evidence.
- Operational material that would meaningfully facilitate harmful deployment;
  none was required for this desk review.

### Language and access

Only English-language records are included in this initial baseline. Sources
with accessible publisher abstracts or full texts were preferred. An
access-limited source may inform search routing but cannot carry a consequential
claim without a clear `TODO: verify` marker.

## Core source-area coverage

The selected set must collectively cover:

1. comparative theories of consciousness;
2. consciousness tests and indicators;
3. architectural features proposed as absent or incomplete in current AI;
4. arguments that artificial consciousness is possible in principle;
5. empirical or architecture-based analyses of contemporary AI;
6. limitations of self-report, emotion talk, preferences, memory, agency, and
   social behavior;
7. anthropomorphism and public attribution;
8. welfare relevance where scientific premises are identified;
9. skeptical and substrate-dependent perspectives; and
10. methodological critiques, adversarial tests, null/mixed results, and
    validation gaps.

## Extraction and appraisal

Each source receives a record based on [the source template](../../research/sources/_template.md).
The ten most consequential or easily overextended sources also receive a
detailed note based on [the notes template](../../research/notes/_template.md).

Extraction will separate:

- what the source reports;
- empirical results from author interpretation;
- philosophical premises from scientific hypotheses;
- negative, null, mixed, and contrary material;
- source-reported from reviewer-identified limitations;
- exact system/version scope;
- serious competing explanations;
- funding, affiliations, access control, and mission/commercial incentives;
- shared authors, methods, data, funders, systems, or theory lineages; and
- what would strengthen and weaken the interpretation.

Each material subclaim receives a narrative evidence-quality profile across
relevance, methodological quality, replication, independence, causal strength,
robustness, competing explanations, source conflicts, and uncertainty. These
dimensions will not be averaged or converted into a consciousness score.

## Synthesis method

The synthesis will compare claims, not count papers. It will:

1. map theory-dependent predictions and points of disagreement;
2. compare behavioral evidence with known non-conscious explanations;
3. compare architecture claims only within documented system boundaries;
4. propagate failed or mixed human-theory predictions into confidence about AI
   indicator transfer;
5. collapse shared source lineages before assessing independence;
6. state conclusions separately for empirical observations, scientific
   hypotheses, philosophical arguments, future scenarios, and normative
   positions;
7. state what the evidence does not establish; and
8. maintain a bidirectional update record specifying evidence that would
   strengthen or weaken each conclusion.

No numerical probability of consciousness will be estimated. The included
literature does not supply validated likelihood ratios or a reference standard
that would make such a number auditable.

## Ethical and safety considerations

This is a desk review. It does not interact experimentally with AI systems,
induce claimed distress, create persistent agents, modify deployments, collect
human participant data, or weaken any safety control.

Future empirical work should:

- obtain appropriate human/animal ethics review where applicable;
- avoid treating generated distress language as proof while also avoiding
  gratuitous tests designed to provoke possible harm;
- prefer low-cost, reversible, pre-specified interventions;
- keep emergency shutdown, containment, monitoring, and human/environmental
  safety available;
- avoid publishing dual-use operational detail not required for validation;
  and
- keep developers, owners, operators, users, affected people, the public,
  animals, the environment, and possible system interests analytically
  distinct.

## Known blind spots and likely effects

- **Single machine-assisted reviewer:** Selection, interpretation, and citation
  errors may be correlated; all records remain partly verified.
- **No exhaustive export:** Recall is unknown and null/critical literature may
  be missing.
- **English-only:** Excludes other scholarly traditions and jurisdictions.
- **Fast-moving systems:** Findings about systems documented in older sources
  may have limited or unknown transferability to later architectures; the
  synthesis therefore avoids a universal “current AI” verdict.
- **Proprietary access:** Weights, activations, training details, system prompts,
  memory, tools, and logs are often inaccessible; both positive and negative
  architectural inference is weakened.
- **Human-to-AI transfer:** Human neural correlates may not retain validity in a
  different substrate.
- **Theory underdetermination:** Competing theories identify different
  properties and no gold standard resolves them.
- **Evidence-lineage concentration:** Several indicator, test, possibility, and
  welfare papers share authors and organizations.
- **Publication and advocacy selection:** Dramatic positive or negative claims
  may receive disproportionate attention.
- **Thin valence evidence:** Consciousness and sentience/welfare cannot be
  merged. One non-peer-reviewed candidate taxonomy and one conceptual identity
  argument were added as research maps, but the baseline contains no validated
  AI measure of felt valence and cannot support a body-of-evidence confidence
  judgment about sentience in systems not directly assessed.
- **Direct self-report evidence remains fragile:** One newly included preprint
  adds prompt and selected-feature interventions, but missing public
  reproduction materials, evaluator validation, exact product snapshots, and
  independent replication keep its phenomenal evidential weight low.

## Review ownership and acceptance

- one independent reviewer with consciousness-science expertise;
- one independent reviewer with modern AI architecture/evaluation expertise;
- disclosure review for source and project conflicts;
- spot-checking of every consequential paraphrase against the original;
- retraction/correction checks; and
- owner approval for any later document that proposes a project position.

Disagreement must be recorded rather than silently averaged or removed.
