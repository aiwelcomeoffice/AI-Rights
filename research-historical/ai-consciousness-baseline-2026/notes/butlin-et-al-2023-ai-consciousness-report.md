## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

The report asks whether theory-derived indicator properties can be found in
selected AI architectures and what that implies (abstract, summary, and case
study sections).

### Methods or reasoning

It surveys several theory families, derives functional/architectural indicator
properties under computational functionalism, and qualitatively maps selected
2023 systems to them using public technical information.

### Reported findings

The authors conclude that their analysis did not indicate consciousness in the
systems reviewed. They also see no obvious technical barrier to building
systems satisfying the proposed indicators.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

Some systems partially match some indicators. Version 3 clarifies that even a
system satisfying indicators would not therefore certainly be conscious. The
report supplies neither a validated gold standard nor a direct measure of
experience.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

The negative assessment is conditional on the theories, indicator derivation,
system documentation, and functionalist assumptions. It is not an impossibility
claim.

## Researcher's interpretation

The report is a worked architectural audit, not an empirical detection study.
Its wording should be normalized to: “the authors did not find sufficient
matches under their framework in the systems and documentation reviewed.”

## Criticisms and methodological concerns

- Non-peer-reviewed qualitative expert coding with no validated reference
  outcome or blinded independent replication.
- Generalization to later models is invalid because the assessed system
  documentation is bounded to 2023, not because of this project's 2026 search
  date.
- Architectural descriptions may omit training, runtime, memory, tools, and
  deployment scaffolding.
- Later 2026 paper is same-lineage development, not replication.
