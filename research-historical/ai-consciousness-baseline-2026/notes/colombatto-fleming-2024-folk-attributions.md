## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

The study asks how U.S. adults attribute consciousness to ChatGPT and which
individual differences are associated with those judgments (abstract and
methods).

### Methods or reasoning

In July 2023, the authors surveyed 300 U.S. Prolific participants using age and
gender quotas. A single principal item recorded graded consciousness judgment;
other items measured LLM use and relevant attitudes. Materials, data, and code
are linked at <https://osf.io/49w7m>.

### Reported findings

In this sample, 67% assigned a non-zero possibility and 23% selected a response
above the scale midpoint under the authors' scale interpretation. More frequent
LLM use was associated with higher attribution.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

Most respondents were not above the midpoint. Cross-sectional association
cannot determine whether use changes beliefs, beliefs drive use, or a third
factor affects both.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

The study demonstrates that public consciousness attributions occur and merit
study; it does not treat those attributions as measurements of LLM experience.

## Quotations

No quotation extracted; numerical results are paraphrased with their sample and

## Researcher's interpretation

The study is evidence about observers, not AI. It shows why attribution,
judgment confidence, use/familiarity, and survey results must be separated from
scientific evidence about the target system. It did not test warmth, social
fluency, or participants' emotional response.

## Criticisms and methodological concerns

- Single U.S. online sample limits cultural and temporal generalization.
- The main construct uses one item and may be affected by question framing.
- A survey mentioning consciousness may prime the concept.
- Exact percentages should be independently reproduced from open data.

## Competing explanations

## Unanswered questions

- Do results replicate cross-culturally and after neutral definitions?
- Which interface and framing features cause attribution?
- Does evidence literacy improve calibration?

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen it:** Independent preregistered replications,
  multi-item measures, and randomized interface/framing studies.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** Failure to reproduce or severe scale/data
  errors.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve it:** A larger attribution percentage by
  itself.
- **Re-review triggers:** Replication, OSF revision, correction, or new
  longitudinal/experimental evidence.
