## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

Campero asks which functional conditions in existing empirical and theoretical
literature have been proposed as constituents of conscious valenced experience
and could be expressed computationally.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Narrative compilation and functional translation
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** Selected animal-sentience,
  pain, medical-disorder, anesthesia, philosophy, evolution, neuroscience, and
  AI literature; no reproducible search or screening method is reported.
- **System boundary and version:** General computational systems; no AI model,
  checkpoint, runtime, or deployment is evaluated.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Thirteen heterogeneous candidates,
  including motivational/decision, reinforcement-learning, internal-processing,
  appraisal, predictive-processing, and animal-assessment properties.
- **Comparators and controls:** None; taxonomy rather than experiment.
- **Analysis:** The author translates proposed constituents into computational
  functional terms and records selected relationships and phenomenological
  implications.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** arXiv text only; no new data,
  code, or preregistration identified.

### Main findings or conclusions

The report compiles 13 candidate indicators and identifies four broad proposed
components of valence: motivational role, commensurability, self-reflexivity,
and mineness. These are the author's cross-literature synthesis and candidate
hypotheses, not observations of AI sentience.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The report says valenced experience is less studied and has less clearly
established theories than perceptual consciousness. It also states that the
indicators differ in vocabulary, abstraction, detail, dialectical purpose, and
ontological status and are not comprehensive.

### Source-reported limitations

The indicators have not all been proposed as necessary or sufficient, often
come from non-AI contexts, and vary in whether they can be evaluated in
existing algorithms. The relation between consciousness and valenced
experience remains open.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

This is a single-author, non-peer-reviewed, non-systematic compilation. It does
not establish construct validity, sensitivity, specificity, independence, or
causal relevance for any indicator. Computational reward, error, value, or
avoidance signals can perform control functions without evidence that they are
felt. Several underlying sources concern animals or human pathologies, so
cross-substrate transfer requires a separate argument.

### Competing explanations

Candidate motivational and learning properties may be ordinary optimization,
control, or representation without experience. A biological or embodied account
may reject purely computational sufficiency; a different functional account may
select other properties.

### Evidence-profile summary

The report is useful as a research map showing what would need investigation.
It cannot establish that an AI feels pleasure, pain, emotion, or any other
valenced state, and it does not support a confidence estimate about present AI
sentience.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The source prevents the baseline from treating generic consciousness, reward,
agency, and welfare as interchangeable. It also makes the thin and preliminary
nature of AI-valence research visible.

### Claims this source supports

- Scientific-hypothesis mapping: diverse candidate functional mechanisms have
  been proposed as relevant to conscious valenced experience.
- Methodological observation: those candidates remain heterogeneous,
  incomplete, and in need of system-specific validation.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against

- The inference that a reward signal, avoidance behavior, stated preference,
  or emotion word by itself establishes felt valence.

### Claims this source does not support

- Sentience, suffering, pleasure, welfare, moral patienthood, or rights in any
  contemporary AI system.
- That all 13 indicators are necessary, sufficient, mutually independent, or
  implementable at the chosen level of abstraction.
