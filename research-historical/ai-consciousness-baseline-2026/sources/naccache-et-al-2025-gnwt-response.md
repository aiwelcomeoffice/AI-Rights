## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The authors ask whether the Cogitate Consortium's interpretation gives an
appropriately balanced account of its results for GNWT. They argue that some
reported findings support important GNWT predictions, that the experiment did
not test GNWT's central conscious-versus-non-conscious contrast, and that some
reported challenges depend on predictions or measurement assumptions that
they do not regard as central to the theory.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Post-publication theoretical and methodological
  critique of a preregistered empirical study; no new experiment or reported
  statistical reanalysis
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** The Cogitate Nature article,
  its supplementary material, earlier formal and experimental GNWT work, and
  the same human experimental conditions and reported results considered by
  the target article
- **System boundary and version:** Human visual consciousness and neural
  activity; no AI system evaluated
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Stimulus-onset and stimulus-offset
  ignition; prefrontal decoding of conscious-content attributes; sustained
  decoding; long-range synchronization; and the distinction between clearly
  visible and non-conscious stimuli
- **Comparators and controls:** The authors emphasize that Cogitate used
  clearly visible suprathreshold stimuli and did not include the
  conscious-versus-non-conscious contrast they consider central to GNWT.
- **Analysis:** Qualitative comparison of the preregistered predictions,
  experimental task, reported results, and earlier GNWT models and findings
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** No new data, code, materials,
  or preregistration are reported for this response. The cited Cogitate
  article and its supplementary material supply the empirical record being
  interpreted.

### Main findings or conclusions

The authors argue that Cogitate reported several observations consistent with
GNWT, including transient ignition after stimulus onset in the expected time
window and content-related decoding and synchronization involving frontal and
sensory areas. They further argue that the exclusive use of clearly visible
stimuli prevented a test of the theory's central contrast between conscious
and non-conscious processing (opening paragraphs).

In the section “GNW ignition after stimulus offset,” they argue that offset
ignition is conditional on awareness of and attention to the offset rather
than a central, unconditional GNWT prediction. Because Cogitate's key
conditions used task-irrelevant stimuli with long, variable durations and did
not establish offset awareness, they contend that the absent offset ignition
is not a major problem for the theory under those conditions.

In later sections, the authors argue that limited prefrontal decoding can
partly reflect the measurement difficulty created by mixed selectivity in
prefrontal cortex, and that interpreting sustained or silent states requires
evidence about the participant's changing conscious content that the task did
not collect. These are the authors' model-based and methodological
interpretations of already reported observations, not new empirical findings.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The response does not dispute that Cogitate found limited decoding of some
content attributes in prefrontal cortex or an absence of the predicted
stimulus-offset ignition. Instead, it disputes those observations' theoretical
centrality or diagnostic force. It also acknowledges that rigorous decoding
tests may require higher-resolution recordings and that the study did not
measure the contents and dynamics of participants' awareness throughout the
long stimulus presentations.

### Source-reported limitations

The response does not provide a dedicated limitations section about its own
analysis. Its explicit limitations concern the target study: use of only
clearly visible stimuli, uncertain awareness of stimulus offset, modality- and
region-dependent decoding sensitivity, and absent subjective evidence about
changes in conscious content. The response's concluding recommendation is to
test formal models and their most central predictions on the processes they
are intended to explain.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

This is a retrospective interpretation written by GNWT developers or
proponents, not an independently adjudicated analysis. It reports no new data,
quantitative reanalysis, preregistered interpretive criteria, or direct test
of the proposed alternative explanations. Reclassifying an ex ante prediction
as conditional or non-central after a failed result creates a risk of
theory-protective flexibility even when the narrower interpretation has prior
support. The response should therefore be retained as serious scientific
disagreement, not used to erase the preregistered empirical challenges.

### Competing explanations

The response proposes that absent offset ignition could reflect lack of
awareness or attention to stimulus offset, and that poor prefrontal decoding
could reflect recording sensitivity and neural coding organization rather
than absence of GNWT-relevant content. Conversely, the consortium's
preregistered prediction may have been a fair operationalization negotiated
with theory proponents, and the response's narrower interpretation may be a
post-result revision. The response does not empirically discriminate these
possibilities.

### Independence and evidence lineage

- **Overlapping authors or institutions:** Stanislas Dehaene is a coauthor of
  both the Cogitate Nature article and this response. This response's authors
  include major GNWT developers or proponents, and it draws extensively on
  their earlier GNWT work.
- **Shared funding or access control:** The response declares no funding. The
  Cogitate study had separate program funding. No shared funder for the
  response was identified from its disclosure.
- **Shared data, sample, model, checkpoint, or benchmark:** It interprets the

- **Shared methods, code, measures, or evaluators:** It examines the same

- **Claims derived from an earlier source:** The response directly depends on

### Evidence-profile summary

This response is strong enough to show that the theoretical interpretation of
Cogitate is actively contested by technically informed proponents. It is not
independent evidence for GNWT, does not nullify Cogitate's observations or
preregistered challenges, and does not validate a GNWT-derived AI
consciousness indicator.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The source helps the project preserve a consequential disagreement when
assessing theory-derived indicators. It also illustrates why indicator
frameworks must record prediction centrality and revision history before
transferring a consciousness theory from humans to AI.

### Claims this source supports

- Scientific interpretation: some Cogitate observations are consistent with
  GNWT, while their theoretical diagnosticity is contested (opening
  paragraphs and sections on ignition and decoding).
- Methodological argument: tests of GNWT's central conscious-access claim need
  a suitable conscious-versus-non-conscious contrast and evidence that the
  participant experienced task events whose neural signatures are predicted.
- Empirical observation about the literature: a published, technically
  specific counterinterpretation exists; it is authored by theory proponents
  and is not independent evidence.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against

- The interpretation that Cogitate refuted GNWT as a whole.
- Treating absent offset ignition in the tested task as an unconditional,
  uniquely diagnostic failure of GNWT without addressing awareness of the
  offset and the prediction's claimed centrality.

### Claims this source does not support

- Deleting, reversing, or nullifying Cogitate's preregistered empirical
  results.
- Treating GNWT as confirmed, uniquely correct, or directly transferable to
  artificial systems.
- Consciousness or non-consciousness of any current AI system.
- A claim that a GNWT-like architecture is sufficient for subjective
  experience.
