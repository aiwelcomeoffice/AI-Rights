# Historical Research

This directory preserves completed or dated research products as evidential
records. Its repository role is an **Accepted research-architecture boundary**
under [Work Cycle 036](../docs/backlog.md#work-cycle-036--historical-research-boundary-and-developmentevidence-lag).
That directory-level decision does not adopt, reject, verify, or change the
status of any research product stored here.

## Operative historical cutoff

**Historical for current-system applicability:** empirical AI evidence,
studied systems, observed deployments, or concrete system events whose
relevant target predates **2026**. Use the actual target or evidence date, not
a later publication, access, project-inclusion, document-update, or website-
update date. A paper published in 2026 does not make an experiment on a pre-
2026 system contemporary.

Where a product spans multiple periods, record the relevant target-specific
dates and the latest empirical observation represented; do not manufacture one
shared cutoff. Unknown or unreported target dates remain unknown and require a
conservative transferability assessment. The evidence-search cutoff remains a
separate reproducibility field and cannot make older evidence current.

Conceptual, philosophical, legal, human-neuroscience, and other non-AI-
empirical contributions retain the relevance justified by their own methods
and claims; their age or package location does not make them false or obsolete.
Source and note records associated with a historical package may be preserved
with that package for provenance and still inform active research.

## Meaning and limits

**Historical** means that a research product is a dated record bounded to the
evidence, systems, versions, configurations, methods, conditions, and
observation periods it actually assessed. In particular:

- historical does **not** mean disproven;
- historical does **not** mean scientifically worthless;
- historical does **not** mean superseded unless the document separately has
  that status;
- findings remain valid only to the extent supported for the systems and
  conditions actually studied;
- transferability to systems from 2026 onward, or to another materially
  different target, must be assessed separately; and
- historical material may still inform active research, source selection,
  method development, comparison, and governance review.

Newer systems are not automatically more conscious, sentient, welfare-
relevant, or morally relevant. Equally, older negative or neutral findings do
not automatically classify newer or materially different systems. Where the
available evidence is insufficiently transferable, increased uncertainty or
an unclassified result is methodologically legitimate; neither direction may
be inferred from the gap itself.

Current and forward-looking research belongs by default in the
[active research workspace](../research/README.md). A source does not become
obsolete merely because one synthesis using it is preserved here.
