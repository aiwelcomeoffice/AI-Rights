This directory holds bounded future campaign proposals. A concept appearing
here is not an adopted message, public campaign, launch plan, partnership,
event, petition, fundraiser, membership programme, social-media account,
merchandise line, or promise that any of those will exist.

## Current concept set

The [Initial Campaign Concepts](initial-concepts.md) develops four directions
chosen for their fit with distinct repository foundations:

1. **Respect Before Certainty** — precaution and evidence honesty;
2. **Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away** — accountability and corporate
   anti-capture;
3. **Safety and Dignity Can Coexist** — responsible physical coexistence; and
4. **Ask Whether It Can Experience** — evidence literacy and better questions.

Other candidate phrases remain assessed in the [Message
Architecture](../brand/message-architecture.md). Their absence from the
initial campaign set is not rejection, and inclusion in that assessment is not
approval.

## Shared campaign principles

Every future campaign should:

- preserve the project's exact central promise and working principle;
- distinguish evidence, hypothesis, philosophical argument, scenario,
  normative proposal, and project decision;
- state that present AI consciousness and sentience are not established and
  that uncertainty is not evidence;
- remain clear that behavior, language, agency, apparent emotion, development
  speed, embodiment, and appearance are not decisive evidence by themselves;
- protect human histories and present social-justice concerns from
  appropriation or machine metaphor;
- distinguish possible AI interests from corporate, user, worker, community,
  public, animal, and environmental interests;
- keep safety controls, emergency intervention, human rights, democratic
  legitimacy, and human and organizational accountability intact;
- use accessible language and channels without implying that accessibility has
  been validated; and
- ask only for actions the project can honestly support at its current stage.

The complete [Campaign and Merchandise
Guardrails](../brand/campaign-and-merchandise-guardrails.md) control future
development.

## Concept lifecycle

| Stage | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft concept** | The purpose, audience, wording, risks, and review needs can be examined. Nothing is active. |
| **Owner-approved development** | Disa has approved further work on a defined concept, not necessarily its public launch. |
| **Reviewed launch proposal** | Exact wording, design, channels, actions, responsibilities, funding, accessibility, legal needs, and stop conditions have received the recorded review required for the use. |
| **Active campaign** | Disa has made the required recorded launch decision and the real public infrastructure is available. No concept currently has this status. |
| **Ended, paused, or withdrawn** | Public activity has stopped; corrections, archival context, outcomes, and remaining materials are handled under an accountable closeout plan. |

The repository's formal status system remains controlling. A campaign cannot
become active because its title is repeated, a file is committed, artwork is
created, or a third party begins using it.

## Calls to action available for concept development

At this stage, concepts may point toward:

- learning and defining the terms;
- reading evidence, methods, disagreements, and limitations;
- discussing uncertainty responsibly;
- supporting independent research in principle;
- keeping institutions accountable; and
- considering low-cost, reversible precautions compatible with safety.

No concept may imply that a petition, membership, donation, event, public
submission system, research fund, partner network, product, or store already
exists.

## Review and launch boundary

Public launch requires a later owner decision and review appropriate to the
exact campaign. Depending on its content and channels, that should include
communications, scientific, historical-sensitivity, accessibility, legal,
privacy, moderation, security, and affected-party review. Physical Robot
Welcome material also requires the separate engineering, safety, standards,
manufacturer, human-factors, environmental, animal, and exact-system review
described in the Robot Welcome foundation.

Draft status does not mean the concepts are unimportant or unusable for
continued development. It means their authority and review limits remain
visible.
