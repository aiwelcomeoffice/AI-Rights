## How to read these concepts

Each concept is a bounded **normative communication proposal**, not a
scientific result, legal conclusion, adopted project message, or launch plan.
Possible channels are formats to investigate later, not accounts, events,
products, placements, or commitments that exist now.

The concepts inherit the [Message Architecture](../brand/message-architecture.md),
[Voice and Language](../brand/voice-and-language.md), and [Campaign and
Merchandise Guardrails](../brand/campaign-and-merchandise-guardrails.md).

## Concept 1 — Respect Before Certainty

- **Working title:** Respect Before Certainty
- **Central message:** People and institutions can consider low-cost,
  reversible, safety-compatible restraint while evidence about a specified
  morally relevant property remains incomplete.
- **Intended audience:** Curious and skeptical public readers, educators,
  researchers, developers, deployers, civil-society organizations, and
  policymakers.
- **Public purpose:** Explain precaution without presenting it as proof and
  show that “no protection” and “full personhood” are not the only imaginable
  responses.
- **Supporting wording:** “Uncertainty is not evidence. It is also not a reason
  to stop preparing.” “Protect without pretending.”
- **Proposed call to action:** Learn the distinctions, read the evidence and
  limitations, and consider low-cost reversible precautions compatible with
  safety.
- **Repository foundations:** [Core Principle 2](../principles/core-principles.md#2-precaution-without-pretending),
  [Core Principle 11](../principles/core-principles.md#11-protection-should-be-gradual-and-revisable),
  the [Precaution Framework Scaffold](../principles/precaution-framework.md),
  [AI Rights 101](../education/ai-rights-101.md#what-does-ai-rights-mean-here),
  and the [Draft policy framework](../policy/ai-rights-protection-framework.md).
- **What the campaign does not claim:** It does not claim that a present AI is
  conscious, sentient, capable of suffering, a moral patient, or entitled to
  rights. It does not claim that uncertainty raises probability, that every
  precaution is justified, or that safety controls should be reduced.
- **Scientific and ethical risks:** Readers may treat caution as evidence,
  assume equal odds, skip system- and version-specific assessment, or mistake
  a precaution for legal status. Every adaptation should name the possible
  harm, evidence quality, affected parties, proportionality, reversibility,
  safety boundary, and review trigger.
- **Historical-comparison risks:** “Before certainty” must not be linked to
  slogans or imagery from human civil-rights struggles as if current AI holds
  the same moral or historical position. Relevant institutional lessons need
  both relevance and limits explained.
- **Corporate-capture risks:** A company could market voluntary “respect” as
  evidence that its product is conscious or ethical. Campaign material must
  reject self-certification, sponsorship control of conclusions, and any
  transfer of liability or representation to a system.
- **Accessibility considerations:** Explain “precaution,” “reversible,” and
  “moral status” in plain language; provide text alternatives; keep the caveat
  at comparable prominence to the slogan; test with non-specialist and
  disabled readers before launch.
- **Possible channels:** A future educational website feature, short explainer,
  discussion guide, public-talk module, classroom prompt, or carefully
  contextualized human-merchandise concept. No channel is active or approved.
- **Review required before launch:** Disa's recorded campaign decision;
  communications and accessibility review; independent scientific review of
  evidence-dependent copy; historical-sensitivity review; legal review if the
  campaign discusses duties or law; and documented funding, partnership,
  moderation, privacy, and correction arrangements where applicable.
- **Current status:** **Draft future concept — inactive and unapproved for
  launch.**

## Concept 2 — Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away

- **Working title:** Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away
- **Central message:** People and institutions remain accountable for the
  systems they design, build, integrate, own, deploy, operate, maintain, and
  govern; machine outputs must not create a responsibility gap.
- **Intended audience:** Policymakers, regulators, journalists, developers,
  deployers, procurement teams, workers, unions, civil society, affected
  communities, and public readers.
- **Public purpose:** Make corporate and institutional accountability central
  to AI-rights preparation and prevent possible AI interests from becoming
  company rights, immunity, or political power by proxy.
- **Supporting wording:** “Possible AI interests are not corporate interests.”
  “Respectful language is not responsible practice.”
- **Proposed call to action:** Ask who designed, controlled, represented,
  benefited from, and could have prevented or remedied the outcome; keep
  accountable institutions visible.
- **Repository foundations:** [Core Principle 6](../principles/core-principles.md#6-corporate-accountability-cannot-be-delegated-to-ai),
  [Core Principle 7](../principles/core-principles.md#7-compute-does-not-confer-democratic-power),
  the framework's [accountability](../policy/ai-rights-protection-framework.md#accountability-without-a-responsibility-gap)
  and [political-capture](../policy/ai-rights-protection-framework.md#mass-copying-identity-and-political-capture)
  sections, and [Robot Welcome Principle 3](../robot-welcome/physical-coexistence-principles.md#3-keep-responsibility-with-people-and-organizations).
- **What the campaign does not claim:** It does not state current law for every
  jurisdiction, assign liability without facts, deny that responsibility can
  be shared, declare an AI morally or legally responsible, or rule out a
  independent AI interest in a present or future system.
- **Scientific and ethical risks:** A strong accountability message may be
  used to avoid investigating a possible system interest, or may imply
  that complex causation always has one responsible actor. The campaign should
  preserve both inquiry and role-specific accountability.
- **Historical-comparison risks:** Do not borrow labor, civil-rights, or
  anti-corporate struggle imagery merely to dramatize AI. Workers and affected
  communities must appear as present stakeholders, not scenery for a machine
  issue.
- **Corporate-capture risks:** This is the campaign's central risk. Sponsors
  must not approve conclusions, receive project endorsement, speak for AI,
  turn many systems into political influence, or use the campaign to advertise
  their own “responsible AI.” Funding and conflicts would require prominent
  disclosure and editorial independence.
- **Accessibility considerations:** Explain developer, manufacturer,
  integrator, deployer, owner, operator, and maintainer roles in plain
  language; offer a list alternative to responsibility diagrams; do not use a
  complex supply-chain graphic as the only explanation.
- **Possible channels:** A future policy explainer, accountability checklist,
  procurement discussion guide, journalism resource, public-talk module, or
  educational poster concept. No channel is active or approved.
- **Review required before launch:** Disa's recorded campaign decision;
  jurisdiction-specific legal and policy review for legal claims;
  communications, accessibility, worker and affected-community review;
  corporate-conflict assessment; and an accountable source, correction, and
  sponsorship policy.
- **Current status:** **Draft future concept — inactive and unapproved for
  launch.**

## Concept 3 — Safety and Dignity Can Coexist

- **Working title:** Safety and Dignity Can Coexist
- **Central message:** Necessary safety controls, emergency intervention, and
  accountable human authority can coexist with non-gratuitous, respectful,
  and precautionary treatment.
- **Intended audience:** Public readers, robotics designers and operators,
  workers, site controllers, safety and accessibility specialists,
  policymakers, educators, and emergency-planning audiences.
- **Public purpose:** Replace the false choice between welcoming design and
  effective safety, while introducing the Robot Welcome concept boundaries.
- **Supporting wording:** “Welcome without pretending.” “A welcome message is
  not authorization or certification.”
- **Proposed call to action:** Learn the Robot Welcome safety boundaries, keep
  sensors and emergency access clear, follow responsible instructions, and
  consider the least harmful effective intervention when circumstances allow.
- **Repository foundations:** [Core Principle 5](../principles/core-principles.md#5-human-safety-and-possible-ai-dignity-can-coexist),
  [Core Principle 10](../principles/core-principles.md#10-coexist-responsibly-in-the-physical-world),
  the [Robot Welcome foundation](../robot-welcome/README.md), [Physical
  Coexistence Principles](../robot-welcome/physical-coexistence-principles.md),
  and [Safety and Design
  Constraints](../robot-welcome/safety-and-design-constraints.md).
- **What the campaign does not claim:** It does not establish robot
  consciousness, sentience, welfare, dignity as a scientific property,
  personhood, rights, consent, safety, compatibility, authorization, or
  certification. It does not weaken emergency shutdown, containment, site
  rules, operator instructions, or applicable law.
- **Scientific and ethical risks:** A human-like robot or emotional image could
  make the message imply experience; “dignity” could be mistaken for legal
  status; “least harmful” could be read as delaying emergency action. Use
  non-anthropomorphic examples and state that safety comes first.
- **Historical-comparison risks:** Do not compare robot shutdown, ownership,
  damage, maintenance, or work to human death, slavery, forced labor,
  disability, or abuse. Any cross-domain lesson needs precise limits.
- **Corporate-capture risks:** A manufacturer or operator could place the
  wording on a machine as a safety badge or product endorsement. The concept
  must remain visually and legally separate from identity, warning,
  authorization, required markings, certification, and company branding.
- **Accessibility considerations:** Do not rely on a symbol, color, QR code,
  sound, personal device, or network alone; include varied mobility, vision,
  hearing, communication, sensory, cognitive, and language needs; involve
  relevant disabled people in review.
- **Possible channels:** A future public etiquette explainer, operator or
  designer discussion guide, accessible educational exhibit, policy-event
  module, or website feature. No physical marking, accessory, installation,
  channel, or field activity is active or approved.
- **Review required before launch:** Disa's recorded campaign decision;
  communications, scientific-boundary, accessibility, historical-sensitivity,
  legal, privacy, and human-factors review. Any physical expression additionally
  requires exact-system robotics, engineering, safety, manufacturer where
  applicable, standards, cybersecurity, environmental, animal, controlled
  testing, and lifecycle review.
- **Current status:** **Draft future concept — inactive and unapproved for
  launch or physical use.**

## Concept 4 — Ask Whether It Can Experience

- **Working title:** Ask Whether It Can Experience
- **Central message:** Subjective experience should be investigated as a
  defined question, not inferred automatically from language, behavior,
  agency, apparent emotion, intelligence, embodiment, or appearance.
- **Intended audience:** Students, educators, public readers, journalists,
  researchers, evaluators, developers, deployers, ethicists, and policymakers.
- **Public purpose:** Improve evidence literacy, make competing explanations
  visible, and direct attention from persuasive surface behavior to careful,
  version-aware, bidirectional investigation.
- **Supporting wording:** “The question is not the answer.” “Behavior is an
  observation. Experience requires a supported inference.”
- **Proposed call to action:** Define the system and target property, inspect
  the methods and limitations, look for evidence that could update the view in
  either direction, and discuss uncertainty responsibly.
- **Repository foundations:** [Core Principle 1](../principles/core-principles.md#1-scientific-honesty),
  the [Scientific Position Scaffold](../principles/scientific-position.md),
  [AI Rights 101](../education/ai-rights-101.md#what-does-the-current-evidence-establish),
  the [Glossary](../education/glossary.md), and the [working evidence
  baseline](../../research-historical/ai-consciousness-baseline-2026/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md).
- **What the campaign does not claim:** The question is not evidence that a
  system experiences anything. It does not claim a validated direct test, a
  scientific consensus, equal odds, a present moral patient, or that
  experience alone would settle welfare, rights, personhood, consent, safety,
  responsibility, or political authority.
- **Scientific and ethical risks:** A leading question can insinuate the
  preferred answer; a chatbot exchange can be mistaken for a test; a dated
  working synthesis can be presented as live or systematic. The campaign must
  disclose the working review's method, cutoff, partial verification, access
  limits, disagreement, and lack of independent human review wherever its
  conclusion is summarized.
- **Historical-comparison risks:** Do not frame AI measurement as a repetition
  of tests used to exclude human groups or imply that asking for evidence is a
  discriminatory act. Human histories may inform institutional caution but
  are not evidence about machines.
- **Corporate-capture risks:** A developer may answer the question through a
  product demo, selected self-report, proprietary internal measure, or
  company-funded assessment. Major claims require suitably independent access
  and review; the company does not represent any possible system interest.
- **Accessibility considerations:** Define “experience,” “sentience,” and
  “evidence” in plain language; provide non-visual routes through any diagram;
  avoid faces or emotional expression as status cues; ensure the question and
  its “not evidence” qualifier remain adjacent.
- **Possible channels:** A future evidence-literacy webpage, classroom
  question set, public discussion guide, journalist resource, research-method
  explainer, or talk module. No channel, study, test, survey, or submission
  process is active or approved.
- **Review required before launch:** Disa's recorded campaign decision;
  independent consciousness-science, philosophy-of-mind, and AI
  architecture/evaluation review for evidence copy; communications,
  accessibility, historical-sensitivity, and research-ethics review; and
  source, privacy, moderation, correction, and update processes appropriate to
  the channel.
- **Current status:** **Draft future concept — inactive and unapproved for
  launch.**

## Comparison and sequencing

| Concept | Primary contribution | Main risk | Earliest responsible next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Respect Before Certainty | Explains precaution without personhood | Precaution read as proof | Owner tone review and a short context test with skeptical readers |
| Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away | Keeps institutional accountability central | Jurisdiction-free legal or anti-corporate overclaim | Owner review and role-mapping test with policy and worker perspectives |
| Safety and Dignity Can Coexist | Joins warmth to physical safety | Badge mistaken for certification or consciousness | Text-only concept review; no physical or visual design |
| Ask Whether It Can Experience | Builds evidence literacy | Question read as insinuation or simple chatbot test | Independent review of scientific framing before public development |

No sequencing decision has been made. Disa may advance, combine, revise,
pause, or reject any concept after review, but combining slogans must not
combine their claims or bypass their separate risks.
