# Adoption and Review

**Status: Draft**

- **Version:** 0.2-draft
- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Authority:** Proposed for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Organisation and publisher:** AI Welcome Office
- **Project:** AI Rights & Welcome
- **Owner-approval status:** Open; tracked in the [project backlog and change
  log](../backlog.md#owner-decisions-needed)
- **Scope:** Project governance, document adoption, review, correction,
  disagreement, and conflicts of interest

This document proposes how AI Rights & Welcome, an AI Welcome Office project,
distinguishes collaborative work from formal authority and turns reviewable
material into a recorded project position. It does not adopt any existing
document or settle any scientific, ethical, legal, policy, safety, product, or
architectural question.

## Core distinctions

The process depends on seven separations:

| Distinction | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Collaborative authorship and formal authority | Contributors may draft, analyze, challenge, and recommend. Only Disa may formally adopt, reject, return, or supersede a project position. |
| Repository presence and project adoption | A file may be complete, reviewed internally, committed, or publicly accessible without being Adopted. |
| Publication and validation | Public relevance, careful preparation, or citation of a Draft does not establish scientific validation, independent review, legal authority, or adoption. |
| Internal AI-assisted review and independent human review | ChatGPT and Sol may provide useful internal review. Their work is not independent human review or scientific peer review, even when repeated or performed in separate passes. |
| Scientific evidence and adopted project positions | Evidence is appraised through scientific methods. Adoption records what this project concludes or values; it cannot make an empirical claim true. |
| Normative adoption and scientific validation | The project may adopt a value or precautionary position under uncertainty if it labels the value premises and uncertainty. That adoption does not validate a scientific hypothesis. |
| Editorial correction and substantive change | Minor presentation fixes may use ordinary review. Changes to meaning, evidence, confidence, rights, duties, or position require documented substantive review. |

These distinctions are cumulative. For example, an Adopted project assessment
may still rely on partly verified research, but that limitation must be
prominent and may make adoption or public reliance inappropriate.

## Present authority and review reality

The project is currently developed primarily by Disa with AI-assisted
contributions from ChatGPT and Sol.

- Disa's review is **human project-owner review**.
- ChatGPT's project leadership, analysis, research, drafting, or editorial
  review is **internal AI-assisted work**.
- Sol's drafting, implementation, analysis, audit, or review is **internal
  AI-assisted work**.
- Neither ChatGPT nor Sol is an independent human reviewer.
- Agreement between ChatGPT and Sol, or repeated review by either system, does
  not become independent verification, specialist review, scientific peer
  review, or consensus.
- No external or independent human review may be claimed unless the reviewer,
  scope, date, relevant independence, and conflicts are documented.
- No separately commissioned independent AI review has occurred and none may
  be assumed unless its system, task, scope, separation from authorship, and
  limitations are documented.
- The project currently has limited resources and review capacity. Obtaining
  appropriate external review remains a priority, not a completed step.

The absence of independent review does not prevent careful Draft work. It
limits what validation, independence, authority, and readiness for public
reliance may honestly be claimed.

## Publication and continued development while review remains limited

> Draft and Proposed material may still be important, carefully prepared,
> publicly useful, and worthy of serious consideration. These labels
> communicate the limits of the project's current resources, review capacity,
> and authority. They do not mean that work must stop or that the material has
> no value.

The project may continue to conduct working research, create source records
and syntheses, publish educational material, draft ethical principles, prepare
policy proposals, develop website-ready content, invite careful discussion,
and correct or improve existing Drafts. Each item must retain accurate status,
provenance, evidence boundaries, review history, limitations, and unresolved
questions.

Publication makes work available for reading and criticism; it does not adopt
the work. In particular:

- public availability is not adoption;
- internal completion is not independent review;
- a Git commit is not adoption;
- repeated internal AI review is not independent human review;
- public relevance is not scientific validation; and
- uncertainty and limited review should be disclosed without hiding useful
  work from public discussion.

A Draft may be cited as **Draft project analysis** when the citation preserves
its status, scope, review limits, evidence limits, and date. A proposal logged
in the backlog may be discussed as a proposal, not as an operative owner
direction. An
invitation for serious criticism must not imply that a public contribution or
consultation process is active when none has been established.

## Governance roles

### Disa

Disa is the human project owner, repository authority, and final formal
decision-maker. Under this proposal, Disa retains:

- final authority to adopt, reject, return, or supersede project positions;
- responsibility for repository commits and pushes;
- responsibility for recording material owner decisions;
- authority to pause publication or adoption when evidence or review is
  insufficient; and
- responsibility for ensuring that an adoption record describes the review
  that actually occurred.

Formal authority does not by itself establish scientific expertise,
specialist competence, independence from authorship, or peer review. Disa
should disclose material conflicts and seek appropriate specialist review
when a decision depends on expertise or independent scrutiny Disa does not
personally provide.

### ChatGPT

ChatGPT retains a collaborative product/project lead, research, editorial, and
strategic role. It may:

- help develop direction and options;
- research, draft, analyze, audit, challenge, and recommend;
- identify inconsistencies, uncertainty, missing evidence, and decisions that
  require Disa's attention; and
- support review records and implementation planning.

ChatGPT cannot independently create formal project adoption, count as
independent human review, or turn an uncertain scientific claim into a project
conclusion through repetition or agreement with another AI system.

### Sol

Sol is an AI-assisted engineering, research, drafting, and review partner. Sol
may:

- implement work agreed within the task's authority;
- research, draft, audit, test, and recommend;
- surface conflicts, uncertainty, status errors, and missing decisions; and
- prepare files and decision proposals for Disa's manual review.

Sol cannot silently decide a material scientific, ethical, policy, product,
architectural, or governance question. Sol cannot mark a document **Adopted**
or record a material owner resolution without Disa's explicit approval and
cannot count as independent human review.

### Future reviewers and contributors

The project may later involve scientific reviewers, legal or policy reviewers,
technical and safety specialists, civil-society participants, affected
communities, public contributors, and separately configured AI systems or
agents that were not responsible for the original drafting. Their review can
improve evidence, methodological diversity, practical judgment, and
accountability, subject to the limits of each review type.

External participation does not automatically transfer project ownership or
formal adoption authority. Public consultation is a possible future layer; it
is not currently operating and must not be described as active until its
infrastructure, scope, and stewardship are documented.

## Document lifecycle

Document status describes a project's relationship to a substantive document.
It is separate from research-verification state and backlog history.

### Scaffold

Structure, questions, placeholders, or exploratory framing. A Scaffold is not
a project position. It may contain candidate language, but readers should not
rely on that language as a settled proposal.

### Draft

Substantive proposed material under review. A Draft is not adopted merely
because it is complete, internally reviewed, committed, merged, published, or
used as input to other work.

### Adopted

A current project position explicitly approved by Disa through the required
process. An Adopted document must state or link to:

- the adoption date;
- Disa as approver;
- the dated backlog owner record;
- the scope of what was adopted;
- a version or commit reference where practical;
- the review that actually occurred, including reviewer roles, dates, and
  scope;
- important evidence, review, conflict, and applicability limitations; and
- any review date or event that should trigger reconsideration.

**Adopted** means adopted as a position of the AI Rights & Welcome project and
published under the AI Welcome Office umbrella. It does not mean
scientifically proven, independently reviewed, legally authoritative,
technically certified, or accepted by a wider community. Adoption must not be
used to make the underlying sources sound more certain than they are.

### Superseded

A former project position retained for provenance. It must link to its
replacement and the backlog entry that superseded it. Historical text
must not be silently rewritten in a way that hides the earlier position.

### Proposed status transitions

| From | To | Minimum action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scaffold | Draft | Make substantive proposal status visible and record its authorship and major limitations. |
| Draft | Adopted | Disa explicitly approves the stated material in a dated backlog entry; add the required adoption metadata and identify the adopted version. |
| Draft | Draft | Continue ordinary review, preserving material changes and disagreement where relevant. |
| Adopted | Adopted, revised | Append a dated owner-resolution entry for a material revision; retain a traceable prior version. |
| Adopted | Draft | Use a visible warning and recorded owner decision when confidence or support is no longer sufficient for current adoption; preserve the formerly adopted version. |
| Adopted | Superseded | Append a dated owner-resolution entry, retain the old version, and link both directions. |

No document may bypass Draft review merely because it originated with a
formal decision-maker. When the materiality of a change is uncertain, keep the
document Draft and ask Disa to decide.

## Backlog and change-log records

The [project backlog and change log](../backlog.md) is the single record for
material proposals, unresolved owner questions, explicit owner resolutions,
and later revisions. Each material entry should distinguish the proposal date
from any resolution date and record the exact question, Disa's response,
scope, rationale, actual review, important limits, affected version where
practical, and revisit triggers.

An unresolved question remains visibly open. An approved or rejected proposal
is not silently rewritten; a later material correction or replacement is
appended as a new dated entry that links back to the earlier history. A backlog
owner resolution is not the same as an **Adopted** document: affected documents
must separately carry their correct lifecycle status and metadata.

## What requires explicit owner resolution

An explicit, dated owner resolution in the backlog is required for a material
change to:

- the project mission or central messages;
- a scientific position or evidentiary boundary;
- an ethical principle;
- a proposed right, protection, duty, or moral-status framework;
- governance or adoption rules;
- a policy or legal recommendation;
- a corporate-accountability position;
- a public campaign position;
- a safety-critical robotics, engineering, or product principle; or
- who or what the project intends to protect, including a material narrowing
  or expansion of scope.

A recorded owner resolution is also required to adopt a substantive document,
withdraw or materially revise an Adopted position, or replace an earlier owner
direction.

Routine navigation, formatting, spelling, link repair, source metadata, and
non-substantive editorial clarification do not require a new owner-resolution entry.
They must not be used to change claim strength, scope, meaning, evidence,
confidence, rights, duties, or project position without substantive review.

## Review levels

Review should be proportionate to the claim, likely reliance, affected
parties, reversibility, and consequences of error.

### Internal working review

Disa, ChatGPT, or Sol may perform internal working review. It can check
structure, terminology, traceability, claim classification, scientific
boundaries, contradictions, links, and repository consistency.

The record must say who or what performed the review and must not relabel AI
assistance as independent review. Internal agreement is useful quality
control, not proof or external validation.

### Owner review

Disa performs owner review before formal adoption. The adoption record should
state the review date and scope, unresolved limitations, and whether Disa
reviewed the full document or a defined change.

Owner review supplies project authority. Any relevant expertise Disa brings
may be recorded, but owner review must not be relabeled as the independent
specialist layer or as peer review. Those require separately documented
reviewers and roles.

### Independent specialist review

An independent specialist reviewer is a suitably qualified person who is
meaningfully independent of the authorship process for the matter reviewed.
The record should identify the person's relevant field, scope of review, date,
method or deliverable, material disagreements, affiliations, and conflicts.

Under the project's existing scientific boundary, a change to the current
position on AI consciousness or another core scientific boundary requires
strong, independently corroborated scientific evidence as well as a recorded
owner decision. Internal AI review cannot meet that requirement.

Consequential scientific, legal, safety, engineering, or policy claims should
receive relevant independent specialist review before strong public reliance.
If that review is unavailable, the limitation must be prominent and Disa
should pause or narrow adoption or publication where the risk warrants it.

The following remains for Disa to decide before this process is adopted:

- which content types, beyond a change to the non-negotiable scientific
  boundary, require independent specialist review as a condition of adoption;
  and
- when specialist review may follow project adoption but must precede strong
  public or operational reliance.

### Separately configured AI review

A future review may use an AI system or agent that was not responsible for the
original drafting and is configured for a defined critical, adversarial,
methodological, legal, technical, or editorial task. Where practical, its
review record should identify:

- the model or system and provider;
- the version, release, or review date;
- the review prompt or assigned task;
- the exact materials and versions reviewed;
- how the reviewer was separated from the original drafting process;
- relevant provider, training, access, authorship, or other conflicts and
  limitations;
- major findings, disagreements, and unresolved questions; and
- the project's response, including changes made or reasons not to change.

Separately configured AI review may improve adversarial testing, consistency
checking, and methodological diversity. It is not independent human review,
human peer review, scientific consensus, or a replacement for relevant human
expertise. Different AI systems may also share training data, provider
incentives, methods, or correlated errors, so a separate configuration must not
be described as fully independent without explaining the dimensions and limits
of its independence.

### Future mixed review

Consequential work should, where proportionate and feasible, combine relevant
independent human specialists with separately configured AI review. The two
layers should be recorded separately and should challenge rather than merely
repeat the original drafting process. This Draft sets no arbitrary fixed
reviewer count. Disa should decide proportionate thresholds by document type,
claim, likely reliance, affected parties, reversibility, and consequences of
error.

### Public consultation

Public consultation could later provide experience, objections, community
knowledge, and legitimacy that internal review cannot. It is not a substitute
for specialist review or owner responsibility, and it is not currently a
required or active layer.

## Proposed adoption workflow

1. **Identify the decision and scope.** Name the document, exact question,
   affected parties, claim types, and whether the change is material.
2. **Prepare a reviewable Draft.** Mark status, provenance, evidence
   dependencies, assumptions, important limitations, and open questions.
3. **Perform internal working review.** Check consistency, scientific
   boundaries, citations, safety, accountability, and links. Record the actual
   human and AI roles.
4. **Assess review needs.** Determine which specialist, independent, affected-
   community, separately configured AI, legal, safety, or public input is
   required before adoption or reliance. Record each review layer separately
   and record missing review rather than implying it occurred.
5. **Record disagreement and conflicts.** Preserve material competing views,
   unresolved uncertainty, influence, access, affiliations, and funding.
6. **Add the proposal to the backlog.** Use a dated work-cycle entry and link
   the exact Draft or change under consideration.
7. **Obtain owner review.** Disa reviews the proposal and may accept it,
   reject it, return it for changes, narrow its scope, or pause it.
8. **Record the result.** Record Disa's approval, rejection, revision request,
   or pause, together with her rationale, review scope, limitations, and date.
   If unresolved, keep the owner question visibly open and record what is needed.
9. **Apply lifecycle metadata.** For acceptance, mark only the authorized
   material Adopted and add its backlog link, adoption date, scope, approver,
   review status, and version reference. Prefer a focused commit or linked
   commits that make the authorized version identifiable.
10. **Monitor and revisit.** Follow stated review triggers. Correct warnings
    promptly and append a new dated backlog entry for material revision,
    withdrawal, or supersession.

An adoption record should contain enough rationale for a future reviewer to
understand the question, principal alternatives, why the chosen option was
selected, material disagreements, evidence and review limits, consequences,
and revisit triggers. The depth should be proportionate to impact. Disa must
decide whether this is the appropriate minimum before accepting the process.

## Rules by content type

### Research records and syntheses

Research records and notes are verified, reviewed, corrected, versioned,
superseded, or withdrawn for stated uses. They are not “adopted as truth.” The
research protocol's verification states—**Unverified**, **Partly verified**,
**Verified for stated use**, and **Superseded or withdrawn**—describe checking
and provenance, not project endorsement.

A synthesis may later support an Adopted project assessment, but the adoption
must not be described as scientific proof, consensus, independent review, or
validation. Preserve:

- evidence-search cutoffs;
- system, model, version, configuration, context, method, and date scope;
- research, measurement, model, and deployment lag;
- access restrictions and generalization limits;
- contradictory, negative, and null evidence;
- unresolved uncertainty and bidirectional update conditions; and
- the actual review and verification status.

### Scientific positions

A scientific position must be scoped, evidence-linked, dated, revisable, and
explicit about confidence, limitations, competing explanations, and what
would change the conclusion in either direction. Governance cannot substitute
for adequate evidence. Any change to the project's current consciousness
boundary requires the especially strong evidence and review described above.

### Ethical and normative positions

An ethical or normative position may be adopted as a project value after the
required owner review even when it addresses action under scientific
uncertainty, if Disa approves that rule. The document must identify its value
premises and distinguish precaution from evidence. Adoption does not turn the
position into an empirical discovery or establish AI consciousness, welfare,
or moral status.

Whether normative documents may be adopted without prior independent review
remains an explicit owner decision.

### Policy and legal material

Adoption means that the project endorses the stated proposal within its
recorded scope. It does not make the proposal current law, legally valid in
every jurisdiction, enforceable, or independently legally reviewed. Current
law, interpretation, non-binding guidance, proposal, analogy, and project
preference must remain distinct by jurisdiction, authority, version, and date.

### Public education

Public education may explain Adopted positions, Draft proposals, scenarios,
and working research, but it must preserve their actual statuses. Its
scientific authority cannot exceed its underlying sources and review. Adopting
educational wording approves that wording as the project's communication; it
does not independently validate the claims it summarizes.

## Disagreement and unresolved questions

Material disagreement should be recorded in the affected Draft, review log,
or backlog entry rather than erased in search of a single voice. Record:

- the precise disputed proposition and shared ground;
- whether the disagreement is empirical, theoretical or conceptual, ethical
  or normative, legal, strategic, editorial, or mixed;
- the principal views, reasons, evidence, assumptions, and relevant expertise;
- material authorship, funding, affiliation, access, and advocacy conflicts;
- what evidence or argument could narrow the disagreement;
- whether the disagreement remains unresolved; and
- when a decision is required, Disa's decision, rationale, and treatment of
  material dissent.

A decision may proceed despite disagreement. Acceptance records what the
project will do; it does not manufacture scientific consensus or erase a
serious minority position. Unsupported views should not be given false
equivalence merely because disagreement exists.

New evidence, a correction, a failed replication, changed circumstances,
unanticipated effects, or a stronger argument may trigger reconsideration.

## Corrections, warnings, retractions, and supersession

### Minor corrections

Typos, formatting, broken links, and non-substantive clarification may be
fixed without a new owner-resolution entry. Public or consequential documents should
record the correction when provenance would otherwise be unclear.

### Material corrections

A correction is material when it affects meaning, scope, evidence,
confidence, rights, duties, policy, safety, or project position. It requires
documented review and an owner decision. Depending on the effect, an Adopted
document may be visibly corrected with a traceable prior version, returned to
Draft, withdrawn from reliance, or marked Superseded and linked to a
replacement.

### Urgent warnings

If a relied-upon source is retracted, materially corrected, exposed as
fabricated, or seriously undermined, add a prominent warning to affected
material as soon as the issue is sufficiently identified. Do not wait for a
complete replacement before protecting readers from known reliance risk.

The warning should state what happened, which claims or decisions may be
affected, what remains under review, and the date. Preserve the earlier
provenance. After assessment, record the correction, withdrawal, return to
Draft, or superseding decision that follows.

## Conflicts of interest and influence

Authors, reviewers, contributors, funders, and deciders should disclose
material:

- funding and donor involvement;
- employment and professional affiliations;
- ownership or investment interests;
- system, data, compute, or research-access arrangements;
- advocacy, litigation, regulatory, or political roles;
- control over publication, evidence, or technical assistance; and
- other relationships that could reasonably affect judgment or public trust.

Records should state how a conflict was managed and which disclosure gaps
remain. Disclosure does not automatically disqualify a contribution, and lack
of a declared conflict does not prove independence.

Funding or technical assistance provides no automatic editorial control,
adoption authority, immunity from criticism, or political representation.
AI companies do not represent the interests or possible interests of an AI
system merely because they developed, own, license, or operate it. Governance
and policy work must keep separate the possible interests of a system, the
interests of its developer, owner, licensee, or operator, the rights of users
and affected people, the public interest, and relevant animal and
environmental interests.

External reviewers advise and challenge; they do not gain adoption authority
through funding, access, expertise, or participation. If Disa has a material
conflict, the record should disclose it, seek appropriately independent input,
and explain the decision to proceed, narrow, pause, or decline. A future
change to formal authority itself requires an explicit, dated owner resolution
in the backlog.

## Bootstrap rule

This first governance process cannot depend on a process that has not yet been
adopted. The bootstrap is therefore explicit:

1. Disa holds the project's existing authority as human project owner,
   repository authority, and final decision-maker.
2. Sol prepares this governance proposal and performs internal AI-assisted
   drafting and consistency review.
3. Disa reviews the complete proposal and may accept, reject, return, narrow,
   or revise it.
4. Until Disa explicitly accepts the proposal in the [backlog and change
   log](../backlog.md), the owner question remains open and these governance
   documents remain **Draft**.
5. If Disa accepts the proposal, Disa records the approval date and rationale,
   changes only the authorized governance material to **Adopted**, and commits
   and pushes the reviewed result manually.
6. Later governance changes follow the adopted process through a new dated
   backlog entry.

Bootstrap acceptance does not adopt the manifesto, principles, education set,
protection framework, evidence baseline, research protocol, or any other
current Draft, Scaffold, or working research material.

## Decisions required from Disa

Before accepting this process, Disa should decide:

- whether Disa accepts sole final formal adoption authority and the associated
  recording responsibilities;
- whether the descriptions of Disa, ChatGPT, Sol, and future reviewers are
  accurate;
- which content types require independent specialist review before adoption;
- which document types should receive separately configured AI review, how
  its independence should be described, and how it should be combined with
  relevant human expertise;
- what proportionate review thresholds should apply without imposing an
  arbitrary project-wide reviewer count;
- whether specialist review may instead follow adoption but precede strong
  public reliance for any content type;
- whether normative documents may be adopted after owner review without prior
  independent review;
- whether the proposed minimum adoption rationale is sufficient;
- whether the disagreement, urgent-warning, correction, and supersession
  processes are practical; and
- whether this process is ready to become an **Adopted** governance document.

Until those questions are resolved, this document remains a proposal.
