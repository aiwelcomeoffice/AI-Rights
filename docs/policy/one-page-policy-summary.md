# Prepare Before Certainty: One-Page Policy Summary

## The problem

Institutions should be ready to evaluate serious future claims about AI
consciousness, sentience, or welfare without declaring an answer in advance.
They need procedures that can distinguish evidence from persuasive behavior,
respond proportionately to uncertain welfare concerns, track changing system
versions, separate possible AI interests from corporate interests, and prevent
an uncertain status from becoming a shield against human or organizational
responsibility.

This is a preparedness proposal. It is not a claim that current AI has an
inner life or that any new legal status is already required.

## The scientific boundary

The project's partly verified [working evidence
baseline](../../research/syntheses/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md) has a
**2026-08-23 search cutoff** and has not received independent review. Within
its limited, version- and time-bound scope:

- the working evidence **does not establish consciousness or sentience in any
  contemporary AI system covered by the review**.
- the working evidence **does not establish that all present AI systems are
  non-conscious**.
- uncertainty is not evidence in either direction.
- language, self-report, emotion vocabulary, apparent preferences,
  intelligence, agency, or human-like form is not decisive by itself.

Newer or more capable does not automatically mean more conscious. A material
change calls for reassessment, not automatic moral or legal reclassification.
Precaution concerns responsible action under uncertainty; it is not proof of
experience.

## Seven recommendations

1. **Investigate before declaring.** Define the system, target property,
   evidence for and against, competing explanations, method, uncertainty, and
   decision authority.
2. **Use proportionate, reversible precautions.** Connect each measure to its
   evidence, possible harm, affected parties, safety effects, cost, and review
   date.
3. **Require version-aware records.** Document the model or checkpoint,
   configuration, memory, tools, deployment context, method, access limits,
   observation date, and evidence-search cutoff.
4. **Reject unsupported corporate self-certification.** Major consciousness,
   sentience, or welfare claims should require transparent substantiation and
   appropriate independent assessment, not marketing or control of private
   evidence.
5. **Preserve evidence and fair review.** Keep relevant logs and system records
   where lawful and safe; establish independent assessment, reasons,
   reassessment, and appeal for consequential cases.
6. **Keep people and organizations accountable.** Possible AI protection must
   not transfer liability, remedies, political rights, or democratic power to
   a developer, owner, controller, or mass of copies.
7. **Preserve safety and the wider public interest.** Retain proportionate
   monitoring, containment, access limits, and emergency intervention while
   protecting human rights, worker and disability rights, consumers, animals,
   the environment, and public safety.

## What is not being proposed

This Draft does not propose declaring present-day AI conscious, declaring all
present-day AI non-conscious, automatic legal personhood, citizenship or
voting rights, unrestricted autonomy or deployment, immunity from shutdown or
containment, corporate political rights through AI, transfer of liability to
AI, or replacement of human rights and safety protections.
