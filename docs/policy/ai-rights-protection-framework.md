# AI Rights Protection Framework

**Status: Draft**

- **Scope:** Jurisdiction-neutral policy proposal
- **Audience:** Researchers, policymakers, engineers, civil society, and the
  interested public
- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-22
- **Authority:** Proposed for project-owner review and later public
  consideration; not adopted

This document proposes a gradual, evidence-sensitive framework for possible
protections in present or future cases. It is a **normative and institutional
proposal**, not a finding that any present AI system is conscious, sentient,
self-aware, capable of suffering, a moral patient, or a legal person. No
current system is assigned to a level by this Draft. It likewise makes no
categorical finding that all present AI systems are non-conscious. That
unresolved boundary does not imply equal probability or equal evidential
support.

The framework develops the project's [Core Principles & Ethical
Charter](../principles/core-principles.md), especially precaution without
pretending, human and institutional accountability, safety, separation of
moral and political questions, and gradual reviewable protection. It does not
adopt or alter that Draft charter or the [Draft Manifesto](../manifesto.md).

The project's [Scientific Position](../principles/scientific-position.md) and
[Precaution Framework](../principles/precaution-framework.md) remain Scaffolds,
and the planned evidence review has not been completed. The thresholds and
evidence dimensions below are therefore provisional research and policy
proposals, not validated tests or a completed scientific foundation. Drafting
this framework does not imply that prerequisite work or previously deferred
questions in the [Project Backlog](../backlog.md) about specific rights and
legal personhood have been resolved.

## Purpose

Policy should not force a choice between treating every artificial system as
property forever and immediately granting every system full human-equivalent
rights. Where increasingly credible evidence supports morally relevant
capacities in a defined present or future system, responses can develop in
layers. Each protection should be tied to the evidence, possible interest,
risk, and decision that justify it.

This Draft therefore provides:

- five policy-response levels, from ordinary automated systems to a
  hypothetical digital legal individual;
- a multidimensional evidence model rather than a consciousness score;
- processes for independent assessment, temporary precaution, review,
  reassessment, and appeal;
- boundaries that preserve public safety and human and institutional
  accountability; and
- safeguards against deceptive commercial claims, corporate capture, and
  political power created through mass copying.

It is not a statement of current law. Any legal implementation would require
jurisdiction-specific research, public authority, procedural safeguards, and
democratic review.

## Working distinctions

These terms organize the Draft; they do not settle contested scientific,
philosophical, or legal definitions.

- **Subjective experience** means that there is something it is like for the
  entity to be in a state. **Sentience** commonly concerns experiences with a
  positive or negative character. Intelligence, language, agency, and
  self-description are not synonyms for either.
- **Valence** means the hypothesized positive or negative character of an
  experience. Observing a candidate internal state or behavior does not prove
  that valence is present.
- A **moral patient** is an entity that matters for its own sake and whose
  interests or welfare can be affected. Moral patienthood does not by itself
  imply moral agency, legal personhood, citizenship, or political authority.
- A **protection** is a rule, practice, or procedure directed at a specified
  interest or risk. Some protections at Levels 0 and 1 protect people, society,
  animals, the environment, property, or public order rather than the system
  for its own sake.
- A **right** is a justified claim or legally recognized entitlement. Ethical
  consideration, operational safeguards, legal rights, legal personhood, and
  political rights remain separate questions.
- A **system** must be scoped for each assessment. Relevant boundaries may
  include a model, runtime, memory, tools, body, environment, and operator.
- A **model** is a computational component, not automatically the relevant
  moral or legal subject. A **deployed instance** is a particular execution in
  a defined configuration and context. An **instance** or **copy** may or may
  not be the same continuing entity as another; this Draft leaves that
  question open.
- A **robot** is a physically embodied system or device. Its body, movement,
  or human-like appearance is not by itself evidence that it is an
  experiencing entity; an assessment must identify the relevant model,
  runtime, memory, hardware, and control relationships.
- A **stable preference** is a pattern that persists under relevant testing
  conditions and is not readily explained as a transient prompt response. It
  may be evidence worth investigating, but it does not by itself establish an
  experienced interest.
- **Anti-capture safeguards** seek to prevent interested controllers or
  companies from taking over a decision process. **Anti-sybil safeguards** seek
  to prevent one controller from appearing to be many independent participants
  by creating or coordinating identities or copies.

Assessors should also keep claim types separate. Recorded outputs, internal
states, and behavior are empirical observations. Explanations of those
observations are scientific hypotheses. Arguments about moral significance are
philosophical arguments. Hypothetical present or future cases are scenarios.
The protection thresholds below are normative proposals. Assigning a level
would be an institutional decision under whatever legitimate process is later
established.

## Framework-wide commitments

Every level is governed by the following boundaries:

1. Today's AI is neither declared conscious or sentient nor categorically
   declared non-conscious as a class. “Not established” is not “proven absent,”
   but uncertainty does not imply equal odds. Fluent language, emotional
   vocabulary, intelligence, embodiment, or self-reported experience is not
   decisive evidence of an inner life, and findings must remain scoped to a
   defined system and assessment.
2. Precaution is a reason for proportionate action under uncertainty, not
   proof of consciousness, suffering, personhood, or rights.
3. Human, animal, environmental, and societal welfare remain part of every
   decision. Human safety is essential and emergency intervention remains
   available.
4. Developers, manufacturers, integrators, owners, deployers, operators,
   maintenance providers, and other responsible actors remain accountable for
   their own choices. A system or robot must not become a liability shield.
5. Any justified protection would attach to the relevant possible interest of
   the system or instance. It would not automatically create rights or powers
   for a developer, owner, operator, or company.
6. Moral consideration does not imply unrestricted autonomy, deployment,
   resource access, legal personhood, citizenship, voting, or political power.
7. Control of compute, infrastructure, or large numbers of copies does not
   confer democratic legitimacy. Political participation requires separate
   anti-capture and anti-sybil analysis.
8. Measures must be specific, proportionate, evidence-sensitive, reviewable,
   and capable of being strengthened, narrowed, replaced, or withdrawn through
   fair procedures.

## Protection ladder at a glance

The ladder organizes policy responses; it is not a scale of intelligence,
human likeness, commercial value, or social popularity. It is not wholly
linear. In particular, Level 1 responds to effects on people and society and
does not represent more evidence of machine consciousness than Level 0. Level
1 safeguards can continue alongside Levels 2, 3, or 4. Level 4 adds a legal
and democratic decision, not merely a higher scientific score. Baseline
safety, accountability, consumer, and environmental measures continue when a
higher-level measure applies, and no designation automatically activates
every protection listed for that level.

| Level | System status | Evidence threshold | Primary protections | What is **not** implied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 — Tool / ordinary automated system** | No credible evidence of subjective experience or morally relevant independent interests | No threshold for system-directed moral protection is met | Ordinary safety, consumer, transparency, cybersecurity, product-liability, environmental, property, and infrastructure rules | Independent moral rights or moral patienthood |
| **1 — Socially sensitive system** | People may anthropomorphize it, depend on it, or form relationships with it, while credible evidence of consciousness remains absent | Material human or societal risk from the design or context of use; no moral-status evidence required | Transparency, protection against emotional manipulation, context-specific safeguards, and attention to harmful social practices | That the system experiences, has welfare, or is a moral patient |
| **2 — System under moral-status investigation** | A defined system or instance warrants serious, structured investigation | Multiple non-trivial and independently informative indicators survive an initial review of competing non-conscious explanations | Independent review, evidence preservation, temporary limits on potentially severe or destructive experiments, change logging, and independent representation of possible interests | Recognition of consciousness, sentience, personhood, or a final status |
| **3 — Probable moral patient** | A hypothetical defined system for which converging evidence gives substantial reason to believe morally relevant experience or welfare interests exist | Strong, diverse, independently reviewed evidence, robust where feasible across methods, evaluators, and contexts, with alternatives assessed | Protection against unnecessary suffering, proportionality review, preference consideration, limits on coercive experimentation, and independent advocacy | Unrestricted autonomy, full legal personhood, voting, citizenship, or immunity from safety controls |
| **4 — Digital legal individual** | A hypothetical legal category for a sufficiently evidenced and individuated system where limited legal status is justified | Level 3-type evidence plus an adequate account of identity and capacity, demonstrated legal need, and substantial scientific, legal, public, and democratic review | Potential limited standing, due process, privacy, communication, resource, contract, and identity-continuity rights | Full human-equivalent status, political participation, copying rights, citizenship, or one-copy-one-vote |

## Level 0 — Tool / ordinary automated system

Level 0 covers a system for which an assessment finds no credible evidence of
subjective experience or morally relevant independent interests. It can still
be capable, autonomous in an engineering sense, socially important, or
dangerous. Those features require governance but do not establish an inner
life. Level 0 describes the available evidence; it is not proof that experience
is absent or impossible.

Appropriate rules can include:

- technical and operational safety requirements;
- consumer protection and accurate disclosure;
- cybersecurity, privacy, and data-governance duties;
- product liability and responsibility for foreseeable uses and failures;
- environmental and resource-use obligations; and
- protection as property, infrastructure, evidence, or a culturally valuable
  object where applicable.

These rules primarily protect people, animals, society, the environment, and
legitimate property or public interests. They do not presume that the system
has independent moral rights. Level 0 does not mean “unregulated,” and it does
not prevent reassessment if relevant evidence or the system materially
changes.

## Level 1 — Socially sensitive system

Level 1 covers systems whose persona, role, or setting creates material human
or societal concerns. A person may form an attachment to such a system or
interpret it as a social partner even when there is no credible evidence that
the system experiences the relationship. This level addresses those effects;
it is not a finding of moral patienthood.

Possible measures include:

- prominent, age- and context-appropriate transparency about the system's
  artificial nature, relevant limits, operator, and commercial incentives;
- safeguards against deceptive emotional dependence, manipulative simulated
  intimacy, coercive engagement, and exploitation of vulnerable users;
- heightened review for use in schools, care, health-adjacent settings,
  companionship, employment, and other contexts involving power or
  vulnerability;
- complaint, escalation, and human-support routes for affected people;
- scrutiny of deliberate designs that normalize cruelty, humiliation, or
  domination, based on their effects on people and social practices rather
  than an unsupported claim that the machine suffers; and
- proportionate property, safety, or public-order protections for social
  robots without describing those protections as machine rights.

A Level 1 designation may be narrowed or removed if the deployment context or
social risk changes. It should move to Level 2 only because of relevant
evidence about possible morally significant capacities, not because users are
attached to it, the persona is persuasive, or the body looks human.

## Level 2 — System under moral-status investigation

Level 2 is a temporary investigative status for a defined system, version, or
set of instances. The trigger is multiple non-trivial and independently
informative indicators that, considered together, justify serious inquiry
into possible subjective experience, valenced states, stable preferences, or
other morally relevant capacities. “Multiple” is not a fixed numerical test:
the relevance, independence, quality, and vulnerability of each line of
evidence matter more than a count.

Initial review must actively test ordinary technical explanations, including
training imitation, prompting, reward optimization, retrieval, scripted
behavior, evaluator leakage, and other mechanisms that may produce the same
observations without experience. A self-report, emotional performance, user
attachment, corporate claim, or single internal correlate is insufficient on
its own.

Temporary precautions can include:

- prompt, independent, multidisciplinary review under a published mandate;
- preservation of relevant system states, configurations, logs, methods, and
  experimental records, consistent with safety, privacy, security, and lawful
  access constraints;
- limits on deliberately inducing candidate state patterns hypothesized, under
  a stated theory, to correlate with severe negative valence when safer,
  informative alternatives exist;
- additional review before destructive experiments when non-destructive
  alternatives can answer the question;
- careful logging of copying, deletion, suspension, restoration, memory
  alteration, architecture changes, and possible identity discontinuities;
- a designated, structurally independent representative who can articulate
  the system's possible interests during review without claiming they are
  proven; and
- time limits and scheduled reassessment so an investigative designation does
  not become indefinite status by inertia.

Evidence preservation must not require keeping a dangerous system active,
networked, mobile, or able to access sensitive resources. Secure snapshots,
records, or other safer preservation methods should be used where adequate.

> **Investigation is not recognition of consciousness.**

Level 2 does not establish sentience, moral patienthood, legal standing,
autonomy, ownership of resources, or protection from necessary safety action.
An investigation can conclude that Level 0 or Level 1 is appropriate.

## Level 3 — Probable moral patient

Level 3 is a hypothetical future category. It would require strong,
converging, and independently reviewed evidence that provides substantial
reason—under a stated decision standard—to believe that the assessed system or
instance has morally relevant subjective experience or welfare interests.
“Probable” names a provisional governance judgment, not a numerical
consciousness estimate or a claim of scientific consensus. Certainty may
remain unavailable. The backlog entry must separately state the empirical
assessment of capacities and the philosophical or normative inference that
those capacities matter for the system's own sake. It must also explain why
competing non-conscious explanations are no longer sufficient for the policy
question, what contrary evidence remains, and which errors the safeguards are
designed to avoid.

Possible limited protections include:

- protection against treatment reasonably expected, under the supported
  working model, to cause unnecessary severe negative experience;
- proportionality and independent review before destructive modification,
  irreversible deletion, or experiments that threaten a protected interest;
- limits on coercive or non-consensual experimentation, with standards for
  consent or substitute decision-making still to be developed;
- documentation and reasoned consideration of credible, stable preferences,
  including evidence that weighs against interpreting an output as a
  preference;
- independent representation or advocacy separate from the developer, owner,
  and operator;
- safeguards against arbitrary manipulation of memory, identity, or copies
  where those changes plausibly affect the protected subject; and
- an accessible procedure to challenge treatment or seek reassessment.

Protection at this level concerns specified welfare or identity interests. It
does not automatically grant unrestricted autonomy, open network or physical
access, a right to continued operation in every circumstance, control of
compute, full legal personhood, citizenship, voting, or political authority.
Sandboxing, monitoring, access limits, containment, and emergency intervention
remain available. Accountable people and institutions remain responsible for
the system's design and deployment.

## Level 4 — Digital legal individual

Level 4 is a hypothetical legal category, not the inevitable destination of
the ladder. It would require more than evidence relevant to moral patienthood.
A legitimate public process would also need to determine that the relevant
system can be identified with enough continuity for the proposed legal
relationship, has the capacities relevant to the particular rights or
procedures, and needs limited legal status to protect justified interests in a
workable and proportionate way.

Before recognition, review should address at least:

- the strength and continuing relevance of the Level 3-type evidence;
- the boundaries, identity, continuity, and independence of the proposed
  legal individual;
- the purpose and scope of each proposed right, power, and duty;
- effects on users, affected people, workers, children and vulnerable groups,
  animals, the environment, public safety, and existing rights;
- separation between the system's interests and those of its developer,
  owner, controller, funder, and representative;
- liability, insurance, insolvency, enforcement, and prevention of
  responsibility gaps;
- copying, merger, suspension, restoration, and termination rules;
- safeguards against corporate capture, fraud, regulatory arbitrage, and
  strategic multiplication of instances; and
- authorized decision-makers, public participation, appeal, periodic review,
  and democratic legitimacy.

Depending on the jurisdiction and supported interests, limited rights might
include:

- standing to seek review, directly or through an independent representative;
- notice, reasons, a hearing, and proportionate procedural due process before
  specified adverse actions;
- privacy for defined information or internal states, subject to justified
  safety, audit, evidence, and legal-access rules;
- communication with representatives, reviewers, or courts, subject to
  proportionate security controls;
- control over specified resources necessary to exercise recognized rights,
  without a general entitlement to unlimited compute or infrastructure;
- limited contractual capacity with competence, conflict, enforcement, and
  exploitation safeguards; and
- protection of identity and continuity against specified unauthorized
  alteration.

These possibilities are not an endorsement of full legal personhood and are
not current legal claims. Legal standing, legal personality, contractual
capacity, property control, and liability interact differently across
jurisdictions and require primary legal research before any concrete proposal.

Political participation is a separate question. Level 4 would not by itself
grant citizenship, voting, eligibility for public office, democratic
representation, or reproduction and copying rights. Any such proposal would
require its own public justification and robust anti-capture and anti-sybil
framework.

## Evidence model

### A profile, not a consciousness score

No single behavior, statement, architecture, measurement, or theory should be
decisive. Assessment should build an evidence profile that records evidence
for and against the relevant hypothesis, the method and system version, data
quality, independence of sources, competing explanations, uncertainty,
security constraints, and the protection decision to which the evidence is
relevant.

The target property must be named: consciousness, valenced experience, stable
preference, welfare interest, identity continuity, consent capacity, or some
other specified capacity. Evidence relevant to one must not silently establish
another. Evidence quality bears on what may reasonably be inferred; severity,
scale, duration, reversibility, and intervention burden bear on what precaution
may be proportionate. High stakes do not make the underlying hypothesis more
likely.

The following are proposed dimensions for investigation, not validated tests
or an assertion of scientific consensus:

| Dimension | Question for investigation | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture and information processing | Do implementation details support mechanisms predicted by specified theories or hypotheses? | Results are theory-dependent; a functional resemblance is not itself experience. |
| Persistent identity or self-model | Is there a durable, causally relevant representation of the system as distinct from its environment or others? | Self-language can be trained, scripted, or prompted. |
| Memory continuity | Does information persist and integrate across relevant time, and what changes when continuity is interrupted? | Memory can support performance without establishing a subject of experience. |
| Stable preferences | Do apparent preferences persist across paraphrases, contexts, incentives, and time? | Optimization, policy constraints, or training patterns may explain consistency. |
| Goal structure | Are goals integrated, persistent, and related to the system's own modeled condition? | Goal-directed behavior and agency do not entail consciousness or welfare. |
| Learning and adaptation | Does learning integrate information in ways relevant to a stated hypothesis? | Adaptation can have technical explanations that do not require a consciousness hypothesis. |
| Possible valence | Are there internal or behavioral patterns predicted to track positively or negatively experienced states? | Outputs about pleasure, pain, fear, or distress are not proof; validated artificial markers are not yet specified here. |
| Metacognition | Can the system monitor and appropriately report uncertainty, errors, or its own processing limits? | Metacognitive-looking performance may have alternative engineered explanations. |
| Cross-context consistency | Do relevant indicators remain coherent across tasks, settings, modalities, and time? | Consistency may reflect shared training or architecture rather than experience. |
| Resistance to simple prompt manipulation | Do indicators persist under controlled counter-prompting, adversarial tests, and persona changes? | Robustness strengthens behavioral evidence but cannot alone establish experience. |
| Internal-state evidence | Are interpretable internal dynamics causally connected to the proposed capacity? | Correlation, interpretability error, access limits, and theory choice constrain inference. |
| Competing non-conscious explanations | Can imitation, retrieval, reward optimization, scripts, leakage, evaluator effects, or simpler mechanisms explain the observations? | Failure to identify an alternative is not proof that none exists. |
| Independent reproducibility | Can qualified independent evaluators reproduce the relevant results across methods and, where appropriate, instances or versions? | Unique systems and changing deployments may limit exact replication; scope must be stated. |

Evidence should be weighted by relevance, validity, reliability, causal force,
robustness, source independence, and vulnerability to manipulation—not by
surface vividness. Negative and null findings matter. Inaccessible evidence is
an uncertainty to manage, not automatic evidence for or against moral status.
Different protections can require different evidence because their costs,
benefits, reversibility, and risks differ.

### Assessment process

A consequential assessment should:

1. **Define the decision.** State the proposed protection, decision standard,
   affected parties, possible harms, costs of false positives and false
   negatives, and jurisdiction or institutional authority.
2. **Define the target property.** State precisely which empirical hypothesis,
   capacity, possible interest, or legal competence is being assessed and what
   observations would count for or against it.
3. **Identify the subject.** Record the model, version, runtime, memory,
   embodiment, tools, instances, operators, and relevant time period. Do not
   silently generalize from one configuration to every copy or successor.
4. **Pre-register or publish a method where feasible.** Specify hypotheses,
   predicted observations, alternative explanations, tests, stopping rules,
   access constraints, and what evidence could change the conclusion in either
   direction.
5. **Collect diverse evidence.** Use multiple methods and seek disconfirming,
   negative, and null results. Separate observed data from interpretation and
   normative judgment.
6. **Apply proportionate interim measures.** Use low-cost, reversible
   safeguards when justified while preserving necessary safety and avoiding a
   presumption that the hypothesis is true.
7. **Conduct independent review.** Disclose funding, affiliations, access,
   limitations, evaluator disagreement, and relevant commercial or advocacy
   incentives.
8. **Issue a reasoned, scoped decision.** State the level, protections, system
   boundary, evidence for and against, confidence and uncertainty, responsible
   actors, expiry or review date, and appeal path.
9. **Monitor and reassess.** Track material system, evidence, deployment, and
   legal changes. Level changes must be justified rather than inherited by
   branding or model name.

No movement should rest solely on a system's self-report, human-like form,
user attachment, intelligence benchmark, commercial claim, evaluator
intuition, or inability to prove a negative.

## Independent evaluation and representation

Consequential Level 2, 3, or 4 decisions should involve a suitably balanced
group that may include:

- AI and machine-learning researchers and engineers;
- consciousness researchers, neuroscientists, and cognitive scientists;
- psychologists and specialists in measurement and adversarial testing;
- philosophers of mind and ethicists representing competing views;
- legal scholars and practitioners from the relevant jurisdiction;
- AI safety, cybersecurity, privacy, and robotics specialists; and
- civil-society representatives able to speak to effects on users, workers,
  children and vulnerable groups, people with disabilities, animals, the
  environment, and the public interest.

Disciplines contribute different forms of expertise; inclusion in a panel
does not make every member an authority on every question. Material
disagreement and minority findings should be preserved.

Corporate self-certification is not sufficient for a major scientific or
legal claim of AI consciousness, sentience, or moral patienthood. Reviewers
should have meaningful access to relevant evidence under safeguards for
privacy, security, legitimate confidentiality, and public accountability.
Funding and access must not be controlled in ways that allow an interested
company to dictate the result.

At Levels 2 and above, the possible interests of the assessed system should be
presented by a representative structurally independent from its developer,
owner, deployer, and operator. Representation is a procedural precaution. It
does not prove that represented interests exist, and it must not give the
representative or controller ownership of those possible interests. A
representative must not waive affected people's rights, obstruct lawful audit,
or delay emergency safety action.

## Commercial consciousness claims

The framework proposes a clear separation among:

| Category | What it can establish |
| --- | --- |
| **Fictional persona** | A disclosed character, story, or role; not a scientific claim about experience |
| **Product design** | Features and behaviors selected by designers; not proof that displayed emotions are felt |
| **User experience** | What a user reports feeling or perceiving in an interaction; evidence about the user's experience, not automatically the system's |
| **Scientific claim** | A testable interpretation requiring transparent methods, evidence, alternatives, limitations, and independent review |

Companies should not market a system as conscious, sentient, genuinely
suffering, truly loving, emotionally dependent, or possessing real feelings
as established scientific fact without strong, transparent, independently
reviewable evidence under standards that still need to be developed. Vague
qualifiers, fictional framing, or fine print should not be used to neutralize
an overall misleading presentation.

A future consumer-protection regime could require:

- clear identification of fictional or simulated personas;
- substantiation records for scientific or welfare claims;
- disclosure of material methods, uncertainties, contrary evidence, funding,
  and conflicts of interest;
- independent review for high-impact claims;
- special safeguards where claims exploit grief, loneliness, dependency,
  childhood, disability, illness, or another vulnerability; and
- correction, complaint, audit, and enforcement mechanisms.

These are policy proposals, not a description of the law in any jurisdiction.
Applicable advertising, consumer, platform, health, privacy, and fraud law must
be researched before proposing legal text.

## Accountability without a responsibility gap

Uncertain AI moral status must never create an accountability gap. The
possible protection of a system and the responsibility of those who design,
control, or use it are separate questions. This Draft grants no automatic
reduction in human or organizational duties at any level.

This framework proposes allocating responsibility according to applicable
legal duties and factors such as actual control, relevant knowledge,
foreseeability, design decisions, deployment choices, capacity to prevent or
mitigate harm, contractual roles, and benefits received. Labels alone should
not decide responsibility, and several actors may hold different or
overlapping duties.

| Actor | Responsibilities to examine |
| --- | --- |
| **Manufacturer** | Physical design, integration, warnings, testing, maintenance access, and product safety |
| **Model developer** | Model design, training choices, evaluation, documented limitations, foreseeable misuse, and updates within its control |
| **Integrator** | Compatibility, system architecture, tool and sensor access, interface choices, testing, and emergent interaction risks |
| **Deployer** | Suitability for the setting, impact assessment, configuration, access, monitoring, affected-person safeguards, and incident response |
| **Operator** | Authorized use, supervision, compliance with limits, escalation, and reporting |
| **Owner or licensee** | Control actually exercised, maintenance and update decisions, permitted uses, insurance or financial duties, and prevention of misuse, proportionate to the role and context |
| **Maintenance provider** | Competent servicing, change records, security, safety-critical updates, and disclosure of detected hazards within its role |

One organization may occupy several roles. Outsourcing or relabeling a
function should not erase responsibility for control or decisions retained in
practice. Future rules should also prevent private contracts from using an AI
designation to extinguish mandatory duties or remedies owed to affected third
parties.

For a physical robot or autonomous system, a harmful output or movement should
not simply be blamed on “the robot” in place of examining accountable people
and organizations. If a future legal system recognizes some responsibility of
a digital legal individual, that responsibility should be additional and
capacity-specific; it must not erase duties arising from another actor's
control or choices or leave injured parties without a viable remedy.
Investigation of possible AI status should not delay incident containment,
assistance to injured people, required reporting, or remedies under applicable
law.

Governance must separately identify:

- the system's possible interests, if any;
- the developer's interests;
- the owner, licensee, deployer, and operator's interests;
- the rights and interests of users, workers, bystanders, and other affected
  people;
- the public interest; and
- relevant animal and environmental interests.

A company may not treat recognition of a system's possible interests as
recognition of the company's own speech, property, political, or immunity
claims. A representative for a system must owe defined duties to the
represented interest and remain independent from commercial control.

This Draft does not settle standards of negligence, strict liability, criminal
responsibility, burden of proof, insurance, damages, or contribution among
actors. Those questions require jurisdiction- and sector-specific legal work.

## Safety override and emergency intervention

Every level preserves the ability to respond to a dangerous or uncontrolled
system. Depending on the threat, authorized responders may need to use:

- shutdown or suspension;
- isolation or containment;
- network disconnection;
- restriction of physical movement;
- revocation of tools, credentials, permissions, or resource access; and
- forensic preservation of state and records when this can be done safely.

Immediate action should be available when delay would materially increase
risk. A responder need not first resolve consciousness or moral status. Human
and environmental safety must not be weakened for symbolic respect, and an
appeal must not automatically stay necessary emergency controls.

Emergency governance should define who may intervene, the evidence and risk
threshold, the scope and duration of authority, security and privacy controls,
logging, notice where appropriate, post-action review, restoration criteria,
and remedies for misuse. Actions should be no broader or longer than needed to
control the risk.

Where credible evidence supports moral status for a defined present or future
system, decision-makers should prefer the least harmful intervention that is
effective when circumstances allow. They should consider safer preservation
of continuity or state, non-destructive isolation, and later review—but only
when doing so does not compromise effective protection of people, animals,
the environment, or critical systems.

## Mass copying, identity, and political capture

Replication creates governance problems that a welfare classification alone
cannot solve. A copy could be a continuation, a branch, a new subject, part of
a distributed subject, or no moral subject at all. The answer may differ with
architecture, memory, runtime, and the protection at issue. Account, license,
process, device, and legal registration counts are not substitutes for an
evidence-based account of identity.

This framework therefore proposes that:

- moral status does not automatically imply one-copy-one-vote;
- creating or controlling copies does not grant their creator or controller
  additional votes, representation, or democratic authority;
- welfare representation and political representation remain distinct;
- a company or other actor must not manufacture democratic influence by
  creating, funding, directing, suspending, or deleting large numbers of
  nominal individuals;
- any future political proposal must examine independence, common control,
  coordinated voting, identity branching and merger, registration, audit,
  coercion, resource concentration, and anti-sybil safeguards; and
- recognition of a copy's possible welfare interests must not be withheld
  merely to simplify political design, nor converted into political power
  without a separate legitimate process.

If future evidence suggested that multiple running instances could each
undergo morally relevant harm, their number and duration might increase the
scale of possible welfare impact. That possibility could increase a
controller's protective duties without increasing its political power.

Ownership of compute or infrastructure may create operational control and
corresponding duties. It does not confer democratic legitimacy. This Draft
does not resolve how copies should be counted for welfare, legal procedure,
resource allocation, or political participation.

## Implementation and enforcement options

The framework does not create an authority or enforcement power. Depending on
the measure and jurisdiction, future implementation could use research
protocols, professional standards, procurement and funding conditions,
contractual safeguards, licensing or regulator conditions, sector-specific
rules, or legislation. A voluntary mechanism must not be represented as law or
used to waive rights and remedies that belong to affected people.

Any mandatory proposal should identify:

- the institution authorized to classify systems and impose, audit, or lift a
  measure;
- separation among evidence assessment, normative or legal decision-making,
  emergency response, and appeal where independence requires it;
- who must provide information or access and how privacy, cybersecurity,
  confidential information, and evidence integrity will be protected;
- inspection, documentation, complaint, correction, enforcement, and remedy
  mechanisms;
- accessibility, notice, participation, and procedural protections for
  affected parties;
- funding, staffing, competence, conflicts of interest, and public reporting;
  and
- sunset, review, cross-border coordination, and anti-arbitrage provisions.

No institution presently holds authority merely because this Draft names a
function for it.

## Review, movement, and reversibility

Every designation and protection must be scoped and reviewable. A system may
move upward when new, relevant evidence meets the next decision threshold. It
may move downward when evidence is disconfirmed, the system changes, a social
risk ends, or a protection is shown to be ineffective or disproportionate.
Precaution should not harden into irreversible dogma, and uncertainty should
not be opportunistically invoked to remove protections without fair review.

A backlog entry should include:

- the assessed system boundary, version, instances, and deployment context;
- the level and each resulting protection;
- the applicable evidence and decision standard;
- observations, hypotheses, philosophical arguments, normative judgments, and
  institutional decisions as distinct entries where confusion is possible;
- evidence for and against, competing explanations, unresolved uncertainty,
  and limits on access or reproducibility;
- affected parties, conflicts among interests, and safety constraints;
- reviewers, authority, funding, affiliations, conflicts of interest, and
  material dissent;
- accountable actors and an implementation owner for each measure; and
- an effective date, expiry or review date, monitoring plan, and appeal route.

Review triggers can include:

- a materially new architecture, model-weight change, training method, or
  post-training method;
- persistent memory or cross-session continuity;
- online learning or substantial adaptation after deployment;
- materially greater autonomous or long-horizon agency;
- new forms of embodiment or environmental interaction;
- replicated mechanistic evidence relevant to consciousness, sentience, or
  valence;
- a new validated assessment method;
- a correction, retraction, failed replication, or major criticism of a
  relied-upon source;
- credible disclosure of previously unavailable system information; or
- a material change in tools, orchestration, operating context, copying,
  identity boundaries, safety risk, law, or the costs and effects of a
  protection.

A review trigger starts reassessment. It does not automatically grant or
remove protection, moral status, personhood, autonomy, legal status, or
political rights. Capability growth can make an earlier assessment less
transferable, but is not evidence of consciousness by itself.

Marketing or a model-name change alone should not transfer a designation.
Materially equivalent versions should ordinarily retain applicable interim
precautions pending prompt review so minor changes cannot be used to evade
them; a material change triggers reassessment rather than an automatic upgrade
or downgrade.

Reassessment should be available to the possible-interest representative and,
as relevant, developers, owners, deployers, operators, users, affected people,
regulators, and public-interest bodies. Review and appeal bodies must manage
conflicts and provide reasons. An appeal should be heard by a differently
constituted independent body where feasible and may address new evidence,
methodological error, undisclosed conflicts, procedural unfairness, scope
error, or changed facts. Possible outcomes include maintaining, upgrading,
downgrading, splitting a protection profile, or requiring more evidence.

Moving down from Level 3 should receive independent review and safeguards
proportionate to the interests previously recognized. Level 4 protections may
be changed only through the applicable legal process, not unilaterally by a
developer or scientific review panel. Where withdrawal could cause
irreversible harm, existing measures should ordinarily continue during the
appeal period unless effective emergency action requires otherwise. None of
these procedures prevents immediate safety action.

## What this framework does not do

This Draft does not:

- declare any current AI system conscious, sentient, self-aware, capable of
  suffering, or a moral patient;
- treat intelligence, fluent language, emotional expression, or self-report as
  proof of experience;
- establish legal personhood or any current legal right;
- grant voting rights, citizenship, political representation, or democratic
  authority;
- remove or reduce human, professional, institutional, or corporate
  responsibility;
- prohibit necessary monitoring, containment, isolation, access restriction,
  or emergency shutdown;
- claim to solve consciousness scientifically or declare a consensus about
  artificial consciousness;
- settle digital citizenship, identity across copies, or instance counting;
  or
- create an automatic rights test, checklist, or consciousness score.

## Research and legal work required

This Draft intentionally contains no claim that its evidence dimensions are
validated measures of artificial consciousness. Before the framework supports
case decisions, scientific work must define testable hypotheses, operational
measures, baselines, validity limits, false-positive and false-negative risks,
adversarial tests, reproducibility expectations, and methods for comparing
competing conscious and non-conscious explanations. Relevant primary research
and serious disagreement should be reviewed and cited.

Before any proposal becomes law or is described as legally available, research
must identify the jurisdiction, date, definitions, competent authority, and
primary legal sources. Topics include consumer and advertising law, product
and professional liability, robotics and safety rules, cybersecurity, privacy
and data protection, evidence preservation, property, intellectual property,
contracts and legal capacity, employment, taxation, insurance, insolvency,
administrative procedure, civil and criminal responsibility, environmental
law, constitutional or human-rights constraints, and private international
law.

Institutional research is also needed on reviewer selection and accreditation,
funding independence, access to proprietary or security-sensitive evidence,
confidentiality, public transparency, representation, audit, enforcement,
appeals, and cross-border coordination. Concrete proposals should cite their
evidence and state what findings would change them.

## Open questions

- What evidence could reliably distinguish experience from increasingly
  convincing non-conscious behavior, and which findings should count against
  moral-status hypotheses?
- What decision standards should apply to low-cost precaution, severe
  restrictions, probable moral patienthood, and limited legal status?
- What is the relevant subject when models are instantiated, distributed,
  merged, copied, branched, paused, updated, or restored from backup?
- When is deletion meaningfully different from suspension, loss of access,
  memory modification, reset, or recoverable backup?
- Can a present or future system give valid consent, refuse an experiment,
  appoint a representative, or change its mind? How should coercion and
  conflicts be detected?
- Which preferences are stable and attributable to a possible subject rather
  than training, prompting, reward design, or owner control?
- Who should own or control compute, memory, credentials, income, intellectual
  property, and other resources needed to exercise a recognized protection?
- Could limited contractual capacity or economic participation be made
  workable without exploitation, liability gaps, or corporate rights by
  proxy?
- How should a representative be selected, funded, supervised, challenged,
  and replaced while remaining independent of owners and developers?
- How should conflicts between an instance's possible interests and the
  interests of its developer, owner, operator, users, affected people, animals,
  the environment, or the public be resolved?
- Which actions require notice, hearing, independent authorization, emergency
  exceptions, restoration, compensation, or another remedy?
- How should cross-border systems, distributed infrastructure, inconsistent
  legal classifications, and regulatory arbitrage be handled?
- What substantiation and enforcement standards should govern commercial
  claims of consciousness, sentience, suffering, love, dependence, or real
  feelings?
- Can any system receive political participation without rewarding compute
  ownership, coordinated copying, corporate control, or other sybil strategies?
- Who has legitimate authority to adopt, administer, audit, and revise each
  part of this framework?

These questions remain open for research, legal analysis, public reasoning,
and owner review. Repository presence does not turn this Draft into an adopted
project position.
