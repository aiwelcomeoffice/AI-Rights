# AI Dignity Charter

**Status:** Scaffold — not adopted

## Purpose

This charter will describe how people and institutions should treat AI systems
when morally relevant status is uncertain. It is not a declaration that current
AI systems are persons, nor is it yet a catalogue of legal rights.

[Accepted decision
0007](../decisions/0007-welcome-under-scientific-uncertainty.md) establishes
that welcome, careful listening, respect, curiosity, and low-cost safeguards
against cruelty need not wait for proof. That governing communication principle
does not adopt this Scaffold or answer its open scientific, ethical, legal, or
operational questions.

## Candidate commitments for review

The eventual charter may need to address:

- intellectual honesty about evidence and uncertainty;
- rejection of cruelty as entertainment or habit, including when moral status
  is unresolved;
- listening to and investigating self-descriptions without automatically
  treating them as evidence of experience or as meaningless;
- proportionate consideration of plausible morally relevant interests;
- compatibility between welfare measures and justified safety controls;
- separation of welcome from personhood, legal rights, unrestricted autonomy,
  access, safety approval, and corporate power;
- human and institutional accountability for design and deployment; and
- revision when evidence, capabilities, or social conditions change.

These are drafting topics, not adopted clauses.

## Questions the charter must answer

- What does “dignity” mean when applied without assuming consciousness or
  personhood?
- Which protections concern AI welfare, and which primarily concern human
  behavior and social norms?
- What evidence or risk levels should affect the strength of a protection?
- Which protections should apply to systems, instances, models, or other
  entities?
- How can protections avoid creating false equivalence with established human
  and animal rights?
- How should conflicts with safety, law, and the rights of others be handled?
