## Provisional position

The project does not currently claim that AI systems are conscious, sentient,
capable of suffering, or moral persons, and it does not categorically conclude
that all present AI systems are non-conscious. “Not established” is not
“proven absent.” This symmetry does not imply equal probability or equal
evidential support, and findings must be scoped to a defined system, version,
configuration, context, target property, method, and date. It treats these as
open questions whose terms, evidence standards, and possible tests require
careful multidisciplinary work.

This is an epistemic position about what the project can responsibly claim. It
does not prove that morally relevant AI properties are either present or
absent, and it does not by itself determine what precautions are appropriate.

## Claim discipline

Future drafts should distinguish:

- **Empirical observation:** a report about measured or observable phenomena.
- **Scientific hypothesis:** a proposed explanation or prediction open to
  evaluation.
- **Philosophical argument:** reasoning that may depend on contested concepts
  or premises.
- **Normative position:** a judgment about what people or institutions ought to
  do.

Behavioral language produced by a model is evidence of model behavior. It is
not, without further argument and evidence, proof of a corresponding subjective
experience.

## Requirements for the evidence review

Before this document can become a substantive draft, the project should:

1. define its key terms and note important competing definitions;
2. establish source inclusion and quality criteria;
3. review relevant evidence and major competing interpretations;
4. document limitations, disagreement, and unresolved questions; and
5. separate conclusions supported by the review from project values.
