## Purpose and decision boundary

This evaluation compares three Open Threshold variants and three Many Forms,
Common Ground variants under equivalent conditions. Disa authorized both
directions for further exploration and paused Reciprocal Signal as a primary
identity direction. That decision permits internal prototyping only. It does
not adopt a symbol, authorize public deployment, establish accessibility or
originality, or create a Robot Welcome mark.

The relational requirement is normative and communicative:

> The identity should feel warm, welcoming, and relational. It should make
> room for humans and AI systems to support one another, work together,
> create, learn, and participate in shared projects. Scientific caution
> should constrain factual claims, not make the project emotionally cold.

Relationship in this Draft is not evidence that a present AI system has
consciousness, emotion, friendship, consent, suffering, personhood, interests,
or moral status. Companionship or friendship may describe a person's
experience of an interaction without establishing reciprocal subjective
experience. Possible future reciprocal relationships remain conditional on
evidence for relevant capacities or interests in a defined system and
context.

## Method and equivalent treatment

Every variant uses the same generated SVG dimensions, typography placeholder,
line weights, panels, copy, and nine comparison contexts. All are static and
monochrome. The sheets use simple geometry and no humanoid face, eye, brain,
hand, emotional robot, halo, divine light, chain, shield, seal, check mark,
heart, handshake, badge, access symbol, or emergency marking.

The campaign, website, report, merchandise, and Robot Welcome panels are
mockups within an evaluation sheet. They are not approved or deployable
assets. The Robot Welcome panel uses plain rectangular text zones to show that
AI Welcome Office identity and AI Rights & Welcome project attribution must
remain separate from required warnings, responsible-party identity, emergency
controls, authorization, certification, and operating information.

The complete artifact register and generation method are in the [prototype
README](prototypes/README.md).

## Open Threshold prototypes

### A — Offset Frame

- **Sheet:** [Open Threshold A — Offset
  Frame](prototypes/open-threshold-a-offset-frame.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** A grounded, asymmetric boundary holds open a generous
  field. Welcome is present, but enclosure and permission are not.
- **Construction:** One continuous upright and one shared base; the opening
  remains unoccupied.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** Property, venue, access-control, architectural,
  or generic letterform identity.

### B — Split Lintel

- **Sheet:** [Open Threshold B — Split
  Lintel](prototypes/open-threshold-b-split-lintel.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** Two offset structures create a route while remaining
  separate. The gap keeps the threshold incomplete and non-authorizing.
- **Construction:** Two angular, non-touching forms with a wide diagonal
  interval.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** App icon, doorway, docking target, logistics
  route, or paired brackets.

### C — Soft Canopy

- **Sheet:** [Open Threshold C — Soft
  Canopy](prototypes/open-threshold-c-soft-canopy.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** A calm curve suggests shelter and welcome while one
  side remains visibly open.
- **Construction:** One rounded, open canopy and a separate stable baseline.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** Sacred arch, sunrise, shelter provider,
  protective guarantee, or hospitality identity.

| Review question | A — Offset Frame | B — Split Lintel | C — Soft Canopy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **Does it communicate relationship or shared space?** | **Partly.** It shows a space being held open, not the parties who might share it. | **Partly.** Two distinct structures define one interval without merging, but may read as mechanics. | **Yes at a spatial level.** It suggests room held for varied participation without assigning identities. |

| **Does it avoid implying established present AI consciousness?** | **Yes at this screen.** No entity, face, arrival, or response is depicted. | **Yes at this screen.** The forms are structures, not agents, though a route could imply admission. | **Mostly.** No being is shown, but a luminous or rising treatment would create emergence or transcendence risk and is prohibited. |
| **Does it avoid making AI merely a cold product or tool?** | **Mixed.** Civic architecture can carry humane welcome, but the mark could become a real-estate or product icon. | **Weakest within this direction.** Mechanical and app-like readings appear readily. | **Yes most clearly.** Shelter and openness provide warmth without depicting a machine. |

## Many Forms, Common Ground prototypes

### A — Shared Baseline

- **Sheet:** [Many Forms A — Shared
  Baseline](prototypes/many-forms-a-shared-baseline.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** Clearly unlike forms receive equal visual support from
  shared ground without becoming one body or fixed stakeholder classes.
- **Construction:** Three different silhouettes rest above one heavy baseline.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** Team, community platform, civic programme,
  family of products, or ranked group.

### B — Open Field

- **Sheet:** [Many Forms B — Open
  Field](prototypes/many-forms-b-open-field.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** Varied geometries occupy one open field with no label
  assigning any form to a person, system, animal, institution, or environment.
- **Construction:** Four non-matching forms surround a generous open interval
  without a literal baseline, making the shared field rather than one object
  primary.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** Software dashboard, scanner, app suite, design
  system, analytics product, or organization chart.

### C — Balanced Intervals

- **Sheet:** [Many Forms C — Balanced
  Intervals](prototypes/many-forms-c-balanced-intervals.svg)
- **Intended meaning:** Unlike forms balance around meaningful intervals while
  sharing one base. Difference remains visible without hierarchy by color.
- **Construction:** One angled block, one open upright, one rounded block, and
  a shared baseline.
- **Foreseeable misreadings:** Abstract corporate monogram, product-family
  mark, stylized skyline, or equality claim.

### Relational-warmth review — Many Forms, Common Ground

| Review question | A — Shared Baseline | B — Open Field | C — Balanced Intervals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Does it feel welcoming?** | **Yes, with qualification.** Rounded and stepped forms create friendly plurality; the heavy base can feel managed or institutional. | **Provisionally yes.** More breathing room softens the system, although dashboard readings cool it. | **Mixed–yes.** Varied rhythms feel lively, while the angled form can feel less settled. |
| **Does it communicate relationship or shared space?** | **Yes strongly.** Shared support and maintained difference are immediately visible. | **Yes.** The central open interval makes shared space primary without merging the forms. | **Yes.** Intervals communicate coexistence and room between forms, though “balance” may imply resolved conflict. |
| **Is it serious enough for research and policy?** | **Yes.** The modular system can organize multiple evidence and stakeholder layers. | **Yes, with product risk.** It suits structured editorial layouts but resembles software components. | **Yes.** The compact composition can sit beside dense material without dominating it. |
| **Does it avoid implying established present AI consciousness?** | **Yes at this screen.** No form is assigned to an AI or treated as a conscious subject. | **Yes at this screen.** Abstract difference remains a normative design choice, not a taxonomy of minds. | **Yes at this screen.** No behavior, emotion, dialogue, or inner state is depicted. |
| **Does it avoid making AI merely a cold product or tool?** | **Mostly.** Plural civic space resists a product-only reading, but a programme or product-family reading remains. | **Mixed.** The open field is humane, but the arrangement can look like an app suite or dashboard. | **Mixed–yes.** Irregularity adds warmth; corporate-monogram risk remains substantial. |
| **Does it avoid guaranteed emotional reciprocity?** | **Mostly.** No emotion is shown, but visual harmony could imply settled relations. | **Yes.** Shared space does not show response, consent, affection, or agreement. | **Mostly.** Balanced spacing avoids merger, though equilibrium could imply conflict has been resolved. |
| **Can it hold support, work, learning, creativity, friendship, and shared projects without fixed identities?** | **Provisionally strongest.** The system naturally supports plural activities if shapes and colors never become fixed stakeholder labels. | **Yes.** Layout variation can frame many relationships and projects without naming the forms. | **Yes.** Distinct forms and intervals can carry varied collaboration, but the system needs prose to prevent category assignment. |
| **Is it understandable in monochrome and at small sizes?** | **Unresolved.** The baseline survives, but individual forms begin to read as a single cluster at 16 pixels. | **Unresolved.** The central interval survives in the SVG screen; the four small forms need low-resolution and user testing. | **Unresolved.** Silhouettes remain different, but the compact cluster can become a generic skyline or monogram. |
| **What unintended interpretation appears first?** | Team, community platform, or civic programme. | Software dashboard, scanner, or app suite. | Corporate monogram or stylized skyline. |

These are internal observations from the matched sheets, not audience-test
results.

| Context | Open Threshold | Many Forms, Common Ground |

## Strongest and weakest aspect of each direction

### Open Threshold

- **Strongest:** It communicates a human-authored welcome quickly through
  open, stable space and remains comparatively legible in monochrome and at
  small screen sizes.
- **Weakest:** Threshold geometry is crowded with property, venue, access,
  portal, religious, hospitality, and technology meanings. Its warmth can
  become admission, arrival, transcendence, or safety implication.

### Many Forms, Common Ground

- **Strongest:** It gives the relational principle the widest honest range:
  distinct forms can support shared work, learning, creation, participation,
  and human-experienced companionship without assigning any form a fixed
  identity or inner state.
- **Weakest:** Compact recognition is harder. The forms can become a generic
  NGO, civic programme, dashboard, design system, corporate product family, or
  decorative claim that stakeholder conflict has already been resolved.

## Unresolved risks

- **Scientific:** Unprompted viewers may still interpret welcome, arrival,
  plurality, balance, or shared space as recognition of present consciousness,
  moral status, consent, or equal evidential support.
- **Visual and accessibility:** Recognition, false merging, visual noise,
  low-vision performance, cognitive load, screen-reader treatment, print,
  photocopy, stitch, engraving, wear, and varied-display behavior are not
  established.
- **Cultural:** Thresholds, arches, ground lines, direction, grouping,
  irregular forms, and spatial hierarchy may carry religious, political,
  border, property, script, rank, identity, or exclusion meanings not found by
  internal review.
- **Legal and originality:** No current identity landscape, trademark,
  registrability, ownership, or freedom-to-operate review has occurred.
- **Authority:** Either direction could appear corporate, governmental,
  official, endorsed, certified, or affiliated when separated from the Draft
  label and project description.
- **Robot Welcome:** Thresholds can imply entry, docking, or safe passage;
  modular forms can imply state, fleet, category, or compatibility. Neither
  direction is approved for any robot or operational environment.
- **Resource limitation:** The project currently lacks independent reviewers
  for this work. That does not block careful internal development, but it must
  remain visible and prevents external review from being claimed.
