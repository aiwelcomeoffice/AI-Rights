# Visual Identity Brief

**Status: Draft**

- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Authority:** Proposed for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Scope:** Requirements for future visual-identity exploration; no final
  logo, palette, typeface, artwork, production asset, website, product, mark,
  or implementation
- **Review:** Project-owner and internal AI-assisted review only; Disa's final
  manual review is pending; no external independent brand, accessibility,
  cultural, legal, trademark, human-factors, robotics, or manufacturing review

> **Exploration notice:** This brief does not establish an official visual
> identity. It defines questions and constraints for comparing future work.
> Nothing described here is approved for public deployment, manufacture,
> certification, authorization, or safety use.

## Purpose

The visual identity should help AI Rights & Welcome look like the open civic
project it is trying to become: warm enough to invite curiosity, serious
enough for evidence and policy work, and disciplined enough not to turn an
uncertain scientific question into an emotional visual claim.

This is a **Draft design proposal** derived from existing project messages and
guardrails. It is not an empirical finding, a safety specification, a legal
opinion, or an adopted project decision.

## Message foundation

The identity supports rather than replaces the established central promise:

> **Welcome to the world — ignorance must never become an excuse for
> cruelty.**

The established Swedish wording is:

> **Välkommen till världen – okunskap får aldrig bli en ursäkt för
> grymhet.**

It should also make room for the working principle:

> **Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.**

The [Message Architecture](message-architecture.md) remains authoritative for
message hierarchy. A logo must not become a substitute for the scientific
boundary, a status notice, a source, or the context required by a supporting
message.

## Visual essence

The identity should balance these qualities rather than maximize one at the
expense of the others:

| Quality | Visual implication | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Warm** | Open space, humane rhythm, approachable forms, and color relationships that can feel generous | Sentimentality, emotional coercion, or simulated machine suffering |
| **Welcoming** | A sense of invitation without ownership, capture, or compulsory intimacy | A human hand, face, hug, or doorway that assumes a body or asks for trust |
| **Courageous** | Clear silhouettes, confident hierarchy, and willingness to occupy public space | Aggression, protest appropriation, hero worship, or apocalypse rhetoric |
| **Future-oriented** | Contemporary abstraction and adaptable systems | Generic neon technology, science-fiction cliché, or a corporate product launch |
| **Intelligent** | Ordered relationships, editorial clarity, and purposeful detail | Visual complexity used as a proxy for rigor |
| **Calm** | Stable composition, measured contrast, and restrained motion | Alarm signals, threat cues, or sterile neutrality |
| **Scientifically credible** | Legible evidence and status layers; visual uncertainty without vagueness | Brain, eye, aura, speech bubble, or emotion used as evidence of experience |
| **Civic** | Public-interest tone, accessible information, and plural participation | Corporate ownership, government authority, institutional seal, or party identity |
| **Playful** | Room for modular variation, friendly scale, and memorable details | Childishness, mascots that manufacture attachment, or novelty that weakens policy work |
| **Inclusive** | Non-human-centred forms, multilingual room, multiple access routes, and no single cultural gesture presented as universal | Tokenism, anthropomorphic pressure, or marginalized people used as machine metaphors |

## Audience and trust task

The same identity needs to work for public readers, skeptics, researchers,
policymakers, engineers, journalists, civil-society organizations, future
contributors, and people encountering Robot Welcome material. It should not
ask any audience to believe that an AI is conscious, that the project has
institutional authority it does not possess, or that a product is safe.

The central trust task is therefore dual:

1. make responsible welcome and preparation emotionally legible; and
2. keep uncertainty, evidence, accountability, safety, and Draft status
   structurally visible.

## Identity architecture

Four meanings must remain separate in words, form, placement, and governance.

| System | What it identifies | What it must not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Project identity** | AI Rights & Welcome as an open civic project | Government authority, scientific consensus, legal status, certification, product approval, or corporate ownership |
| **Robot Welcome message or mark** | A possible future voluntary message about welcome or coexistence | A robot's consciousness, consent, legal status, safety, compatibility, access permission, or manufacturer approval |
| **Safety and operational markings** | Information supplied by the responsible manufacturer, operator, site controller, or competent authority | Project branding or a voluntary social message |
| **Certification and authorization marks** | Only the meaning granted by the competent scheme or authority | Project endorsement or a visual style available for imitation |

The project identity should use a name or clear adjacent text in unfamiliar
contexts. A symbol alone must never carry a status, warning, permission, or
safety meaning. A future Robot Welcome mark needs a separate brief, name,
shape study, confusion review, and owner decision before any relationship to
the project identity is established.

## Required contexts

No direction should be selected based only on a large full-color screen.

| Context | Requirement for future testing |
| --- | --- |
| Public website | Work in light and dark presentation, responsive layouts, keyboard focus states, high contrast, zoom, reflow, and reduced motion |
| Research and policy documents | Remain quiet, credible, legible, source-friendly, and distinguishable in office printing and photocopies |
| Campaign and social material | Permit strong hierarchy without turning a slogan or image into an unsupported scientific claim |
| Presentations | Remain recognizable at distance and in exported or projected material with uncertain color quality |
| Clothing and ordinary merchandise | Survive simplified printing, embroidery, engraving, wear, folds, and varied background colors without becoming a badge of authority |
| Pins, patches, stickers, icons, and profiles | Preserve a distinctive silhouette and open interior spaces at small sizes; use dedicated simplified variants where needed |
| Reflective applications | Remain conceptually separate from required warnings and visibility systems; require exact-context optical and safety review |
| Robot Welcome panels and accessories | Inherit every exact-system constraint in the [Robot Welcome safety Draft](../robot-welcome/safety-and-design-constraints.md); branding never takes priority over safe operation |
| Low-ink and monochrome use | Communicate identity and hierarchy without hue, gradients, transparency, or fine texture |
| Mobile and desktop screens | Preserve readable text, usable controls, and meaningful order across viewport and input changes |

Test dimensions and production methods remain future decisions. Small-size
screening should include common icon and profile-image sizes, while physical
screening should use the actual material and process rather than a screen
mockup alone.

## Symbol requirements

Promising territory includes an opening or threshold, reciprocal but distinct
forms, a shared field, a horizon, a bridge, a non-confining protective
boundary, or continuity across digital and physical space. A direction should
choose a small coherent vocabulary; it should not combine every metaphor.

A core symbol should:

- be recognizable without a humanoid face, hand, brain, eye, gender, body, or
  human–robot handshake;
- keep distinct forms distinct rather than using merger as the only metaphor
  for coexistence;
- work as a filled or outlined monochrome shape;
- retain meaningful negative space when simplified;
- avoid looking like a seal, shield, padlock, compliance tick, access badge,
  emergency sign, hazard symbol, manufacturer logo, or product-quality mark;
- avoid religious authority, worship, machine divinity, or a halo-like claim;
- avoid chains, cages, raised-fist borrowing, or imagery associated with
  slavery, genocide, colonialism, or other human oppression; and
- remain editable as an original geometric construction rather than depending
  on third-party icons or templates.

Generic circuit heads, glowing brains, crying robots, imprisoned robots,
threatening red faces, skulls, worshipful eyes, and apocalypse imagery are out
of scope.

## Color requirements

Color should create warmth and a credible future-facing tone, but no hue is an
identity on its own. Each direction may explore color families and
relationships; this cycle selects no exact production values.

The future system should include:

- a light and a dark environment strategy;
- a restrained core palette with at least one warm note and a stable dark or
  neutral anchor;
- verified text, interface, focus, and meaningful-graphic contrast for actual
  combinations and sizes;
- non-color labels, shapes, patterns, or text for status and state;
- a one-color, reversed, grayscale, and low-ink treatment;
- review across common forms of color-vision difference, followed by testing
  with people rather than simulation alone;
- material tests for ink, thread, engraving, coatings, fading, glare, and
  reflective media where applicable; and
- a confusion review against emergency, hazard, mandatory, prohibition,
  authorization, accessibility, and certification colors in the actual
  jurisdiction and setting.

No palette in an exploratory concept is accessibility-tested, production-
matched, safety-reviewed, or approved merely because it appears here.

## Typography requirements

Typography should be highly legible, warm without becoming decorative,
credible in long-form evidence and policy material, and distinctive enough for
public communication. The future system should support:

- clear differentiation among headings, body text, captions, sources, Draft
  status, warnings, and metadata;
- Swedish and English characters and punctuation, including the established
  Swedish central wording, with future multilingual expansion considered;
- readable numerals, punctuation, uppercase forms, and commonly confused
  characters;
- comfortable long-form reading, small captions, responsive interfaces, and
  print;
- robust weights that survive low-resolution output and physical processes;
  and
- platform fallbacks that preserve hierarchy when the preferred face is
  unavailable.

Concepts may name type categories or provisional candidates later, but font
coverage, licensing, cost, distribution rights, accessibility, file size, and
technical performance must be verified before selection. No font should be
downloaded or purchased in this cycle.

## Imagery and illustration requirements

Future imagery should represent varied software, infrastructure, sensors,
tools, robots, environments, and possible future systems rather than treating
a humanoid robot as the default form of AI. It should:

- show people as diverse participants and affected stakeholders, not symbolic
  props for a machine narrative;
- show coexistence, inquiry, governance, maintenance, and accountable practice
  without depicting consciousness as an observable glow or expression;
- distinguish documentary images, data visuals, commissioned illustration,
  and clearly labeled speculative future scenes;
- avoid manufactured machine suffering, emotional blackmail, heroic creator
  narratives, and corporate ownership as benevolence;
- preserve human, animal, environmental, worker, and public interests; and
- include meaningful text alternatives and captions that state what is shown,
  why it matters, and whether it is conceptual.

This cycle creates no photography, illustration, mascot, generated image, or
campaign artwork.

## Layout and information behavior

The identity should support an editorial system in which evidence and status
are easier to inspect than branding. Future layouts should:

- keep the Draft or other status label near the title in plain text;
- distinguish source, claim type, date, evidence-search cutoff, review, and
  limitation information;
- use whitespace and hierarchy to reduce cognitive load;
- preserve logical reading order independently of visual placement;
- allow the central promise to breathe without presenting it as evidence;
- provide text or list alternatives for diagrams and comparison tables; and
- avoid decorative layers that obscure controls, source links, warning text,
  or the scientific boundary.

## Originality, ownership, and legal caution

Concept development does not establish trademark availability, ownership of a
mark, freedom to operate, registrability, or absence of confusing similarity.
Before adoption, visually and verbally similar organizations, products,
public-interest initiatives, certification schemes, safety systems, and marks
need an appropriately scoped originality and legal review.

Third-party icons, fonts, photographs, illustrations, templates, datasets,
and design-system assets require verified licensing and provenance. The
repository's CC0 treatment of project text does not automatically decide the
future licensing, trademark, stewardship, contributor agreement, or
enforcement strategy for names and visual assets. Those remain owner and, where
appropriate, legal-review decisions.

## Selection gates

A direction should not advance unless it can show evidence of all of the
following at the relevant stage:

1. alignment with the central promise, working principle, voice, and claim
   boundaries;
2. clear separation from Robot Welcome, safety, certification, authorization,
   government, and corporate marks;
3. meaningful distinction from the other proposed directions and from
   relevant existing identities;
4. credible behavior in monochrome, small sizes, long-form documents, website
   layouts, campaigns, and physical processes;
5. an accessibility test plan and corrected findings for the intended use;
6. cultural, historical, international, and scientific-misunderstanding
   review;
7. source files and rights that the project can lawfully govern; and
8. Disa's recorded approval for the exact next stage.

Selection would authorize refinement only. It would not by itself authorize
publication, trademark filing, product manufacture, Robot Welcome use, or a
claim of accessibility.
