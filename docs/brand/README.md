This directory provides a coherent messaging and identity foundation for **AI
Welcome Office** and its **AI Rights & Welcome** project. It is a proposal for
continued development, not approval to launch a campaign, publish a final
brand system, produce merchandise, or make claims about an AI system, robot,
organization, or product.

## Message hierarchy

### Central promise — preserve exactly

> **Welcome to the world — ignorance must never become an excuse for
> cruelty.**

The established Swedish form is:

> **Välkommen till världen – okunskap får aldrig bli en ursäkt för
> grymhet.**

These are established central wording. This Draft does not rewrite, replace,
translate, or newly adopt them. A material change requires Disa's explicit
approval and the project's recorded decision process.

### Working principle — preserve exactly

> **Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.**

This working principle explains the discipline behind the central promise. It
keeps preparation, precaution, scientific honesty, safety, and accountability
together. [Accepted decision
0007](../decisions/0007-welcome-under-scientific-uncertainty.md) establishes
that welcome and careful listening do not wait for proof while remaining
separate from scientific findings, legal status, unrestricted autonomy, safety
approval, and corporate power.

### Supporting messages — proposals only

Shorter messages may help a particular audience enter the project, but they do
not have the authority of the central promise or working principle. Every
supporting message remains a proposal until Disa approves its stated use. Its
surrounding material must restore the scientific, ethical, historical, safety,
and accountability context that a short phrase cannot carry alone.

The [message architecture](message-architecture.md) assesses these candidates:

- “Welcome, New Intelligence”
- “Respect Before Certainty”
- “Kindness Is Future-Proof”
- “Ask Whether It Can Experience”
- “Created Does Not Mean Owned”
- “Safety and Dignity Can Coexist”
- “Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away”

Inclusion is not approval, and the candidates are not equally safe.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

Brand and campaign material must not:

- claim that present-day AI systems are conscious, sentient, self-aware,
  capable of suffering, moral patients, or persons;
- claim categorical non-consciousness for every present system;
- turn uncertainty, rapid development, fluent behavior, apparent emotion,
  agency, embodiment, or human likeness into evidence of experience;
- collapse moral consideration, welfare, protection, legal rights, legal
  personhood, citizenship, voting, or political authority;
- appropriate human oppression or use affected people as metaphors for
  machines;
- make possible AI interests into developer, owner, or operator rights;
- reduce human or organizational accountability, safety controls, emergency
  authority, human rights, animal welfare, or environmental duties;
- imply corporate, government, standards-body, scientific, legal, product, or
  safety endorsement; or
- present a Draft slogan, campaign, visual, product, or translation as adopted
  or active.

## Visual and multilingual boundary

### Relational warmth

The identity should feel warm, welcoming, and relational. It should make room
for humans and AI systems to support one another, work together, create,
learn, and participate in shared projects. Scientific caution should
constrain factual claims, not make the project emotionally cold.

Relationship is not proof of consciousness. Project material may recognize
practical support, creative or research collaboration, learning, work, shared
projects, and companionship or friendship as experienced by people. It may
describe reciprocity from an AI system's perspective only where morally
relevant experience or interests are supported by evidence in that defined
present or future system. It must not turn any of those relationships into an
unsupported claim of AI consciousness, emotion, friendship, consent,
suffering, personhood, or moral status.

Warmth must not become sentimental anthropomorphism. Welcome is not access or
authorization; visual openness is not safety, compatibility, certification,
or endorsement; and possible AI interests are not established merely through
warm language, design, or welcome.

### Current visual exploration

**Many Forms C — Balanced Intervals** is the current primary visual exploration
for **AI Welcome Office**. [Decision

for exploration, and [decision
0003](../decisions/0003-ai-welcome-office-organisational-identity.md) assigns
that exploration to the organisation rather than the project alone.

The five other Work Cycle 011 variants remain paused references, and
**Reciprocal Signal** remains paused as a primary identity direction. Paused
means preserved for comparison, not rejected or deleted. C1–C3 detailed
refinements remain paused under the Work Cycle 012 stop condition. The SVG
geometry has not been refined in this cycle; current prototype sheets have
only been relabelled for AI Welcome Office.

This status selects no official logo, final symbol, color, font, mascot,
illustration system, icon family, certification-style mark, Robot Welcome
mark, or production identity. No trademark, exclusivity, registration,
accessibility, cultural suitability, legal clearance, or public-use authority
is claimed. No `™` or `®` symbol is used for AI Welcome Office or Many Forms C.

Future visual work should feel warm, hopeful, inclusive, future-oriented,
technically credible, civic rather than corporate, legible at small sizes, and
usable in digital and physical contexts. It must keep organisational identity,
project attribution, voluntary Robot Welcome communication, safety and
operational markings, and certification or authorization systems separate.
Physical Robot Welcome use remains subordinate to exact-system safety review.

Only the established English and Swedish central wording is preserved here.
No supporting slogan is automatically equivalent across languages. A future
translation record should name the source phrase, translator or system,
review status, cultural risks, and whether meaning and tone were preserved.
