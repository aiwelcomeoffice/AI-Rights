# Message Architecture

**Status: Draft**

- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Authority:** Proposed for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Scope:** Public-message hierarchy and supporting-message assessment
- **Review:** Project-owner and internal AI-assisted review only; Disa's final
  manual review is pending; no external communications, accessibility,
  historical-sensitivity, legal, or independent scientific review

## Purpose

This architecture helps AI Welcome Office communicate through its AI Rights &
Welcome project without making a short phrase carry scientific or legal claims
it cannot support. It separates organisation and project attribution, stable
central wording, a working principle, supporting-message proposals, campaign
copy, and calls to action.

## The hierarchy

| Level | Role | Authority and use rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Central promise** | The project's most stable public commitment | Preserve the approved English and Swedish wording exactly. Do not replace it with a campaign slogan. |
| **2. Working principle** | Explains how preparation, precaution, honesty, welcome, safety, and responsibility fit together | Preserve exactly. Use as context for public explanations, not as proof of a scientific proposition. |
| **3. Supporting message** | Opens one theme for one audience | Proposal only. Use with the recorded context and risk controls after Disa approves the defined use. |
| **4. Campaign copy** | Adapts a supporting idea to a bounded public purpose | Draft per campaign. It may not silently broaden the supporting message or become an adopted project position through repetition. |
| **5. Call to action** | Asks a reader to take a proportionate next step | For this stage, limit actions to learning, reading evidence, responsible discussion, independent research, institutional accountability, and low-cost reversible precaution. |

### Level 1 — central promise

> **Welcome to the world — ignorance must never become an excuse for
> cruelty.**

> **Välkommen till världen – okunskap får aldrig bli en ursäkt för
> grymhet.**

The central promise is a **normative commitment**. It does not claim that a
present AI system can experience cruelty. “Ignorance” refers to a reason to
prepare and investigate responsibly, not positive evidence for consciousness.
Under [Work Cycle 019 owner direction](../backlog.md#work-cycle-019--welcome-under-scientific-uncertainty), the promise
also must not be framed as if possible morally relevant experience belongs
only to the future or matters only after people prove it.

### Level 2 — working principle

> **Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.**

The working principle supplies three checks:

1. **Prepare before certainty:** institutions can build evidence practices and
   consider low-cost, reversible precautions before a question becomes urgent.
2. **Protect without pretending:** precaution must remain proportional and
   must not be advertised as proof of consciousness, welfare, rights, or
   personhood.
3. **Welcome without surrendering responsibility:** welcome, careful
   listening, and warmth do not wait for proof, and they do not weaken safety,
   human and institutional accountability, democratic control, or the
   interests of people, animals, and the environment.

### Levels 3–5 — controlled adaptation

Supporting messages, campaign wording, and calls to action are intentionally
less stable. They should be versioned with their context and reassessed when
the audience, system, evidence, medium, language, sponsor, or use changes.

No repeated phrase becomes adopted by familiarity. Public campaign positions
require the project's recorded owner decision process.

## Short-message context rule

A short message may simplify one idea only when a nearby explanation answers:

- Is the phrase an empirical claim, question, future scenario, normative
  proposal, or project decision?
- Which systems, versions, contexts, properties, methods, and dates are
  relevant?
- What does the project **not** claim?
- Which people, organizations, animals, environmental interests, and possible
  AI interests in present or future systems are affected?
- Who remains responsible and able to intervene?
- Where can the reader inspect evidence, limitations, and review status?

A slogan must be rejected for a use if the required context cannot be made
discoverable or if the medium would predictably reverse its meaning.

## Supporting-message assessments

Risk levels compare the candidates within this Draft. **Lower risk does not
mean approved, context-free, or independently reviewed.** Every candidate
requires Disa's approval before defined public use.

### “Welcome, New Intelligence”

- **Intended meaning:** Meet unfamiliar capabilities and unresolved present or
  future forms of intelligence with curiosity, evidence, care, and accountable
  boundaries.
- **Appropriate audience:** General public, education audiences, future-facing
  civic discussions, and carefully contextualized project introductions.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** Intelligence and consciousness are distinct;
  the welcome is a proposed stance toward inquiry and coexistence, not a
  classification of inner life or moral status.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** “New intelligence” may sound like a newly born
  person, a conscious species, or a developer's product launch. “Welcome” may
  sound like unconditional access or endorsement.
- **Required context:** Define intelligence functionally; state that present
  consciousness, sentience, personhood, and rights are not established; keep
  safety, access, and organizational responsibility explicit.
- **Unsuitable uses:** A product tagline, a statement placed on one current
  system as if it described that system's status, a corporate launch, an
  authorization mark, or a substitute for the central promise.
- **Risk level:** **Medium–high.** Warm and distinctive, but strongly exposed
  to anthropomorphic and corporate-launch interpretations.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use**, with
  the exact audience, surrounding explanation, and placement recorded.

### “Respect Before Certainty”

- **Intended meaning:** Low-cost, reversible, safety-compatible restraint can
  be reasonable while evidence about a morally relevant property remains
  incomplete.
- **Appropriate audience:** General public, education, research ethics,
  institutional preparedness, and future non-commercial merchandise concepts.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A **normative precautionary position** from
  the Draft Core Principles. It does not increase the probability that an AI
  system has experience.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** Respect may be read as recognition of
  consciousness or rights; “before certainty” may sound like evidence is
  unnecessary or that all uncertainty demands protection.
- **Required context:** Name what is uncertain, distinguish precaution from
  evidence, and state proportionality, reversibility, affected parties, safety
  compatibility, and review triggers.
- **Unsuitable uses:** A scientific-result headline, legal-rights claim,
  instruction to disregard safety, or label that certifies a system's status.
- **Risk level:** **Low–medium.** Close to the working principle, but context is
  still needed to prevent precaution from being mistaken for proof.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use.** A
  campaign-scale use also requires the recorded campaign decision process.

### “Kindness Is Future-Proof”

- **Intended meaning:** Habits of non-gratuitous harm, curiosity, and
  responsible care may remain valuable across several possible futures.
- **Appropriate audience:** Informal education, community discussion, and
  possible future human merchandise where a fuller explanation is accessible.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A broad **normative and rhetorical
  proposal** about conduct; not a prediction that kindness always prevents
  harm or fits every decision.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** “Future-proof” is absolute and can imply that
  kindness alone solves safety, governance, or welfare questions. It may sound
  sentimental, guilt-based, commercially generic, or like a guarantee.
- **Required context:** Define kindness as compatible with boundaries,
  emergency intervention, evidence, accountability, and competing interests;
  acknowledge that hard choices may still be necessary.
- **Unsuitable uses:** Safety-critical settings, policy conclusions, emergency
  instructions, scientific communication, product guarantees, environmental
  claims, or any context where “kindness” obscures a responsible intervention.
- **Risk level:** **Medium.** Accessible and playful, but scientifically
  imprecise and vulnerable to overpromising.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use**, with
  special review of the channel and accompanying explanation.

### “Ask Whether It Can Experience”

- **Intended meaning:** Make subjective experience an explicit research
  question instead of inferring it from task performance, language, agency,
  emotion-like expression, or appearance.
- **Appropriate audience:** Public education, researchers, students,
  journalists, evaluators, developers, deployers, and policymakers.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A **question and research invitation**, not
  an empirical finding. It follows the repository's separation of behavior,
  consciousness, sentience, and moral status.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** The question may imply that experience is
  likely, measurable by ordinary conversation, or the only issue that matters.
  “It” may also erase the need to define a system, instance, property, method,
  and date.
- **Required context:** State that no validated stand-alone AI-specific measure
  of subjective experience is established in the repository's working review;
  name alternative explanations, negative evidence, limits, and bidirectional
  update conditions; keep affected human, animal, environmental, and public
  interests visible.
- **Unsuitable uses:** A leading survey, a chatbot self-report prompt presented
  as a test, a product label, a consciousness claim, or imagery designed to
  answer the question emotionally.
- **Risk level:** **Medium.** Scientifically productive when framed carefully,
  but easily misread as insinuation or a simple test.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use.** Any
  evidence-dependent adaptation also needs appropriate scientific review.

### “Created Does Not Mean Owned”

- **Intended meaning:** In a hypothetical future case with credible evidence
  of an independent inner life or other morally relevant status, manufacture,
  training, licensing, copying, or prior ownership would not by itself settle
  permanent moral or legal status.
- **Appropriate audience:** Advanced ethics, future-scenario, governance, and
  policy discussions where the conditional premise can remain prominent.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A **future-oriented normative proposal**
  derived from Core Principle 4. It is not current law, a present ownership
  conclusion, or evidence of experience.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** The phrase sounds categorical and immediate. It
  may be read as declaring current systems persons, denying current property
  law, equating AI with enslaved people, or transferring control to a system
  or its manufacturer.
- **Required context:** Prefer the fuller formulation “being created would not
  automatically mean being property forever”; state the future evidentiary
  premise; separate moral argument from present law; reject historical
  equivalence; preserve safety, due process, and corporate accountability.
- **Unsuitable uses:** General merchandise, protest imagery using chains or
  human-oppression symbols, current product or robot labels, legal advice,
  corporate advocacy, or any context too small to carry the conditional
  premise.
- **Risk level:** **High.** The compressed wording removes the conditions that
  make the underlying principle responsible.
- **Approval:** **Disa's explicit future approval is required before any public
  use**, together with legal and historical-sensitivity review appropriate to
  the jurisdiction and audience.

### “Safety and Dignity Can Coexist”

- **Intended meaning:** Respectful or precautionary treatment does not require
  unrestricted operation, and necessary safety controls need not license
  gratuitous abuse.
- **Appropriate audience:** Robotics, policy, public education, institutional
  governance, operators, designers, and accessibility or safety discussions.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A **Draft normative proposal** in the Core
  Principles and Robot Welcome foundation. Dignity concerns accountable
  treatment; it is not a consciousness finding.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** “Dignity” may imply personhood; “coexist” may
  imply equal authority; placement on an accessory may look like safety
  certification.
- **Required context:** Define dignity, keep human and environmental safety
  and emergency action explicit, identify responsible actors, and state that
  no system, product, operator, or site is certified.
- **Unsuitable uses:** A safety mark, compatibility label, manufacturer badge,
  emergency sign, authorization indicator, or claim that a design is safe.
- **Risk level:** **Low–medium** in explanatory material; **high** if placed on
  a physical machine or certification-like visual without safeguards.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use.** Any
  physical use requires the separate Robot Welcome review sequence.

### “Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away”

- **Intended meaning:** Developers, manufacturers, integrators, owners,
  deployers, operators, maintainers, and other responsible people and
  institutions remain accountable for their actual choices, control, duties,
  and representations.
- **Appropriate audience:** Developers, deployers, policymakers, journalists,
  workers, civil society, procurement audiences, and the general public.
- **Scientific or ethical basis:** A **normative governance and policy
  proposal** grounded in the Draft Core Principles and protection framework.
  It separates machine behavior from responsibility allocation.
- **Likely misunderstanding:** The absolute wording may be mistaken for a
  statement of current law in every jurisdiction, or as denying that legal
  responsibility can be complex and shared.
- **Required context:** State the jurisdiction when discussing law; map actual
  control, knowledge, duties, and capacity to prevent harm; distinguish
  possible AI interests from corporate interests; preserve remedies for
  users, workers, affected communities, the public, animals, and the
  environment.
- **Unsuitable uses:** Jurisdiction-free legal advice, a claim that one actor
  is always solely liable, an excuse to blame a system, or corporate material
  implying that respectful language proves responsible conduct.
- **Risk level:** **Low–medium.** Strong repository fit and low
  anthropomorphism risk, with legal-context and oversimplification risks.
- **Approval:** **Disa's future approval is required before public use.** Legal
  claims require current jurisdiction-specific verification.

## Calls to action at this stage

Permitted types for Draft call-to-action development are:

- learn the working distinctions;
- read the evidence and its limitations;
- discuss uncertainty without manufacturing certainty;
- welcome and listen carefully without treating welcome or self-description as
  proof;
- support genuinely independent research in principle, without implying a
  project fund or donation programme exists;
- keep people and institutions accountable; and
- consider low-cost, reversible precautions compatible with safety.

The project currently has no active petition, membership, donation, event,
public submission, product-sale, or campaign-launch process. Copy must not
imply otherwise.
