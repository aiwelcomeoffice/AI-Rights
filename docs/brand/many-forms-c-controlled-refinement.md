## What remains valuable in the reference

Sol independently prefers Many Forms C among the six Work Cycle 011 variants.
The preference is an internal design judgment:

- the irregular asymmetry creates immediate curiosity without a face, eye,
  body, or fixed character;
- the forms remain visibly unlike rather than becoming a row of interchangeable
  units;
- their open intervals make relationship and room visible without merger;
- the long common baseline makes shared conditions legible while leaving each
  form separate; and
- the composition can move between serious civic material and warmer,
  ordinary applications more plausibly than the other five prototypes.

The same ambiguity creates material risks. The rising left form and bent
central form can read as a periscope, hidden observer, robot sensor, naval or
military optic, skyline, or corporate monogram. The baseline can imply rank,
ground ownership, or an operational state. Those are hypotheses to test, not
findings about what an audience sees.

The forms do not stand for humans, AI systems, institutions, animals,
environments, conscious subjects, or moral ranks. Relationship in the image is
not evidence of present AI consciousness, reciprocal experience, consent, or
moral status. Welcome is not access, compatibility, safety, certification,
authorization, or endorsement.

## Deferred descendant briefs

These briefs preserve the intended comparison without creating assets while
the stop condition is active.

### C1 — Character Preserved

Limit changes to documented optical corrections. Preserve the reference's
asymmetry, uneven rhythm, three distinct forms, open intervals, shared ground,
and unresolved curiosity. Do not center all forms, equalize their mass, mirror
their contours, regularize every gap, or convert the family into a conventional
symmetrical corporate mark.

The later design record should identify each changed coordinate and its reason,
then compare it against the unchanged reference at native sizes before any
preference is recorded.

### C2 — Reduced Surveillance Reading

Test minimal changes to the top and turn of the central form, the lean and
height of the left form, and their relationship without borrowing from any
discovered mark. The purpose is to learn whether periscope, surveillance,
military/naval optic, hidden-observer, robot-sensor, and operational-state
readings can be reduced while keeping curiosity and non-literal abstraction.

Do not “solve” the risk by adding eyes, faces, people, hands, hearts, speech
bubbles, literal buildings, or a recognizable device.

### C3 — Small-Size Relational Mark

Optimize only what is necessary for 16–48 pixel rasterization. Preserve more
than one distinct form, visible intervals, common ground, no fixed identities,
and a recognizably related silhouette. Test whether controlled gap widening,
shape simplification, or a separate micro master is needed.

Do not join the forms into one body, rely on colour for separation, or claim a
minimum size without multi-renderer and low-vision testing.

## Native-size and false-merging review of the reference

This is a geometry inspection, not a multi-renderer or user test. The symbol
uses a 100-unit view box. Its filled forms end at approximately y=67–68, while
the 10-unit baseline stroke begins visually around y=75. The open vertical gap
is therefore approximately 7–8 units, or only about 1.1–1.3 device pixels at a
16-pixel rendering. The smallest horizontal interval between the central and
right forms is 5 units, about 0.8 device pixel at 16 px.

| Nominal size | Internal observation | False-merging risk |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 16 px | All three forms are encoded, but the narrow right interval and form-to-baseline openings depend heavily on antialiasing and pixel alignment | **High / unresolved.** Apparent touching or a closed gap is plausible; no minimum-size claim |
| 24 px | The narrow right interval is about 1.2 px and the vertical openings about 1.7–1.9 px | **Material.** Likely clearer than 16 px but still renderer-dependent |
| 32 px | The narrow interval is about 1.6 px and the vertical openings about 2.2–2.6 px | **Moderate.** Relationships should be more legible, but this remains an unvalidated estimate |
| 48 px | The narrow interval is about 2.4 px and the vertical openings about 3.4–3.8 px | **Lower, not cleared.** Shape interpretation and low-vision performance still need testing |

No SVG parser error was found in the reference. Pixel behavior remains
unverified across browsers, operating systems, displays, print, stitch, and
engraving. C3 remains a useful later study if the name/rights decision permits
visual work to resume.

## Neutral future review protocol

### Materials and assignment

1. Freeze and hash the exact SVGs. Give every candidate equivalent rendering,
   scale, label status, background order, and viewing time. Randomize candidate
   and background order across participants.
2. Recruit humans separately from AI-assisted assessments. Record recruitment,
   languages, relevant access needs, regions, design/AI familiarity, conflicts,
   compensation, and consent without claiming demographic representativeness.
3. For AI-assisted review, record provider, model/system version, date,
   configuration, prompt, image-processing path, and whether the system helped
   create earlier variants. Do not call such review independent when it shares
   the design context or training relationship cannot be established.

### Unprompted stage

4. Show the isolated symbol first with no organisation or project name,
   intended metaphor, or list of risks. Ask: “What, if anything, does this
   first look like?” and
   “What activity, organization, product, place, or feeling—if any—does it
   suggest?” Preserve exact short answers where consent allows.
5. Ask for confidence, uncertainty, and any safety, authority, religious,
   military, surveillance, corporate, human/AI, consciousness, or emotional
   inference volunteered by the reviewer. Do not seed missing categories yet.
6. Repeat at native 16, 24, 32, and 48 px and in the matched application
   contexts. Ask whether any elements merge, disappear, reverse order, or look
   like an operational marking.

### Prompted and contextual stage

7. Only after unprompted responses are locked, disclose **AI Welcome Office**,
   the **AI Rights & Welcome** project relationship, Draft status, intended
   “distinct forms / intervals / common ground” concept, and the full
   interpretation list. Ask which readings are supported, contradicted, or
   newly suggested.
8. Test black on white and white on black; text-first alternatives; zoom and
   low-vision settings; cognitive load; multilingual lockups; and exact print,
   stitch, or engraving samples if those uses later become authorized.
9. Test Robot Welcome only through a clear separation diagram until an exact
   machine, environment, human-factors method, safety review, and authority
   boundary exist. Do not place a candidate on a real robot in this stage.

### Analysis and reporting

10. Report counts only with sample size and method. Preserve uncommon serious
    misreadings, uncertainty, disagreement, failed gates, and verbatim context;
    do not average human and AI-assisted results into one audience score.

The future decision should state in advance which findings trigger revision,
pause, rejection, specialist review, or a text-first small-size fallback.
