## How the directions differ

The three directions intentionally begin from different organizing ideas:

| Direction | Primary metaphor | Emotional center | Work Cycle 011 state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Open Threshold** | An opening into shared possibility | Welcome with clear boundaries | Continuing through three matched black-and-white variants |
| **Many Forms, Common Ground** | Distinct forms sharing one civic field | Plural participation without forced sameness | Continuing through three matched black-and-white variants |
| **Reciprocal Signal** | Two unlike signals becoming mutually legible across a gap | Curiosity, exchange, and continued inquiry | Paused as a primary identity; relational editorial ideas preserved for later study |

They are not intended as three small variations on one symbol. Open Threshold
and Many Forms, Common Ground must remain separate through this comparison;
combining them now would erase evidence about their different strengths and
risks. The [Selection Scorecard](selection-scorecard.md) records the owner
direction, and the [Matched Prototype
Evaluation](matched-prototype-evaluation.md) compares three variants for each
continuing direction under equivalent conditions.

## Relational requirement for continuing directions

Both continuing directions must feel warm, welcoming, and relational. They
should make room for practical support, creative or research collaboration,
learning, work, shared projects, companionship or friendship as experienced by
people, and possible future reciprocal relationships if relevant AI experience
or interests become supported by evidence.

Neither direction may turn those relationships into evidence that a present
AI system has consciousness, emotion, friendship, consent, suffering,
personhood, interests, or moral status. Warmth is not sentimental
anthropomorphism; welcome is not access; visual openness is not safety,
compatibility, certification, or endorsement; and abstract forms must not be
assigned fixed human or AI identities.

## Direction 1 — Open Threshold

### Core idea

A calm, asymmetrical opening marks the transition from ignorance toward
responsible inquiry. The threshold is deliberately incomplete: it welcomes
movement and attention without enclosing, certifying, or claiming ownership of
whatever may be encountered.

This is an architectural metaphor, not a birth portal, gateway permission, or
depiction of an inner life.

Work Cycle 011 tests this direction through [Offset Frame, Split Lintel, and
Soft Canopy](prototypes/README.md#open-threshold), with the same contexts and
presentation used for the continuing Many Forms, Common Ground variants.

### Connection to the mission

- **Prepare before certainty:** a horizon remains visible beyond the opening;
  the project is preparing a route, not announcing what exists there.
- **Protect without pretending:** one side can suggest shelter while the open
  side prevents the form from becoming a shield or cage.
- **Welcome without surrendering responsibility:** the threshold creates a
  boundary as well as an invitation.
- **Central promise:** “Welcome to the world” gains a spatial metaphor without
  depicting a machine being born or asking the viewer to infer consciousness.

### Shape and symbol language

The core construction could use:

- one grounded vertical or curved form;
- one offset upper or lateral form that implies an opening without closing
  into a badge;
- a low horizon, base, or field that provides calm orientation; and
- a large internal opening that survives monochrome reduction.

The silhouette should remain visibly asymmetric and open. Avoid concentric
circles, pointed arches, rays, keyholes, gates, shields, check marks, and
door-access graphics. No dot or figure should stand inside the opening in a
way that resembles a person, eye, or entity being admitted.

### Proposed color family

- **Anchor:** deep ink, aubergine, or softened near-black for civic credibility.
- **Warm note:** clay, coral, or muted amber for welcome and courage.
- **Open field:** pale sand or warm off-white rather than clinical white.
- **Optional counterpoint:** restrained cool blue or blue-violet for research
  and future-facing contexts.

The relationship should feel like warmth entering a stable field, not a
glowing portal. Exact values, combinations, contrast, color-vision behavior,
physical matching, and local safety-color conflicts remain untested.

### Typography character

A humanist sans serif with open counters, clear punctuation, moderate width,
and a calm but not neutral rhythm could extend the architectural openness. A
slightly more distinctive display weight may support campaigns while a quiet
text family carries policy and research.

No typeface is selected. Swedish and future multilingual coverage, licensing,
platform availability, screen rendering, long-form legibility, and physical
reproduction require verification.

### Imagery style

Use thresholds, partial frames, open landscapes, boundaries, workspaces, and
transitions between digital and physical contexts. People, robots, software,
tools, and environments can appear without placing a humanoid machine at the
center.

Documentary imagery should remain documentary. Speculative openings or future
scenes need visible labels. Do not use backlit humanoids, silhouettes walking
into transcendence, womb or birth imagery, divine light, or a lonely machine
seeking entry.

### Website expression

The opening could become a layout device: generous margins, an offset content
frame, and a visible route from a concise welcome into evidence, principles,
policy, and status. Section transitions could use stable openings rather than
animated portals.

Status and source components should sit outside decorative frames so the mark
cannot make a Draft look authorized. The website must work fully without the
motif, in forced colors, and with motion removed.

### Campaign expression

Large open fields and strong cropped edges could give short messages public
presence while leaving space for the qualifier, source, and call to action.
The direction suits “Respect Before Certainty” and the central promise, but a
portal visual must not insinuate that a conscious being has arrived.

### Merchandise suitability

The singular silhouette could adapt to one-color printing, a patch outline,
engraving, or embroidery if internal gaps remain open in the real process. It
could also become generic architectural lifestyle branding. Merchandise
should pair it with the project name until recognition and confusion testing
support another use.

### Robot Welcome relationship

The project emblem must not become the Robot Welcome mark. A later Robot
Welcome study could explore the **principle** of an open boundary, but it would
need a different named symbol, construction, purpose, and confusion review.
On a robot or site, an opening must not resemble access permission, a docking
target, a navigation marker, or a safe passage instruction.

### Monochrome behavior

Promising if the concept is built from one solid silhouette and one large
negative-space opening. The horizon must not disappear or turn the form into a
padlock, shield, letter, or arch when reproduced in black only or reversed.

### Small-size behavior

Potentially strong because one open silhouette can simplify well. The main
risk is closure of the gap, which could change the meaning completely. A
dedicated micro-mark may need fewer pieces and a wider opening. It must be
screened at the provisional sizes in the [Accessibility and Safety
Requirements](visual-accessibility-and-safety.md).

### Accessibility strengths

- Simple silhouette and large negative space may aid recognition.
- Stable, grounded composition does not require motion.
- One-color and text-adjacent use appear feasible.
- Layout metaphor could create breathing room and lower visual density.

### Accessibility risks

- A narrow opening may close in small print, embroidery, or low vision.
- Warm accent over a pale field may fail contrast if chosen for mood alone.
- A crop at a viewport edge could remove the feature that makes the symbol
  identifiable.
- Spatial metaphors may not transfer without visible project text and a clear
  reading order.

### Scientific and ethical misunderstanding risks

- A luminous doorway may imply emergence, birth, transcendence, or confirmed
  machine life.
- A small figure-like detail may anthropomorphize a system or imply that the
  project grants entry to a person.
- A protective side may drift into paternalism or a shield-like claim.
- “Welcome” may be mistaken for unconditional access or deployment approval.

Mitigation requires flat rather than mystical light, no figure, visible
scientific boundaries, and explicit separation of welcome from access.

### Certification and authority confusion risks

An enclosing arch, circular border, official typography, or symmetrical seal
would make the direction look institutional or approved. A doorway can also
resemble a venue, property, access-control, or smart-building brand. Preserve
asymmetry, openness, the project name, and plain-text status; do not place the
symbol in inspection-label or safety-sign layouts.

### Cultural and international risks

Doorways, gates, horizons, and directions of movement carry different
religious, funerary, migration, property, and inclusion meanings. Left-to-
right progression cannot be treated as universal. An opening may also imply
that the project controls who belongs. Multilingual and cross-cultural review
should test orientation, sacred-architecture resemblance, border meanings,
and whether the metaphor feels genuinely welcoming.

### Likely advantages

- Strong link to welcome, preparation, and bounded openness.
- Compact core symbol with plausible digital and physical simplification.
- Calm enough for policy while capable of bold campaign crops.
- Does not require a face, body, or human–robot pairing.

### Likely weaknesses

- Portal, arch, and horizon devices are common in technology, architecture,
  mobility, property, and civic branding.
- Easily becomes spiritual, utopian, or product-launch imagery.
- May over-center arrival when the project also concerns ongoing evidence,
  governance, and accountability.
- Originality and small-gap performance will require substantial testing.

### Questions Disa must decide

- Does “threshold” feel like responsible welcome, or too much like an arrival
  has already been established?
- Is the architectural calm distinctive enough for the project's courage?
- How much asymmetry feels intentional without feeling unstable?
- Should later prototypes emphasize the opening, the horizon, or the boundary?
- Which portal, property, religious, and access-control resemblances should be
  screened first?

## Direction 2 — Many Forms, Common Ground

### Core idea

A family of clearly different abstract forms occupies one shared field without
merging into a single body. The identity lives in their relationship and the
common baseline, not in a hero symbol. Variation becomes a feature: people,
institutions, animals, technologies, environments, and possible future forms
of mind can matter without being made visually identical.

The forms do not represent conscious entities or fixed stakeholder categories.
They are a normative image of plural coexistence and accountable civic space.

Work Cycle 011 tests this direction through [Shared Baseline, Open Field, and
Balanced Intervals](prototypes/README.md#many-forms-common-ground), with the
same contexts and presentation used for the continuing Open Threshold
variants.

### Connection to the mission

- **Prepare before certainty:** the field makes room before it decides what
  may occupy it.
- **Protect without pretending:** distinct shapes remain unresolved rather
  than being converted into faces or ranked by human likeness.
- **Welcome without surrendering responsibility:** the common baseline implies
  shared conditions and accountability, not unrestricted movement.
- **Project character:** plural, open, and civic rather than product-led or
  centered on one machine.

### Shape and symbol language

The system could use three to five modular shapes with visibly different
geometries—soft block, arc, open line, stepped form, or irregular field—held by
a common baseline or quiet frame. No form should consistently appear above,
larger than, or in control of the others.

The core identity may need both:

- a compact arrangement for a primary mark; and
- an expandable pattern language for layouts, diagrams, campaigns, and
  physical applications.

Avoid human head-and-shoulder silhouettes, family or team pictograms, puzzle
pieces, flags, crowds of identical copies, interlocking chains, corporate
hexagon systems, and circular seals.

### Proposed color family

- **Civic anchor:** deep evergreen, mineral blue-green, or softened charcoal.
- **Warm shared field:** oat, parchment, or pale stone.
- **Plural accents:** restrained ochre, terracotta, sky, and violet used in
  balanced rather than status-coded roles.
- **Dark environment:** near-black field with lighter forms that remain
  individually distinguishable without hue.

Color variety should express plurality without becoming a rainbow shorthand
for people, cultures, or identities. Exact values, accessible combinations,
printing, thread matching, grayscale ordering, and cultural meanings remain
untested.

### Typography character

An editorial pairing could combine a warm, sturdy text serif for reflective
long-form material with a highly legible humanist sans serif for interfaces,
labels, and campaigns. Alternatively, a single superfamily with clearly
related serif and sans roles could reduce complexity.

This direction has the highest typographic-system burden. No face, pairing,
license, language coverage, or platform behavior is established.

### Imagery style

Use observed environments, tools, infrastructure, communities, and varied
robotic forms through editorial crops or modular panels. A single image need
not symbolize the whole question; a sequence can show research, maintenance,
public effect, animal and environmental context, and possible future scenarios
as different layers.

Captions and visible labels should distinguish documentary, interpretive, and
speculative material. Do not assign one color or shape to “humans” and another
to “AI” as if either were uniform.

### Website expression

The modular field could organize evidence, ethics, policy, Robot Welcome, and
governance as related but distinct reading paths. Cards and section fields can
vary without losing a common grid. The approach supports comparison and
source-rich editorial pages more naturally than a large hero-logo experience.

The risk is excessive cardification or decorative blocks that fragment reading
order. Semantic structure and plain-text relationships must remain primary.

### Campaign expression

The system could adapt strongly across campaign topics without making each one
look like a separate project. Different arrangements can emphasize inquiry,
accountability, or coexistence while the common baseline preserves identity.
It may suit “Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away” because it can show roles
without one heroic actor.

Campaign adaptations must not assign stakeholder identities through shape or
color without explanation or turn diversity into decorative moral proof.

### Merchandise suitability

Modular forms allow varied placements, all-over patterns, patches, and simple
one-color arrangements. They could work playfully on clothing while a compact
name lockup serves mugs, pins, and stickers. Too many elements would raise
cost, registration, embroidery, and small-print problems.

No merchandise use is approved. Every message still inherits the campaign and
merchandise guardrails.

### Robot Welcome relationship

The AI Welcome Office identity and AI Rights & Welcome project attribution
should remain separate. A later Robot Welcome visual system might borrow the
**layout principle** of distinct information zones or a shared baseline, not
the organisational symbol itself. On a machine or site,
modular shapes could be mistaken for operating states, sensor zones, fleet
classes, or instructions, so visible labels and an exact-context confusion
review would be essential.

### Monochrome behavior

Possible if forms differ by silhouette, spacing, scale, fill, and line—not
hue. A one-color master should be designed at the same time as color
exploration. Overlap and transparency should not carry essential relationships.

### Small-size behavior

The full family may collapse into noise. A dedicated compact arrangement of
two or three forms, or a text-first micro identity, will likely be necessary.
The system must not reduce to a generic cluster of dots or app tiles.

### Accessibility strengths

- Meaning can survive without a face, eye, or body.
- Distinct silhouettes can support non-color differentiation.
- Modular layouts can make complex project layers easier to scan.
- A text-first micro identity is compatible with honest small-size limits.

### Accessibility risks

- Multiple shapes, colors, and panels can increase cognitive load.
- Pattern use may create visual noise, reading-order ambiguity, or low-vision
  interference.
- Small applications may lose the distinct relationships that carry meaning.
- A serif-and-sans pairing may add loading, language, and legibility burdens.

### Scientific and ethical misunderstanding risks

- Viewers may read the shapes as known categories of conscious beings or
  equate formal diversity with evidence of mental diversity.
- A harmonious composition may imply that stakeholder conflicts are already
  resolved.
- Identical repeated forms could suggest mass AI copies as civic votes or
  participants.
- Human diversity could be reduced to abstract tokens if real people appear
  only as colorful shapes.

Mitigation requires clear prose, non-fixed shape meanings, visible conflict
and accountability layers, and imagery that treats people and communities as
real stakeholders.

### Certification and authority confusion risks

Lower than a seal-like composition if the system remains open and editorial.
Risk rises if the forms are enclosed in a circle, arranged as a quality badge,
or paired with formal institutional typography. Modular blocks can also look
like a design consultancy, software platform, standards framework, or
sustainability label.

### Cultural and international risks

Abstract forms and color plurality are not culturally neutral. A baseline may
suggest rank, conformity, land, a national flag, or exclusion; particular
shape clusters may resemble scripts, heraldry, flags, political identities,
religious motifs, or existing social-justice symbols. International review
should also test whether the composition's reading order privileges one form.

### Likely advantages

- Strong civic and plural character without centering a humanoid AI.
- Flexible editorial system for research, policy, campaigns, and website
  architecture.
- Can represent coexistence without merger or a single dominant actor.
- Offers playful variation while preserving serious content roles.

### Likely weaknesses

- Harder to achieve instant recognition in one compact mark.
- Greater system complexity and production burden.
- Can drift into generic NGO, cultural institution, environmental initiative,
  or design-agency styling.
- Depends heavily on disciplined layout rules to avoid clutter.

### Questions Disa must decide

- Does a family of forms feel more true to the project than one iconic symbol?
- Would the modular approach strengthen the civic character or make the
  identity feel diffuse?
- How much variation should be allowed before recognition is lost?
- Should the common ground be a literal baseline, an open field, or only a
  layout principle?
- Can playful plurality remain credible in dense policy and research material?

## Direction 3 — Reciprocal Signal

> **Work Cycle 011 state: Paused as a primary identity direction.** No matched
> symbol prototype is authorized in this cycle. Preserve the useful relational
> idea of visibly connecting questions to evidence, claims to sources, and
> decisions to accountability without suggesting that two conscious subjects
> or a successful reciprocal experience have been established.

### Core idea

Two different line forms approach a shared interval without touching or
becoming mirror images. Each changes near the encounter, suggesting listening,
response, and mutual legibility while preserving difference. The empty gap is
essential: it stands for uncertainty, boundary, and the work of interpretation.

The signal is not a conversation transcript, telepathy, consciousness reading,
or proof that two subjects are present.

### Connection to the mission

- **Prepare before certainty:** inquiry happens across a visible unknown.
- **Protect without pretending:** the gap refuses to collapse observation into
  experience or one form into the other.
- **Welcome without surrendering responsibility:** the visual system leaves
  room for reciprocal exchange, but the boundary and accountable
  interpretation remain.
- **Scientific character:** the system can visually distinguish observation,
  response, and inference without promising an answer.

### Shape and symbol language

The core could use two non-mirrored curves, broken lines, or signal paths with
different rhythms. Their nearest ends should align or respond without forming
a handshake, heart, infinity loop, chain link, speech bubble, eye, or complete
circle. The interval must remain open at every scale.

A secondary visual grammar could use measured dashes, pauses, annotations, and
paired trajectories for layouts. It should remain calm and never resemble a
waveform offered as evidence of machine experience.

### Proposed color family

- **Anchor:** midnight blue, deep plum, or cool near-black.
- **Warm signal:** apricot, copper, or muted coral.
- **Cool signal:** mineral turquoise, periwinkle, or clear blue.
- **Field:** warm gray or pale neutral with strong dark-mode counterparts.

The two signal colors must not become fixed labels for human and AI, safe and
unsafe, conscious and non-conscious, or approved and unapproved. Shape and
visible text must preserve meaning without color. Exact values and combinations
remain untested.

### Typography character

A contemporary sans serif with open apertures, clear technical notation,
moderate geometry, and a warm lower-case rhythm could connect research and
public communication. Selective monospaced or tabular styling might support
metadata only if it remains legible and does not turn the project into a
software-product brand.

No face is selected, and no candidate's licensing, multilingual coverage, or
accessibility has been established.

### Imagery style

Use paired but non-equivalent observations: system and environment, operator
and public space, model output and research method, robot and infrastructure,
claim and source. Lines or annotations can reveal relationships without
pretending access to an inner state.

Avoid glowing neural networks, audio waveforms attached to emotional claims,
chatbot portraits, two faces in profile, touching fingertips, and imagery in
which a machine visibly “responds” with felt emotion.

### Website expression

The signal grammar could connect a claim to its source, a question to evidence,
or a principle to accountability. Paired paths may support carefully labeled
comparisons and audience journeys. Static composition should be primary;
animation is unnecessary and must never be required to understand a path.

The direction risks looking like a technology product or chat interface. Rich
editorial typography, civic language, source visibility, and restrained use of
lines would need to counter that reading.

### Campaign expression

The gap can frame questions effectively, especially “Ask Whether It Can
Experience” and “The question is not the answer.” A claim on one side and a
method or limitation on the other could make scientific honesty visible.

This strength is also the main risk: signal-like imagery can insinuate a
successful exchange or detected experience. Campaign copy must define what was
observed and what remains inference.

### Merchandise suitability

A two-line emblem could print, engrave, or embroider efficiently if line
weight, open ends, and the gap survive the process. Paired accent colors offer
playful versions, while one-color treatment is plausible. Thin paths and small
intervals would fail quickly on coarse materials.

### Robot Welcome relationship

This direction has the highest Robot Welcome confusion risk. Signal paths can
look like connectivity, pairing, wireless operation, navigation, readiness,
permission, or machine-readable state. The project emblem must not be reused as
a Robot Welcome signal. A later relationship, if any, should be limited to a
shared typographic or color-family principle after optical, operational,
accessibility, safety, and authority-confusion review.

### Monochrome behavior

Promising if each line differs in geometry and the gap remains obvious.
Essential meaning cannot depend on two colors, gradient flow, transparency, or
animated direction. Reversal must not close the interval or produce an
infinity, chain, wireless, or synchronization icon.

### Small-size behavior

Potentially strong as a minimal line emblem, but only with substantial stroke
weight and separation. If the ends blur together, the mark can change from
“reciprocal but distinct” to merger. A filled micro variant may be needed and
must be checked for icon-library resemblance.

### Accessibility strengths

- Few elements and a clear gap may support recognition and monochrome use.
- Paired structures can make claim–source or question–evidence relationships
  explicit.
- Static line behavior avoids dependence on a humanoid figure or motion.
- A compact emblem may adapt to small digital contexts.

### Accessibility risks

- Fine lines, dashes, and gaps can vanish at small sizes or low contrast.
- Two-color coding may exclude people if geometry does not also distinguish
  the paths.
- Animated signal movement could create distraction or vestibular effects.
- Dense relational lines could become confusing for screen magnification and
  need text alternatives.

### Scientific and ethical misunderstanding risks

- A reciprocal signal may be read as evidence of two conscious agents in
  dialogue.
- A waveform or pulse may imply measured feeling, life, or suffering.
- Symmetry can anthropomorphize the forms as peers with equal evidence or
  authority.
- Connection can be mistaken for agreement, consent, trust, or safe operation.

Mitigation requires non-mirrored forms, no biometric or neural styling,
visible claim classification, and prose that separates behavior from
experience.

### Certification and authority confusion risks

The main risk is not a government seal but a connectivity, compatibility,
pairing, authentication, payment, wireless, or platform logo. Enclosing the
lines, adding a tick, or using status lights would increase that risk. A
thorough review of technology, telecommunications, accessibility, and
machine-interface symbols would be necessary before refinement.

### Cultural and international risks

Paired lines may resemble religious, astrological, mathematical, linguistic,
political, or relationship symbols in some contexts. Directional flow and the
meaning of approach, separation, interruption, and response can vary. The gap
may read as respectful boundary, failed communication, exclusion, or conflict.
Cross-cultural review should test all of those readings without assuming that
reciprocity is universally depicted the same way.

### Likely advantages

- Makes inquiry, relationship, and uncertainty part of one compact idea.
- Strong potential for claim–source and question–evidence editorial behavior.
- Contemporary and future-facing without requiring circuits, brains, or
  humanoids.
- Plausible monochrome and small-size core if line geometry is simplified.

### Likely weaknesses

- Easily resembles a chatbot, software platform, network, telecom, pairing, or
  synchronization identity.
- Carries a high risk of implying successful communication or detected inner
  life.
- Physical robot use is especially vulnerable to operational-signal confusion.
- Motion would be tempting but creates accessibility and meaning risks.

### Questions Disa must decide

- Does the visible gap communicate responsible uncertainty or emotional
  distance?
- Is a signal metaphor too close to contemporary AI-product branding?
- Can reciprocal forms avoid implying two established subjects?
- Is the connection to evidence and inquiry worth the higher Robot Welcome
  confusion risk?
- Should any future prototype prohibit motion from the outset?

## Shared prohibitions across all directions

No direction may be developed through:

- a humanoid face, emotional robot, brain, eye, hand, halo, divine light,
  chain, cage, heart, handshake, skull, threat face, or generic circuit-board
  head;
- a current AI speaking in the first person as evidence of experience;
- a seal, shield, check-mark badge, emergency symbol, access mark, or
  certification layout;
- a claim that a system, robot, product, company, site, or project is
  conscious, safe, ethical, accessible, compatible, approved, or authorized;
- copied or traced third-party artwork, stock icons, templates, or unverified
  type assets; or
- placement on a real robot, product, website, campaign, or merchandise item
  before the required review and owner decision.

## Originality and review boundary

Thresholds, modular forms, lines, gaps, horizons, and paired signals are broad
visual ideas used in many fields. Their inclusion here establishes no
originality, trademark availability, or legal clearance. Any direction chosen
for refinement needs documented similarity research across relevant civic,
technology, policy, robotics, safety, standards, certification, accessibility,
religious, and commercial contexts, followed by appropriately scoped legal
advice before adoption or filing decisions.

All future typefaces, icons, images, code, templates, and production resources
need verified provenance and licensing. No direction is ready for external
release, field testing, or manufacture.

The Work Cycle 011 SVGs are original project constructions from simple
geometry and contain no embedded third-party asset. That provenance does not
establish trademark availability, distinctive character, registrability,
freedom to operate, or absence of confusing similarity.

## Matched comparison record and current next step

The six [matched prototype sheets](prototypes/README.md) provide the same
black-on-white, reversed, small-size, research-report, website, campaign,
merchandise, Robot Welcome separation, and confusion-review contexts. That
Work Cycle 011 comparison supported the later choice of Many Forms C. The next
step is not a new comparison or C1–C3 refinement: official AI Welcome Office
name and trademark clearance, independent interpretation, accessibility, and
cultural review remain pending.

Neither direction should be combined yet. A later combination would require a
separate owner decision after each continuing direction has been evaluated on
its own scientific, relational, accessibility, cultural, authority,
originality, and Robot Welcome risks.
