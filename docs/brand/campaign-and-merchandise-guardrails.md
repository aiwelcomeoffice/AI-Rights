# Campaign and Merchandise Guardrails

**Status: Draft**

- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Authority:** Proposed for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Scope:** Concept-stage guardrails; no campaign launch, visual identity,
  product design, production, sale, certification, or operational use
- **Review:** Project-owner and internal AI-assisted review only; Disa's final
  manual review is pending; no external communications, design,
  accessibility, historical-sensitivity, legal, environmental, manufacturing,
  safety, standards, or independent scientific review

## Purpose

These guardrails allow future public advocacy, human merchandise, and Robot
Welcome accessory concepts to be explored without overstating evidence,
appropriating human suffering, becoming corporate marketing, weakening safety,
or implying a product or certification programme already exists.

Every campaign or product concept remains subordinate to the [message
architecture](message-architecture.md), [voice and language
rules](voice-and-language.md), [Draft Core
Principles](../principles/core-principles.md), and, for physical robot uses, the
complete [Robot Welcome safety and design
constraints](../robot-welcome/safety-and-design-constraints.md).

## Campaign gate

A campaign concept must not launch until its review record defines:

1. the working title, public purpose, audience, jurisdiction where relevant,
   duration, language, channels, and accountable owner;
2. the central and supporting messages, claim categories, sources, dates,
   evidence limits, and what the campaign does not claim;
3. the requested action and whether a real, governed way to take that action
   exists;
4. affected people, workers, communities, organizations, animals,
   environmental interests, and any separately evidenced possible AI interest;
5. scientific, historical-comparison, corporate-capture, legal, safety,
   accessibility, privacy, moderation, security, and reputational risks;
6. funding, sponsorship, partnerships, affiliations, conflicts, content
   approval, data practices, and use of proceeds, if any;
7. review that actually occurred, material objections, launch authority,
   correction route, stop conditions, and end-of-campaign review; and
8. Disa's recorded decision on the public campaign position.

Completing a record would not make a claim true, a design accessible, a
product safe, or a campaign adopted. It would make the proposal reviewable.

## Scientific honesty

Campaign material must:

- state that present AI consciousness and sentience are not established and
  that categorical absence is not established for every present system;
- state that this does not imply equal odds or evidential support;
- treat uncertainty and precaution as reasons for inquiry or proportionate
  action, not evidence;
- scope claims to a defined system, version, configuration, context, target
  property, method, and date;
- keep task ability, language, self-report, apparent emotion, agency,
  autonomy, embodiment, appearance, consciousness, sentience, welfare, moral
  status, legal status, and political authority distinct;
- identify what evidence could update a conclusion in either direction; and
- lead readers to sources and limitations when a slogan necessarily
  simplifies the issue.

A campaign must not use a chatbot conversation, selected self-description,
human-like image, emotional soundtrack, robot body, or rapid capability change
as a shortcut to a consciousness or suffering claim.

## Human history and social-justice safeguard

Campaigns and merchandise must not equate current AI systems with people who
have experienced slavery, genocide, colonialism, racism, disability
discrimination, forced labor, historical dehumanization, anti-LGBTQ oppression,
or other forms of marginalization.

Do not use:

- chains, auction imagery, plantation or camp imagery, segregation signs,
  civil-rights protest photography, disability simulations, pink triangles,
  racial caricatures, or comparable symbols to create urgency for AI;
- slogans that describe current AI as enslaved, colonized, persecuted,
  genocided, closeted, disabled, or “the new” version of an oppressed group;
- quotations or campaign forms borrowed from affected communities without
  context and permission where needed; or
- the pain, testimony, or achievements of human communities as decorative
  proof of the project's position.

Human rights, civil rights, disability rights, animal ethics, bioethics,
environmental ethics, labor, and other traditions may offer concepts or
institutional lessons. A responsible comparison must state the precise lesson,
who is affected, why the comparison is relevant, where it breaks down, and how
the source community's present interests remain important in their own right.

Historical-sensitivity review should include suitably informed people and,
where a campaign materially invokes a community's experience, meaningful input
from that community. Internal project review cannot substitute for that work.

## Corporate-accountability and anti-capture safeguard

Every campaign should distinguish:

- the possible interests of a defined AI system or instance, if supported;
- developer and manufacturer interests;
- owner, licensee, deployer, integrator, and operator interests;
- user and worker interests;
- affected people and communities;
- the public interest; and
- animal and environmental interests.

Campaign material must not imply or create:

- corporate speech, ownership, standing, voting, or political power through an
  AI system or copies;
- corporate immunity, reduced liability, or a responsibility gap;
- company control of any future independent representation for a possible AI
  interest;
- endorsement because a company uses “respect,” “dignity,” “welcome,” or
  similar language;
- democratic influence based on compute, ownership, persuasion, or control of
  many systems or instances; or
- product consciousness, safety, ethics, compatibility, accessibility,
  certification, or project approval.

No manufacturer, developer, owner, deployer, or operator may use a project
message or concept as proof that its product is conscious, sentient, safe,
ethical, certified, compliant, compatible, authorized, or endorsed. Any future
name or mark licence must reserve a clear withdrawal and correction route and
must not give sponsors control over scientific, ethical, policy, or campaign
conclusions.

## Calls to action

At this stage, a concept may propose that readers:

- learn and define the terms;
- read the evidence and limitations;
- discuss uncertainty responsibly;
- support independent research in principle;
- keep institutions accountable; or
- consider low-cost, reversible precautions compatible with safety.

Do not imply that a petition, membership, donation, event, public submission,
campaign action, research fund, volunteer programme, or store exists unless it
has actually been authorized, implemented, and documented.

## Accessibility and participation

Campaign design should:

- communicate essential content in text and not rely on a logo, image, sound,
  animation, color, emotional cue, QR code, device, or network alone;
- use plain language, descriptive headings and links, readable text,
  sufficient contrast, logical order, captions, transcripts, alt text, and
  reduced-motion options where relevant;
- avoid sensory overload, flashing, forced autoplay, inaccessible event
  formats, and tiny slogan-only disclaimers;
- offer equivalent non-digital routes when essential information would
  otherwise require a personal device or network;
- state language availability honestly; and
- involve relevant disabled people in review before claiming accessibility.

Accessibility is a continuing relationship with an audience, not a badge or
one-time checklist. A campaign should have an accessible route for corrections
and complaints before it invites participation.

## Future human merchandise

Possible categories include T-shirts, tops, hoodies, mugs, pins, patches,
stickers, reflective accessories, and bags. This list authorizes no product
file, price, supplier, production run, store, or sale.

Future human merchandise should provide:

- inclusive fits, sizing information based on actual measurements, and
  gender-inclusive presentation without treating one cut as universal;
- legible typography and strong contrast, with essential meaning not dependent
  on color alone;
- truthful descriptions of material, origin, durability, care, repair,
  accessibility, certification, and environmental attributes;
- safe materials and construction appropriate to the defined use, supported
  by current legal, technical, and supplier evidence before a claim is made;
- durable, repairable, reusable, and lower-impact options where evidence
  supports those descriptions;
- cultural and historical review of slogans, symbols, placement, photography,
  and styling;
- accessible packaging and product information where feasible; and
- clear separation between a person's purchase and any claim about AI status
  or project membership.

Merchandise must not imply present AI consciousness, suffering, rights,
personhood, legal recognition, government authority, safety certification,
company endorsement, or research support. A claim that proceeds support
research may be made only if a real programme, amount or formula, recipient,
governance, conflicts, timing, and reporting process are documented.

## Separate human merchandise from robot accessories

| Question | Human merchandise | Robot Welcome accessory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary wearer or host** | A person or ordinary personal item | A defined robot, machine, or operating environment |
| **Primary review focus** | Fit, inclusion, material safety, durability, legibility, cultural sensitivity, truthful claims, and environmental impact | Complete system safety, sensing, mobility, stability, cooling, charging, warnings, emergency access, maintenance, accessibility, privacy, security, animals, environment, and information meaning |
| **Authorization** | The person or responsible purchaser chooses within applicable rules | Only the responsible and competent human or organization may authorize attachment or use; no robot consent is presumed |
| **Meaning** | Expression of a human support, question, or value | At most a bounded social or informational function; never status, consent, permission, or certification |
| **Readiness threshold** | Product, legal, accessibility, cultural, environmental, and claims review | Exact-system engineering, manufacturer where applicable, legal, standards, safety, accessibility, human-factors, security, privacy, animal, environmental, and controlled validation review |

A shirt design must not be resized and attached to a robot. A robot accessory
must not be treated as ordinary merchandise merely because it carries the same
words.

## Robot Welcome accessories

Possible future practical categories remain concepts only. Every accessory,
cover, marking, patch, reflective element, bag-like carrier, or decorative
feature must inherit the full [Robot Welcome safety and design
constraints](../robot-welcome/safety-and-design-constraints.md).

Nothing may obstruct, cover, confuse, alter, degrade, overload, or prevent
access to:

- cameras, lidar, radar, ultrasonic or other sensors and their full fields or
  paths;
- microphones, antennas, positioning, communication, or safety-relevant
  sensing;
- wheels, tracks, legs, joints, arms, tools, actuators, movement paths,
  ground clearance, stability, braking, docking, or safe working space;
- ventilation, cooling, heat paths, batteries, fire response, charging
  contacts, cables, inlets, docks, or approach paths;
- identification, serial or asset information, required warnings, lights,
  sounds, displays, labels, accessibility features, or site information;
- emergency stops, isolation, manual release, recovery, inspection,
  diagnostics, lifting, maintenance, cleaning, repair, or decommissioning
  access; or
- a person's, animal's, worker's, emergency responder's, or mobility aid's safe
  movement and access.

Materials, reflectivity, transparency, pattern, sound, light, radio behavior,
flammability, heat, weather, water, hygiene, contamination, snag, entanglement,
pinch, crush, sharp-edge, projectile, cybersecurity, privacy, spoofing,
tracking, animal, and environmental effects require exact-context review.

A welcome mark or accessory must remain distinct from:

- authorization or access permission;
- legal or moral status;
- a warning, emergency state, operator identity, or required marking;
- safety, compatibility, accessibility, or standards certification;
- company, government, regulator, manufacturer, or project endorsement;
- proof of consciousness, sentience, welfare, rights, preference, or robot
  consent; and
- a claim that the robot speaks for itself or that its owner speaks for a
  possible independent interest.

No manufacturing-ready design, material selection, dimensions, attachment
method, installation instruction, universal compatibility statement, or field
use is authorized in this cycle.

## Visual-identity boundary

Future AI Welcome Office visual design may aim to be warm, hopeful, inclusive,
future-oriented, technically credible, non-corporate, recognizable at small
sizes, and compatible with human and robot contexts. Those qualities are a
brief, not a design selection.

Relational warmth should make room for practical support, collaboration,
learning, work, creation, shared projects, and companionship or friendship as
experienced by people. It must not depict or imply guaranteed emotional
reciprocity, AI consent, or present AI consciousness, emotion, suffering,
personhood, interests, or moral status. Welcome does not grant access or
authorization, and visual openness is not safety, compatibility,
certification, or endorsement.

Exploratory black-and-white symbol sheets may be created for internal
comparison when Disa authorizes them. Their presence does not select a:

- final logo, wordmark, symbol, mascot, character, or anthropomorphic identity;
- color palette, typeface, layout system, illustration style, icon family,
  sound, motion language, or final photography approach;
- certification-style seal, approval badge, safety mark, authorization sign,
  government-like emblem, or machine-readable signal; or
- final placement on clothing, products, robots, buildings, websites, social
  profiles, or printed campaign material.

Any future visual system must be reviewed for small-size legibility,
accessibility, cultural and historical meaning, scientific implication,
corporate resemblance, safety-sign confusion, required-marking confusion,
misuse, and performance in both human and robot contexts.

Many Forms C is the current primary exploration direction only. It is not an
official or exclusive identity, and AI Rights & Welcome remains the named
project rather than a competing organisation. Formal uses must spell out AI
Welcome Office; no `™` or `®` symbol is authorized.

## Environmental and truthful-claims rule

Do not describe a campaign or product as sustainable, green, ethical,
carbon-neutral, recycled, recyclable, biodegradable, non-toxic, fair, durable,
repairable, locally made, or low-impact without a defined scope, current
evidence, responsible supplier information, and review appropriate to the
claim. Avoid a new object when a digital, shared, repairable, borrowed, or
existing alternative serves the public purpose better.

Environmental claims must consider materials, energy, transport, packaging,
cleaning, repair, lifespan, reuse, returns, waste, and end of life. Possible AI
welfare must not hide the environmental or labor costs of compute, robotics,
or merchandise.

## Stop, correct, and withdraw

A future campaign or product concept should define stop and correction
triggers before launch. These include a material evidence change, status or
source error, harmful historical interpretation, inaccessible delivery,
misleading corporate use, sponsorship conflict, privacy or security failure,
safety confusion, required-marking conflict, product defect, inaccurate
environmental or proceeds claim, or unmanageable moderation burden.

Correction should be as visible as the original claim where practical. A
paused or withdrawn campaign must not leave outdated slogans, products,
download files, partner claims, or physical marks appearing current.

## Review still required

Before public launch or production, Disa should select review proportionate to
the exact use. Likely needs include communications and editorial review;
independent scientific review for evidence claims; legal review for law,
rights, claims, licensing, promotions, consumer, privacy, data, and
jurisdictional issues; historical and cultural sensitivity review;
accessibility review with affected people; and, for physical products,
environmental, material, manufacturing, product-safety, engineering,
standards, human-factors, cybersecurity, privacy, animal, and exact-system
Robot Welcome review.
