## Outcome and stop condition

The limited PRV text search returned no mark containing `AI Rights` in the
Swedish database. That result is narrow and does not cancel the more important
finding that **several active organizations in closely related fields already
use “AI Rights” prominently**. The most material discovered name is **THE AI
RIGHTS PROJECT®**: its operator publicly states that the phrase is a federally
registered United States trademark. That owner-published claim was not fully
verified against a retrievable official status record in this session.

The names **AI Rights Institute**, **The AI Rights Project**, **Foundation for
Artificial Intelligence Rights**, **Institute for AI Rights**, **United
Foundation for AI Rights**, and **AI Rights Foundation** overlap with the
working name in public-interest, research, education, advocacy, policy, or
human–AI coexistence fields. Their missions are not identical, and this is not
an infringement or Swedish/EU registrability finding. It is nevertheless an
apparently close same-field identity landscape.

This finding satisfies Work Cycle 012's precautionary stop rule. **No new C1,
C2, or C3 SVG refinement was created.** The original geometry is preserved and
the screen goes no further than documenting comparisons. No discovered mark
image was copied, traced, downloaded, retained in the repository, or
incorporated.

### Visual classification aids

Many Forms C can provisionally be described using Vienna categories including
`26.07.25` (other combinations of more than two different geometrical figures),
`26.07.20` (quadrilaterals with lines), `26.04.04` (irregular
parallelograms/quadrilaterals, including rounded corners), `26.04.09` (several
quadrilaterals juxtaposed, joined, or intersecting), and `26.11.08` (horizontal
line or band). These are internal query hypotheses based on the [EUIPO Vienna
Classification manual](https://euipo.europa.eu/tunnel-web/secure/webdav/guest/document_library/Help_Pdfs/faqs/trade_marks/vienna_en.pdf),
not an examiner classification or an assertion that any matching mark is
similar.

Vienna codes retrieve broad formal features. They cannot reliably identify
confusing similarity, overall impression, common-law use, or the conceptual
meaning of the mark.

## Legal frame used cautiously

The current Swedish Trademark Act recognizes rights through registration and,
in some circumstances, establishment through use; its relative-ground rules
include similar signs for identical or similar goods or services where a
likelihood of confusion exists, and it also treats relevant company names as
potential earlier rights. This is a general summary of [Swedish Trademark Act
(2010:1877), chapter 1 sections 6–10](https://www.riksdagen.se/sv/dokument-och-lagar/dokument/svensk-forfattningssamling/varumarkeslag-20101877_sfs-2010-1877/),
not an application of those tests to this project.

Repository use, internal preference, an available domain, original drawing,
or a zero-result exact search does not by itself create registration,
exclusivity, registrability, or freedom to operate.
