# Identity, Licensing, and Trademark Policy Proposal

**Status: Draft — Proposed for Disa's review; not adopted and not legal
advice**

- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Prepared by:** Sol (internal AI-assisted legal-policy synthesis)
- **Scope:** Future names and identity assets, existing Draft prototypes,
  community reuse, authorization, provenance, and professional-review triggers
- **Current effect:** None. This proposal does not change the repository
  licence, reserve existing assets, create ownership, register a mark, grant
  permission, prohibit use, or establish an exclusive right.

## Purpose

The repository currently uses the [CC0 1.0 Universal dedication](../../LICENSE).
That is well suited to open civic, educational, policy, and research material,
but a future official identity may need a separate, prospective treatment so
people can reuse knowledge freely without reasonably implying project
affiliation or endorsement.

This proposal separates four questions that should not be collapsed:

1. whether a work has copyright protection and who owns it;
2. what copyright or related rights the repository's CC0 dedication affects;
3. whether a sign functions or is registered as a trademark; and
4. whether a particular use creates confusion, false affiliation, or another
   legal issue in a particular jurisdiction.

## Current factual and legal posture

### Prototype provenance

The Work Cycle 011 SVG prototypes were generated through internal AI-assisted
collaboration using original geometric construction. The repository records no
stock art, icon-library component, third-party template, embedded font, or
discovered mark as a source. The files use local SVG geometry and generic
placeholder text.

That provenance is an **empirical project record**, subject to correction if
new information appears. Original construction does not establish copyright,
distinctiveness, registrability, freedom to operate, or absence of similarity
to earlier rights.

### AI-assisted copyright

The following is a cautious summary of current public guidance, not a final
legal opinion:

- [PRV's guidance on copyright in AI
  images](https://www.prv.se/sv/upphovsratt/for-anvandare/upphovsratt-pa-ai-bilder/)
  says copyright requires a human author and free, creative choices. It says
  an image created entirely by AI from a text prompt does not receive
  copyright, while a person who sufficiently transforms AI-assisted material
  may create protected material. The legal assessment is fact-specific and is
  ultimately for a court.
- An [EU Parliamentary Research Service briefing on generative AI and
  copyright](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/thinktank/en/document/EPRS_BRI%282025%29782585)
  describes an EU framework without specific rules for AI output and a general
  originality standard rooted in an author's own intellectual creation and
  free creative choices.
- The [European IP Helpdesk FAQ](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/regional-helpdesks/european-ip-helpdesk/europe-frequently-asked-questions_en)
  likewise describes human creative contribution as relevant and emphasizes
  case-by-case assessment.

For these prototypes, it is unresolved whether the recorded human selection,
direction, evaluation, and any later manual SVG decisions would be sufficiently
substantial and creative for copyright in all or part of a final asset. Do not
mark the current prototype as “copyright protected,” “copyright owned,” or
“public domain because AI made it” without scoped advice.

### AI-assisted marks and designs

[PRV's guidance on AI and registered
trademarks](https://www.prv.se/sv/varumarke/vad-ar-ett-varumarke/registrerade-varumarken-och-ai/)
states that the Swedish Trademark Act does not require a logo or mark to be
human-made; an AI-generated sign may therefore potentially be filed as a
trademark if the applicable requirements are met. Registration, use,
distinctiveness, earlier rights, ownership, and infringement remain separate
questions.

[PRV's guidance on AI and registered
designs](https://www.prv.se/sv/design/bra-att-veta-om-design/designskydd-och-ai/)
states that a completely AI-created design cannot be registered under the
current Swedish position because a human designer is required, while the
degree of acceptable AI assistance is not yet tested. This reinforces the need
for a factual human-contribution record and professional advice if design
registration is considered.

### CC0 and trademark are different

The repository's CC0 legal code permanently and irrevocably waives or licenses
the affirmer's copyright and related rights to the stated extent. It also says
that:

- trademark and patent rights are not waived, licensed, or otherwise affected;
- the affirmer makes no warranty of non-infringement; and
- the affirmer does not clear rights held by other people.

See [CC0 1.0 Universal legal code, sections 2–4](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en)
and the [CC0 deed](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

Consequently:

- CC0 does not itself grant or prove trademark rights;
- a trademark policy cannot silently retract the CC0 treatment already
  attached to copies of a copyright work;
- an affirmer cannot waive rights it does not own;
- future copies or assets may be placed under a different, clearly documented
  policy only prospectively and with proper authority; and
- misleading affiliation questions cannot be answered solely by reading the
  copyright licence.

Repository use, internal consensus, public display, a Git commit, or selection
as a primary exploration direction does not by itself establish a registration
or exclusive trademark right.

## Proposed policy

### 1 — Preserve open civic knowledge

Educational, scientific, research, policy, governance, and public-information
material should remain openly reusable under CC0 by default unless Disa adopts
a narrower exception for a specific justified asset. Open reuse should not be
conditioned on agreement with the project's views.

Factual source citations and third-party material remain governed by their own
rights and attribution requirements. CC0 should never be presented as clearing
third-party rights or validating a factual claim.

### 2 — Create a prospective official-identity exception only if adopted

Before any name or visual asset becomes official, Disa should decide whether
future official identity files and lockups will be explicitly excluded from
the repository's default CC0 treatment. If accepted, the implementation should:

- amend the root licensing notice prospectively and name the exact excluded
  files or directory;
- add a short `TRADEMARKS.md` or equivalent notice that names the owner,
  authorized marks, permitted uses, prohibited implications, contact route,
  and effective date;
- place a machine-readable and human-readable notice beside every official
  asset package;
- state that Drafts, historical prototypes, look-alikes, and project content
  are not made official merely by proximity;
- preserve the existing CC0 treatment of copies already distributed under
  CC0; and
- receive legal review before publication because a policy cannot manufacture
  ownership or retract prior permissions.

This proposal does **not** recommend changing the licence now. No official
identity exists, the name landscape is unresolved, and the prototype copies
already sit in a CC0 repository.

### 3 — Keep Draft prototypes unmistakably Draft

Draft prototype files and evaluation sheets should continue to state that they
are:

- exploratory internal studies, not official project marks;
- not approved for social profiles, campaigns, publications, products,
  garments, partnerships, fundraising, filing, Robot Welcome, access, safety,
  compatibility, certification, or endorsement;
- not evidence of consciousness, moral status, legal status, or authorization;
  and
- subject to replacement, pause, rejection, correction, or deletion from
  future official packages without implying that existing CC0 copies have been
  revoked.

Community members may discuss and evaluate Drafts within the applicable
licence. They should not be told that the project endorses, sponsors, certifies,
or is affiliated with their use unless an authorized project representative
has actually granted that relationship.

### 4 — Use a narrow affiliation rule

A future policy should welcome accurate statements such as “based on CC0
material from AI Rights & Welcome” when factually true, while prohibiting
claims that a reuse is official, certified, approved, sponsored, partnered,
authorized, or endorsed when it is not.

The project should respond proportionately:

1. preserve evidence and identify the exact statement, asset, audience, and
   likely harm;
2. distinguish good-faith attribution, criticism, parody, commentary,
   compatibility discussion, and independent reuse from misleading
   affiliation;
3. request a correction or disclaimer before escalation when appropriate;
4. prioritize public safety, fraud, fundraising, impersonation, discriminatory
   harm, false certification, and material public confusion; and
5. obtain legal advice before making legal threats, platform complaints,
   enforcement claims, or public accusations.

The project should not use identity policy to suppress criticism, competing
research, or good-faith disagreement.

### 5 — Assign ownership and authorization explicitly

No AI system, model provider, developer, platform, contractor, repository
host, or informal collaborator should be treated as owner or authorizer by
default. Possible future AI moral status is irrelevant to the current legal
chain-of-title record and must not become a route for corporate ownership or
immunity.

Before official adoption, a decision record should name:

- the human or legal entity that owns or is authorized to use each final name
  and asset;
- every human designer, contributor, employer, contractor, and tool involved;
- written assignments, licences, waivers, or permissions, if any;
- who may approve routine use, partnerships, merchandise, filings, licences,
  modifications, and enforcement;
- who remains accountable for representations and operational use; and
- what happens if the project changes owner, becomes an entity, dissolves, or
  forks.

Under the current project model, Disa remains the only formal decision-maker,
but that governance fact is not itself proof that Disa owns every possible IP
right. A future entity must not receive political legitimacy or immunity
through identity ownership.

### 6 — Maintain a human creative-decision record

For every candidate that proceeds, preserve:

- date, tool, model/system version where available, and prompt or design brief;
- the source geometry and hashes of reference versions;
- Disa's and other human contributors' selection, rejection, arrangement,
  proportion, spacing, shape, and revision decisions in their own words;
- AI-assisted proposals and reviews, labeled as such;
- manual vector edits and who made them;
- every external asset, font, template, licence, and permission—or an explicit
  statement that none was used;
- comparison and rights-search records without copying discovered marks; and
- the exact accepted file, decision record, licence notice, and release date.

The record should describe actual choices rather than inflate routine prompting
or approval into creative authorship. It supports provenance and later legal
analysis; it does not predetermine the legal result.

### 7 — Require professional advice at reliance points

Qualified IP advice becomes required before:

- adopting or publicly launching a project name, logo, lockup, campaign name,
  product-family identity, certification-like sign, or Robot Welcome sign;
- forming an entity or making a company-, association-, or domain-name
  commitment around the identity;
- filing, opposing, cancelling, licensing, assigning, or enforcing a trademark
  or registered design;
- changing the repository licence or excluding official assets from CC0;
- distributing official merchandise or entering manufacturing, sponsorship,
  fundraising, partnership, co-branding, or retail arrangements;
- responding to a close mark, demand, objection, takedown, or allegation;
- expanding into a new jurisdiction, language, or materially different goods
  and services;
- relying on copyright, design, passing-off, unfair-competition, or
  affiliation claims; or
- making a public statement about ownership, registration, exclusivity,
  clearance, infringement, or legal safety.

Advice should be scoped to actual territories and uses. “Reviewed by a lawyer”
should not be generalized beyond the reviewed facts, date, assets, and
questions.

## Draft implementation sequence

1. Disa decides whether the working name remains viable enough for a
   professional search or whether naming should reopen first.
2. A professional reviews the name and relevant symbol landscape in the
   intended territories and services.
3. Human creative and chain-of-title facts are completed for any selected
   refinement.
4. Disa chooses whether to keep official assets under CC0 or create a
   prospective, file-specific identity exception.
5. Legal review confirms the wording and transition before `LICENSE`, an asset
   directory, or a future `TRADEMARKS.md` changes.
6. A separate decision record adopts the exact name, files, owner,
   authorization policy, licence treatment, use scope, and review record.

No step authorizes public use until the later decision says so explicitly.

## Decisions for Disa

- Should the working name be professionally screened now, renamed now, or
  retained only as a private working name while alternatives are explored?
- Should future official identity assets be prospectively excluded from the
  repository's default CC0 treatment, or should the project intentionally
  keep them CC0 and manage only accurate affiliation statements?
- Who should own future official identity rights: Disa personally or a defined
  future legal entity, and under what transfer and accountability rules?
- Which accurate community-attribution uses should be expressly welcomed?
- Who may authorize partnerships, merchandise, filings, modifications, and
  enforcement?
- Which jurisdictions and concrete goods/services justify professional review
  before Work Cycle 013?
- Should the project commission a copyright/authorship assessment for a later
  human-revised asset, pursue trademark protection only, consider design
  protection, or rely on none of them?

Until those questions are decided, the root CC0 notice remains unchanged and
all existing visual prototypes remain Draft exploratory files with no claim of
exclusive rights, registration, or public-use authority.
