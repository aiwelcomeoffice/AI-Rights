## Context

AI Rights & Welcome already distinguishes Scaffold, Draft, Adopted, and
Superseded documents and states that repository presence does not create an
adopted position. It does not yet have an accepted procedure defining who may
adopt a position, what review must be recorded, how decision statuses differ
from document statuses, or how material disagreement and corrections are
handled.

The current project is developed primarily by Disa with AI-assisted
contributions from ChatGPT and Sol. Disa is the human project owner, repository
authority, and final formal decision-maker. ChatGPT and Sol contribute
analysis, drafting, review, and recommendations, but neither is an independent
human reviewer and neither can create formal adoption. Repeated internal AI
review does not become independent scientific peer review.

The project needs an honest bootstrap: the first governance process cannot be
approved under rules that do not yet exist. Disa's existing owner authority is
therefore the basis on which Disa may accept, reject, return, narrow, or revise
this proposal.

No existing Draft, Scaffold, or working research document is within the scope
of adoption by this record.

## Decision question

Should Disa approve the linked Draft adoption and review process as the
project's initial governance process, subject to Disa's resolution of the open
review points below?

## Options considered

### 1. Treat repository presence or internal review as adoption

This would be simple but would blur authorship, review, publication, and formal
authority. It could make incomplete evidence or repeated AI analysis appear
independently validated. This option is not recommended.

### 2. Use owner approval without a structured record

This would preserve Disa's authority with little administrative work, but it
would leave future readers unable to identify the adopted version, actual
review, limitations, disagreement, conflicts, or reasons for revision.

### 3. Use recorded owner adoption with proportionate review

Under this option, collaborators prepare and review Drafts; material choices
receive sequential decision records; Disa alone gives formal approval; and the
record identifies scope, version, review, limitations, disagreement,
conflicts, rationale, and revisit triggers. Specialist review is proportionate
to claim type and reliance. This is the proposed option.

### 4. Transfer adoption authority to an external board now

This could later broaden legitimacy and expertise, but no such board,
selection process, mandate, accountability structure, or public-participation
infrastructure exists. External reviewers can inform decisions without
automatically receiving ownership or adoption authority. This option is
premature.

## Open points for Disa

Disa should decide:

- whether Disa accepts sole final formal adoption authority;
- whether the role descriptions are accurate;
- which content types require independent specialist review before adoption;
- how separately configured AI review should be documented and combined with
  relevant independent human review;
- which proportionate thresholds should apply by document type without a
  fixed project-wide reviewer count;
- whether normative documents may be adopted before independent review;
- how much rationale an adoption record must contain;
- whether the disagreement and correction processes are practical; and
- whether the governance process is ready to be accepted.

## Decision

Unresolved. This record remains **Proposed**.

Disa may accept, reject, return, narrow, or revise the proposal during manual
review. Only Disa may set a decision date and change this record to
**Accepted** or **Rejected**. If Accepted, the exact approved governance
version or commit should be recorded where practical, and the linked
governance documents may then be marked **Adopted** within the accepted scope.

## Consequences if accepted

- Disa remains the sole final formal adoption authority unless a later
  Accepted governance decision changes that authority.
- Document statuses and decision statuses have separate meanings and
  transition rules.
- Material project positions require an Accepted decision record; routine
  editorial corrections do not.
- Review records must distinguish owner, internal AI-assisted, separately
  configured AI, independent human specialist, and public-consultation
  layers.
- Research verification remains separate from adoption.
- Material disagreement, corrections, source warnings, supersession, and
  conflicts of interest receive traceable treatment.
- Existing substantive Drafts and Scaffolds remain unchanged and unadopted.

The process adds documentation work proportional to the significance of a
decision. It may slow consequential adoption when evidence, expertise, or
independent review is insufficient; that is an intended safeguard.

## Consequences while unresolved or if rejected

The governance documents remain Drafts. The repository's existing status
conventions and Disa's bootstrap authority remain, but the proposed lifecycle
and review procedure does not become an adopted project rule. A revised
proposal may be recorded without pretending this proposal was accepted.

## Review and supersession triggers

If accepted, revisit the process when:

- project ownership or formal authority changes;
- public contribution or consultation infrastructure becomes active;
- a standing reviewer, advisory group, or governing body is proposed;
- separately configured AI review becomes a regular review layer or exposes a
  need to revise independence and conflict rules;
- the process proves too weak, burdensome, ambiguous, or vulnerable to
  conflicts or capture;
- law, safety needs, or project scale materially changes; or
- a governance failure, disputed adoption, or material correction exposes a
  gap.

A material governance change requires a new decision record that links to and,
where appropriate, supersedes this one.
