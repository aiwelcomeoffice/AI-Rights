# Markings, Signals, and Accessory Concepts

**Status: Draft**

- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Scope:** Conceptual communication and accessory ideas for responsible
  physical coexistence; no visual design, product design, or implementation
- **Authority:** Prepared for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Review:** Project-owner and internal AI-assisted review only; Disa's final
  manual review of this version is pending; no independent robotics, safety,
  accessibility, legal, standards, cybersecurity, privacy, or human-factors
  review

> **Concept notice:** Nothing in this document is a finalized symbol,
> specification, product, instruction, authorization, compatibility statement,
> safety certification, standards claim, or finding about robot consciousness,
> preference, or consent.

## Purpose

Voluntary markings and accessories could one day help people understand that a
space supports careful human–robot coexistence, find accountable information,
or make a machine more visible and maintainable. Those purposes are useful
only if the feature remains honest about what it communicates and does not
interfere with the machine or its surroundings.

Every concept here is subordinate to the [Safety and Design
Constraints](safety-and-design-constraints.md). No concept is ready for
manufacture, attachment, field trial, sale, or use.

## Keep six meanings separate

| Meaning | Proper question | What a Robot Welcome concept may do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Welcome** | Does a person, organization, or place encourage responsible coexistence? | Communicate a voluntary social intention, subject to local rules and safety. |
| **Safety** | What hazards, controls, warnings, and procedures apply to this exact machine and setting? | Point to accountable information without replacing required warnings, instructions, or validation. |
| **Identity** | Which machine, responsible organization, or information record is this? | Offer plain-language or authenticated reference information without implying trustworthiness. |
| **Authorization** | May this machine or person enter, operate, control, modify, or access something here? | Never grant or imply permission merely because a welcome feature is present. |
| **Certification** | Has a competent body assessed a defined claim against defined requirements? | Never imitate or claim certification. This project has created none. |
| **Legal or moral status** | What rights, duties, ownership, standing, or moral consideration applies, and under whose authority? | Make no determination. A welcome feature is not personhood, consent, rights, or political representation. |

A visual system should not collapse these meanings into a single badge, color,
sound, tag, or data field. Where relevant, identity, warnings, authorization,
and legal requirements must remain independently available even if a welcome
feature is removed.

## What a voluntary welcome message may communicate

Subject to later review, a human-readable concept might express one or more of
the following narrow ideas:

- “This robot is welcomed here.”
- “Please give this machine safe working space.”
- “Treat this machine responsibly.”
- “Operator and safety information is available.”
- “This environment supports respectful human–robot coexistence.”

These are proposed messages, not approved copy. The responsible organization,
scope, location, and information date would need to be clear. “Welcomed” must
remain conditional on applicable access rules, safety requirements, operator
instructions, and lawful emergency action.

## What a welcome message must never mean

A welcome message, mark, panel, tag, signal, or environmental feature must not
state or imply that:

- the robot is conscious, sentient, self-aware, capable of suffering, or a
  moral patient;
- the robot has legal personhood, rights, citizenship, political standing, or
  authority;
- the robot is harmless, risk-free, safe for unsupervised interaction, or safe
  to touch, approach, ride, or modify;
- the robot, accessory, operator, organization, or location is certified,
  approved, compliant, compatible, or endorsed;
- the machine is authorized to enter every place or disregard a site's access
  conditions;
- the operator, owner, manufacturer, developer, or linked information is
  trustworthy;
- the robot has consented to a marking, accessory, task, interaction, or data
  practice;
- a manufacturer, owner, operator, or project representative speaks for any
  possible interest of the robot; or
- a possible machine interest creates corporate rights or weakens human and
  organizational accountability.

The feature must not resemble a government, regulator, standards-body,
manufacturer, emergency, access-control, or certification mark. This Draft
does not choose a shape, icon, color, wordmark, logo, animation, sound, or
machine-readable code.

## Human-readable information concepts

Possible future information layers include:

- a short plain-language identity and responsibility panel;
- a high-visibility panel showing that operator and safety information is
  available;
- reflective or illuminated visibility elements that remain distinct from
  required warnings and do not impair people or sensors;
- a durable contact or incident-reporting reference;
- concise keep-clear or safe-working-space guidance supplied by the
  responsible organization; and
- tactile or audible access to essential information where visual-only
  communication excludes people.

Important information should identify its issuer, scope, last update, and a
non-digital route where reliance on a network or personal device would be
unsafe or exclusionary. A voluntary panel cannot replace a robot's official
identity, instructions, warnings, emergency information, or site controls.

## Machine-readable information concepts

QR or similar visual codes, NFC or other local mechanisms, and
machine-readable environmental markers could link to information or help a
machine recognize a voluntary environmental cue. They remain only concepts.

A future proposal would need to define:

- the exact information and permitted action, if any;
- the issuer, accountable data owner, and update responsibility;
- authenticity, integrity, expiry, revocation, replacement, and offline
  behavior;
- who can read or write the mechanism and what is logged;
- personal-data, location, surveillance, and tracking limits;
- accessible human alternatives;
- security testing and incident response;
- conflicts with required markings, site systems, or other robots; and
- the safe result of missing, damaged, malicious, duplicated, or outdated
  information.

No machine-readable welcome cue should override or weaken:

- the robot's safety policy or protective functions;
- site access control or physical security;
- geofencing or location restrictions;
- operator or emergency commands;
- human authority and responsible supervision;
- required warnings, identification, or operating constraints; or
- applicable law and emergency response.

“Machine-readable” does not mean “machine-authoritative.” A cue may be data
without being permission, a command, or a trustworthy statement.

## Information risk analysis

| Risk | Example failure | Conceptual safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spoofing** | A copied code falsely identifies an operator or makes a site appear welcoming. | Authenticated provenance where proportionate; visible issuer and scope; never treat the cue alone as authority. |
| **Malicious replacement** | A sticker or tag redirects people to unsafe instructions or changes machine behavior. | Tamper awareness, controlled replacement, independent contact route, and no safety-critical dependency. |
| **Unwanted tracking** | Stable identifiers allow observers to follow a robot, operator, worker, resident, or visitor. | Data minimization, contextual identifiers, access controls, retention limits, and privacy review. |
| **Personal-data exposure** | A public panel reveals an operator's name, schedule, phone number, or live location. | Publish only necessary organizational contact and incident information; define lawful access to restricted details. |
| **Surveillance** | Interaction with a code or tag logs a person's device or location without a clear need. | Prefer no tracking, give notice where data is collected, provide offline alternatives, and obtain applicable review. |
| **False authorization** | A robot or person treats “welcome” as permission to enter or control equipment. | Separate access decisions technically and visually; deny by default when an authorization source is absent or invalid. |
| **Outdated information** | Emergency or operator details remain visible after reassignment or a system change. | Visible update date, owner, expiry, review triggers, revocation, and safe failure. |
| **Inaccessible format** | Essential information exists only in a visual code or smartphone webpage. | Plain-language and multimodal alternatives; test with relevant disabled people. |
| **Network dependency** | A page is unavailable during an incident. | Keep required and emergency information local and independently available. |
| **Required-marking confusion** | A welcome badge looks like a warning, official approval, or compliance label. | Distinct placement and language; legal, standards, manufacturer, accessibility, and human-factors review before use. |

## Accessory concept catalogue

The catalogue records ideas for later research. The phrase “sensor-safe” is
not used as a product claim: only exact-system validation could establish that
a particular design does not impair relevant sensing or operation.

Every entry inherits the complete constraints for sensors, mobility and
stability, ventilation and cooling, batteries and charging, warnings and
identification, emergency controls, accessibility features, inspection, and
maintenance access. The shorter concept-specific fields below do not narrow or
replace that system-wide checklist.

### Visibility and information concepts

#### 1. Removable reflective panel

- **Intended benefit:** Improve detection of the machine or a keep-clear area
  in relevant low-light settings without altering required warnings.
- **Intended users:** Pedestrians, workers, operators, maintainers, cyclists or
  drivers where relevant, and emergency responders.
- **Safety constraints:** Exact optical and sensor effects; glare and dazzle;
  secure attachment; complete sensor, ventilation, movement, warning,
  emergency, charging, and service clearance; weather and cleaning behavior.
- **Possible misuse:** Resembling an official warning or authorization mark;
  implying safe approach; using high reflectivity where it impairs people or
  sensors; attaching it to an unassessed robot.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system optical, sensing, mechanical,
  environmental, accessibility, human-factors, legal, and applicable standards
  review, plus manufacturer approval where applicable.
- **Who benefits:** Primarily people, operators, and emergency responders;
  possibly property and equipment through collision avoidance.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None.

#### 2. Operator-information holder

- **Intended benefit:** Keep replaceable, plain-language responsibility,
  reporting, or inspection information in a known location.
- **Intended users:** Bystanders, workers, operators, maintainers, property
  controllers, and emergency responders.
- **Safety constraints:** No obstruction or snag point; legibility and
  accessibility; secure but maintainable attachment; data minimization;
  offline usefulness; distinction from required identity and safety labels.
- **Possible misuse:** Exposing personal data; displaying stale or false
  contacts; implying the named party is trustworthy, certified, or the sole
  accountable actor.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system mechanical and access review, privacy and
  security review, responsibility mapping, accessibility testing, and review
  of applicable labeling duties.
- **Who benefits:** People, responsible organizations, operators, maintainers,
  and incident responders.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None; a future evidence-based welfare reporting
  route could be added only through separate review.

#### 3. Multimodal accessibility cue

- **Intended benefit:** Provide equivalent orientation or state information
  through a carefully selected combination of text, contrast, tactile,
  audible, or other modes.
- **Intended users:** Disabled people, bystanders, workers, operators, and
  visitors with varied language or device access.
- **Safety constraints:** No sensory overload, alarm confusion, masking of
  required sounds, trip or reach hazard, ambiguous touch invitation, or
  dependence on a single mode; protect privacy in audible output.
- **Possible misuse:** Treating a nominal tactile or audible feature as proof
  of accessibility; inviting touch near a hazard; revealing private
  information; using a friendly voice as anthropomorphic pressure.
- **Validation needed:** Co-design and testing with relevant disabled people,
  exact-system safety and human-factors assessment, acoustic and tactile review,
  privacy review, and applicable requirements research.
- **Who benefits:** People with varied access needs, plus operators and site
  controllers through clearer interaction.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None.

### Protection and maintenance concepts

#### 4. Task-specific weather cover

- **Intended benefit:** Protect an authorized, stationary, or otherwise
  precisely defined part of a machine from a defined environmental exposure.
- **Intended users:** Owners, operators, maintainers, and property controllers.
- **Safety constraints:** No use beyond an exact validated state; ventilation,
  heat, water, drainage, wind loading, sensors, antennas, charging, movement,
  energy isolation, fire access, controls, and inspection remain addressed.
- **Possible misuse:** Operating or charging the robot while covered outside
  the validated condition; retaining heat or water; concealing damage;
  implying all-weather capability or universal fit.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system manufacturer review where applicable;
  mechanical, thermal, fire, electrical, environmental, cleaning,
  electromagnetic, operational-state, and removal testing.
- **Who benefits:** Owners, operators, maintainers, property, infrastructure,
  and the machine as equipment.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None.

#### 5. Repairable modular covering

- **Intended benefit:** Allow a defined protective or appearance surface to be
  repaired or replaced without discarding a larger assembly.
- **Intended users:** Maintainers, owners, operators, and organizations seeking
  longer service life and lower material waste.
- **Safety constraints:** Structural and fire behavior; secure interfaces;
  inspection visibility; no altered loads, cooling, sensing, movement,
  cleaning, emergency, or maintenance access; controlled replacement parts.
- **Possible misuse:** Concealing structural damage; unauthorized replacement;
  using incompatible materials; treating modularity as permission for public
  modification.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system engineering, lifecycle, maintenance,
  materials, environmental, cleaning, and quality-control review, with
  manufacturer approval where applicable.
- **Who benefits:** Maintainers, operators, owners, the environment through
  possible waste reduction, and the machine as maintainable equipment.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None; repair should not be described as welfare
  care without separate evidence and argument.

### Soft appearance and personalization concepts

#### 6. Soft decorative panel

- **Intended benefit:** Add a warm, replaceable visual or tactile element in a
  precisely defined low-risk context without requiring a human-like body or
  identity.
- **Intended users:** Site communities, visitors, designers, and operators.
- **Safety constraints:** No loose fibers, ingestion, flammability, hygiene,
  heat, static, snag, entanglement, sensor, movement, warning, or cleaning
  risk; no invitation to touch a moving or hazardous machine.
- **Possible misuse:** Anthropomorphic manipulation; toy-like attraction for
  children or animals; obstruction; unapproved attachment; gendering or
  assigning a social role to the robot.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system mechanical, material, fire, hygiene,
  sensory, accessibility, child and animal, human-factors, and maintenance
  review.
- **Who benefits:** Primarily people and the social environment; possibly
  operators if clear personalization improves identification.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None unless future evidence supports a stable,
  relevant machine preference for appearance; no such preference is assumed.

#### 7. Non-obstructive personalization carrier

- **Intended benefit:** Provide a controlled place for authorized names,
  artwork, community messages, or replaceable appearance elements without
  placing items elsewhere on the robot.
- **Intended users:** Owners, operators, maintainers, designers, and site
  communities.
- **Safety constraints:** Exact approved content area; no warning, identity,
  access, or certification confusion; no discriminatory or manipulative
  content; all physical and information constraints remain satisfied.
- **Possible misuse:** Advertising, surveillance codes, political capture,
  false endorsement, harassment, dehumanizing imagery, unauthorized claims of
  robot feelings or consent, or concealment of responsible ownership.
- **Validation needed:** Exact-system engineering and accessibility review;
  content governance, privacy, intellectual-property, legal, ethical, and
  human-factors review; lifecycle and removal process.
- **Who benefits:** People, organizations, and maintainers through controlled
  communication and easier restoration.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None at present; any future preference input would
  require the procedure in the [Physical Coexistence
  Principles](physical-coexistence-principles.md#10-create-a-careful-procedure-for-possible-future-preferences).

### Interaction-boundary concept

#### 8. Defined safe-working-space indicator

- **Intended benefit:** Help people understand a responsible organization's
  defined keep-clear or interaction boundary for a specific task and site.
- **Intended users:** Pedestrians, workers, disabled people, operators,
  maintainers, and site controllers.
- **Safety constraints:** The actual boundary must come from validated system
  and site analysis; the indicator must remain detectable through varied modes,
  avoid trip or access barriers, and never be attached in a way that changes
  the operating envelope.
- **Possible misuse:** Inventing an arbitrary clearance; claiming a moving
  visual element creates a safety zone; excluding people from accessible or
  public space; treating the indicator as a substitute for guarding,
  supervision, or control functions.
- **Validation needed:** Exact robot, task, environment, movement, stopping,
  site, accessibility, human-factors, legal, and applicable standards review;
  controlled testing with affected users.
- **Who benefits:** People, operators, maintainers, property controllers, and
  animals where human supervision uses the cue appropriately.
- **AI-welfare dependency:** None.

## Concept comparison

| Category | Current-value basis | Possible-interest relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visibility and information | Safety, accessibility, accountability, and incident response | Could later provide a route for responsibly labeled concern reporting; does not establish welfare |
| Protection and maintenance | Equipment protection, repairability, reliable operation, and environmental benefit | Could matter to a defined present or future system only if continuity or welfare relevance were independently supported |
| Appearance and personalization | Human social environment, identification, warmth, and controlled expression | A stable preference in a defined present or future system might become one input; generated persona output is not enough |
| Interaction boundary | Safety, predictable movement, accessible shared-space management | No welfare assumption is needed |

## Concept lifecycle

Any future mark, signal, or accessory proposal should record:

1. its exact issuer, intended meaning, users, beneficiaries, and prohibited
   interpretations;
2. the defined robot, configuration, task, mode, site, and date;
3. accountable manufacture, installation, data, operation, inspection,
   maintenance, incident, and removal roles;
4. applicable manufacturer instructions, law, site rules, and standards;
5. accessibility, safety, security, privacy, animal, environmental, and
   human-factors review;
6. validation evidence and important limitations;
7. expiry, update, damage, revocation, correction, and removal rules; and
8. triggers for reassessment after system, software, payload, environment,
   task, evidence, or legal change.

No mark should remain in use after the responsible issuer, scope, information,
or validation has become unclear or outdated. Removal of a voluntary welcome
feature must not remove required identity, warning, authorization, or emergency
information.

## Open design questions

This cycle deliberately leaves the visual answer open. Disa and future
reviewers should decide:

- whether a voluntary Robot Welcome marking is useful enough to pursue;
- which narrow meaning or meanings it should carry;
- how to keep it visibly unlike a certification, access, warning, manufacturer,
  government, or legal-status mark;
- whether it belongs on a robot, in an environment, in operator information,
  or some combination;
- how people can verify issuer, scope, and freshness without surrendering
  privacy or requiring a personal device;
- which concepts deserve robotics, accessibility, human-factors, legal,
  standards, security, privacy, animal, and environmental research first; and
- whether any concept remains useful after its foreseeable misuse is weighed.

No logo, symbol, product, prototype, dimension, material selection,
manufacturing file, or compatibility list is created or approved by this
Draft.
