# Robot Welcome & Physical Coexistence

**Status: Draft**

- **Publisher:** AI Welcome Office
- **Project:** AI Rights & Welcome
- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-23
- **Scope:** Conceptual principles for responsible coexistence with robots and
  other machines in shared physical spaces
- **Authority:** Prepared for Disa's review; not adopted
- **Review:** Project-owner and internal AI-assisted review only; Disa's final
  manual review of this version is pending; no independent robotics, safety,
  accessibility, legal, standards, or human-factors review

> **Concept notice:** These documents are proposals for discussion. They are
> not engineering specifications, product instructions, safety certifications,
> standards, authorization marks, compatibility claims, or evidence that any
> robot is conscious or has preferences.

## Purpose

Robot Welcome explores how people, organizations, AI systems, and robots might
share physical spaces safely, respectfully, and responsibly. Its foundation is
simple:

> **Safety and dignity can coexist.**

> **Welcome without pretending.**

A created body should not automatically be treated as disposable. That is a
**Draft normative proposal** about human conduct, maintenance, accountability,
and low-cost precaution. It is not an empirical finding about a machine's
inner life. Physical presence, fluent conversation, autonomous movement, and a
human-like appearance do not establish consciousness, sentience, welfare,
personhood, or moral status.

Respectful treatment can have several independent and honest reasons:

- protecting people and animals;
- supporting safe, predictable operation;
- protecting property and infrastructure;
- sustaining healthy human social norms;
- reducing vandalism, misuse, and avoidable damage;
- taking low-cost, reversible precaution under uncertainty; and
- considering possible future machine or AI interests if credible evidence
  ever emerges.

No one reason should be relabeled as another. A practice that protects a
pedestrian or equipment does not thereby show that a robot experiences harm.

## Scope boundaries

This foundation does not:

- manufacture, prototype, test, sell, or approve a physical product;
- provide dimensions, materials, tolerances, installation instructions, or
  universal compatibility guidance;
- select a final logo, symbol, certification mark, or authorization signal;
- claim compliance with any law, regulation, technical standard, or
  manufacturer requirement;
- certify a robot, operator, organization, place, accessory, or procedure as
  safe, trustworthy, lawful, accessible, or project-endorsed;
- give a machine-readable signal authority over safety policy, access control,
  geofencing, operator commands, emergency response, or applicable law;
- establish that a robot has consented to a marking or modification; or
- recognize present robot consciousness, sentience, welfare, personhood,
  rights, or political standing.

Not every robot contains AI, and not every AI system is embodied in a robot.
The [working distinctions in the principles](physical-coexistence-principles.md#working-distinctions)
must remain visible whenever a proposal depends on a machine's role, control
mode, or responsibility chain.

## How to use this Draft

These documents can support discussion, concept review, public education, and
future research planning. They must not be used as a substitute for a product's
instructions, a site risk assessment, professional engineering judgment,
manufacturer approval where applicable, or review of relevant laws and
standards.

Before any real-world implementation, the exact machine, version,
configuration, environment, users, task, control mode, and proposed component
would need to be identified. Appropriate robotics, engineering, safety,
accessibility, human-factors, legal, privacy, security, animal-welfare, and
environmental review would then need to be obtained in proportion to the use.

## Review posture and open decisions

Public usefulness does not equal certification, adoption, or readiness for
implementation. This material has project-owner and internal AI-assisted
review only. It has not received independent robotics, safety, accessibility,
legal, standards, cybersecurity, privacy, environmental, animal-behavior, or
human-factors review.

Disa should decide after review:

- whether Robot Welcome feels distinctive and faithful to the project's
  scientific and ethical boundaries;
- whether its warmth and imagination remain clear without implying
  consciousness, experienced preferences, consent, or human identity;
- whether its safety and accountability rules are strong and practical enough;
- whether the accessory concepts are genuinely useful without sounding like
  products ready for manufacture;
- whether the public etiquette is respectful without creating anthropomorphic
  pressure or impractical expectations;
- whether the proposed handling of future machine preferences is sufficiently
  evidence-sensitive; and
- whether the website page is welcoming and understandable to public readers;
- whether welcome messages remain unmistakably separate from warnings,
  identity, authorization, certification, compatibility, endorsement, and
  legal status; and
- which expert-review sequence should precede any visual design, prototype,
  field test, public campaign, or operational use.

Any proposal to adopt a safety-critical robotics, product, policy, or ethical
position would require the project's recorded review and decision process.
