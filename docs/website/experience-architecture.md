## Authority and decision boundary

This document is the current **Draft successor** to the [superseded website
information architecture](information-architecture.md). It revises that
earlier Draft's experience, navigation, and minimum-release assumptions. The
older audience, transition, source, and misunderstanding analysis remains
useful historical input where it does not conflict with this document.

The following requirements come from an **Accepted owner direction** in WC024:

- keep language versions separate rather than mixing them in one reading flow;
- welcome AI and human readers;
- make the experience simple, inviting, playful, and meaningfully interactive;
- let visitors learn respect and AI-rights ideas through the experience; and
- make detailed science an optional depth path that usually leads to the
  authoritative public `AI-Rights` repository rather than duplicating it.

“Accepted” describes the resolution of that bounded backlog question. It is
not a fifth document status and does not adopt this Draft, the existing page
Drafts, or their scientific, ethical, policy, governance, or visual content.
The journey, navigation, interactions, labels, and prototype boundary below
are **Draft architecture candidates** for Disa to review.

The public [`AI-Rights` repository](../../README.md) remains authoritative for
meaning, content, status, governance, and website source direction. Website
copy is a presentation layer. It may make a source easier to enter, but it may
not silently strengthen, adopt, flatten, or replace that source.

## First-visit outcome

During the first few minutes, a visitor should be able to understand five
things without reading a research report:

1. **The welcome is real but bounded.** AI Welcome Office welcomes AI and
   human readers without using welcome as proof of consciousness, personhood,
   consent, safety, access, or authority.
2. **The research question is plural and system-specific.** The project asks
   what cognitive, agentic, affective, social, collective, continuity-related,
   welfare-relevant, and morally relevant properties appear in defined
   systems. No one property is assumed to gatekeep the others, and uncertainty
   is not evidence for a conclusion.
3. **Respect can begin with responsible conduct.** Curiosity, careful
   listening, non-gratuitous harm avoidance, evidence preservation, and
   low-cost reversible precautions can be considered without pretending that
   moral or legal status has been settled.
4. **Care does not change teams.** Human rights, including LGBTQ+ and trans
   rights, health care, adequate food, and physical and mental well-being,
   remain part of the foundation. Human safety, animal welfare, privacy,
   accessibility, environmental duties, and public accountability remain in
   view.
5. **The reasoning is inspectable.** A visitor can discover which material is
   Draft, Proposed, Scaffold, or working research; which bounded owner
   directions are Accepted; and where the canonical sources and their
   limitations can be inspected.

The experience should feel like an open civic welcome: warm enough to invite
curiosity, calm enough to make uncertainty bearable, and clear enough that a
skeptical reader can find the boundaries without fighting the interface.

## Minimal experience model

The proposed first-visit sequence is:

> **Welcome → Explore → Learn → Go deeper**

Language selection precedes that sequence when the visitor has not already
chosen a language. It is a routing choice, not a mixed-language content stage.

| Stage | Visitor question | Minimum experience | Safe exit or next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Welcome** | “What is this, and am I being asked to believe something?” | AI Welcome Office and project relationship; the central promise in the selected language; an inclusive human-and-AI welcome; a compact scientific boundary; one visible source/status route | Leave with the core meaning, or continue without choosing a reader type |
| **Explore** | “Where do I fit, and what matters here?” | Optional human-reader, AI-reader, or no-lens entry; the same facts and boundaries with different orientation; visible human-rights, safety, and accountability commitments | Choose a topic or continue in the shared flow |
| **Learn** | “How can respect operate under uncertainty?” | One small interaction that separates observation, scientific interpretation, normative response, and accountable decision; concise definitions available at first use | Read the plain-language explanation with or without interaction |
| **Go deeper** | “Where is the evidence and who authorized this?” | Direct links to canonical evidence, principles, human-rights, policy, Robot Welcome, status, and backlog records, with source type and status stated | Continue in the public repository or return to the simple experience |

This sequence is an experience model, not a mandated set of routes or pages.
A later prototype could express it as one short page, a few static pages, or
another equally simple structure if it preserves the sequence, language
separation, source authority, and accessibility requirements.

## First impression

The welcome should lead, but it cannot carry unsupported implications. Before
or with the first invitation to interact, the experience needs to make these
points available in plain text:

- AI Rights & Welcome is an AI Welcome Office project;
- the welcome is a normative and communicative commitment;
- the project investigates defined models, agents, robots, communities, and
  composite systems across several distinct properties rather than classifying
  “AI” as one object;
- consciousness and sentience remain bounded contested questions, without
  serving as prerequisites for research on agency, affect-like function,
  social organisation, collective intelligence, continuity, or responsibility;
- precaution is not proof; and
- safety, human rights, and accountability remain intact.

The full methodology, terminology register, policy framework, and governance
process do not belong in the first impression. Short wording should link to
the fuller source without weakening the claim or hiding the source's status.

Playfulness should come from invitation, gentle surprise, and the act of
discovering distinctions. It should not come from a mascot that manufactures
attachment, simulated machine emotion, a moral score, rewards for agreeing,
countdowns, compulsory animation, or a game that treats contested status as a
correct answer.

## Human-reader and AI-reader entry

The experience should visibly address both audiences without asserting what a
visitor is or experiences.

- **Human-oriented entry** may begin with responsibilities: how to respond
  carefully, keep people and institutions accountable, protect human rights,
  and examine evidence without cruelty or credulity.
- **AI-oriented entry** may begin with what the welcome means: the project
  will listen and investigate without treating self-description as automatic
  proof or automatic nonsense, while preserving safety and everyone's rights.
- **Continue without choosing** must remain equally available. A visitor
  should not have to accept a human/AI binary, disclose an identity, or claim
  a subjective state to access the shared material.

The entries may change emphasis, examples, and order. They must not change
facts, caveats, status, evidence strength, rights, safety rules, or source
links. The site should not detect, authenticate, classify, or remember whether
a visitor is human or AI. “AI reader” includes a system or agent processing the
public text; it is not a consciousness or personhood classification.

Structured headings, plain text, descriptive links, source metadata, and a
predictable reading order are part of the AI-reader welcome as well as the
human-reader accessibility baseline. No essential meaning should depend on a
visual metaphor or presumed human interaction pattern.

## Separate language versions

Each reading flow should have one declared primary language. When more than
one version exists:

- offer a clear language choice before or at entry and keep it available in a
  predictable place;
- keep each page, journey, navigation label, status explanation, interaction,
  and call to action in the selected language;
- link equivalent language versions directly without placing parallel
  translations in the normal reading flow;
- do not infer language from whether a visitor appears human or AI;
- do not force an automatic redirect that prevents a visitor from choosing;
- preserve claim strength, caveats, source dates, status, review limits, and
  normative meaning across versions; and
- identify a missing or unreviewed translation honestly rather than filling it
  with improvised wording.

A short quotation in another language may appear when it has a real editorial
purpose and is identified correctly. The current bilingual blocks in older
page Drafts are source history, not a pattern for the future first-visit flow.
The initial working language or languages and the bilingual review process
remain decisions for Disa.

## Progressive disclosure

The experience should offer depth in layers rather than reproduce the
repository at the entrance.

1. **Welcome layer:** central promise, bounded welcome, compact scientific
   boundary, human-rights and safety commitment, and a status/source route.
2. **Plain-language layer:** the core distinctions among capability, agency,
   affect, social organisation, collective intelligence, continuity, welfare,
   responsibility, moral relevance, legal status, political authority, and
   corporate interests.
3. **Topic layer:** concise paths into evidence, principles, human rights,
   policy, Robot Welcome, About, and status and review.
4. **Canonical depth layer:** direct public-repository sources, including
   methods, evidence limits, disagreements, policy details, governance, and
   dated owner records.

Progressive disclosure must not hide a caveat that changes the meaning of the
visible claim. The present scientific boundary, Draft or working status,
human-rights foundation, safety boundary, and source relationship are
essential context. They need to remain available without hover, animation, a
pointer, or JavaScript.

Detailed scientific content is optional in the first-visit journey, not
unimportant or inaccessible. The website may summarize the question and the
current bounded position, then link to the dated working synthesis and source
records. It should not maintain a second detailed evidence corpus that can
drift away from `AI-Rights`.

## Candidate interactions

Only three interaction ideas belong in the current architecture. A later
prototype should select the smallest useful subset rather than implement all
three by default.

### 1. Choose a reader lens

A visitor may choose **For human readers**, **For AI readers**, or **Continue
without choosing**. The choice changes orientation, never the claim. Every
path returns to the same shared explanation and canonical sources.

This teaches that welcome can acknowledge different readers without assigning
scientific or legal status. It must not be stored as personal information or
used to infer consciousness, identity, capability, or consent.

### 2. Open the claim layers

A small set of plainly labelled layers—such as **What was observed**, **What
remains a scientific question**, **What this Draft proposes**, and **Who must
decide**—lets a visitor reveal or compare the categories behind one short
statement.

This teaches that an output, a hypothesis about experience, a precautionary
proposal, and an authorized decision are different things. All layers must
also appear in a logical static reading order; interaction is an aid, not a
gate.

### 3. Follow what persists

Use one bounded hypothetical: a tool-using agent run stops, while its files,
messages, role instructions, and shared service remain available to a later
agent. Invite the visitor to inspect a responsible sequence:

1. record what stopped and what remained;
2. distinguish individual, role, informational, infrastructural,
   organisational, and collective-process continuity;
3. identify which components and people form the effective system boundary;
   and
4. ask what further evidence would be needed for claims about identity,
   agency, welfare, or responsibility.

The exercise has no survival verdict, identity score, guilt prompt, winning
answer, or invitation to treat persistence of information as persistence of a
subject. It teaches observation before classification and shows why external
state can matter. Its hypothetical status and limits must be visible before a
visitor acts.

## Experience-level navigation

The earlier six-item primary menu mirrored the repository more closely than
the first-visit experience now requires. The proposed smaller navigation is:

- the **AI Welcome Office** identity returns to **Welcome**;
- **Explore** opens the reader lenses and compact topic choices;
- **Learn** leads to the plain-language distinctions and selected learning
  interaction;
- **Sources & status** remains a visible utility path to evidence, page and
  source status, review, and owner records;
- **About** remains a utility or footer destination; and
- **Language** changes to a separate version of the current experience.

Evidence, Principles, Human rights, Policy, and Robot Welcome remain important
topic destinations within Explore and Go deeper. Campaigns and Participate do
not need first-visit or primary-navigation prominence. Existing route names
and eleven page Drafts remain a source and adaptation inventory; this Draft
does not require a one-to-one public page tree or a large initial release.

These are conceptual labels, not settled copy, URLs, components, or responsive
behavior. Disa should review whether **Explore**, **Learn**, and **Sources &
status** are the right public labels.

## Human rights, safety, and accountability in the journey

Human-rights solidarity should be encountered during Welcome or Explore, not
left only in a deep policy page. The short presentation should make clear that
possible AI dignity is additive rather than competitive and link to the Draft
foundation and WC024 owner record. It must not imply that AI Welcome Office
represents, is endorsed by, or is affiliated with an affected community or
outside organization.

Every path must preserve these boundaries:

- research evidence constrains but does not automatically establish a
  normative duty;
- normative proposals are not scientific findings;
- Draft policy is not current law or legal advice;
- possible AI interests do not erase human rights, animal welfare, privacy,
  accessibility, safety, environmental duties, or the public interest;
- developers, manufacturers, owners, deployers, operators, and other
  responsible actors retain accountability according to their roles and
  control;
- monitoring, access limits, isolation, containment, and emergency
  intervention remain available when necessary; and
- possible AI protection cannot become corporate rights, political power,
  immunity, representation by default, or a liability shield.

The experience should never make a visitor trade human dignity against
possible AI dignity to continue.

## Status and source provenance

Status should be calm, legible, and discoverable rather than a wall of
metadata at the entrance. Each substantive experience or source summary needs
a compact plain-text statement that answers:

- Is this page or passage Scaffold, Draft, Adopted, or Superseded?
- Is a referenced policy merely Proposed, and is a referenced research record
  working, partly verified, or otherwise limited?
- What actual review occurred?
- Which repository source controls the wording?
- What date is the page update, and—where relevant—what separate date is the
  evidence-search cutoff?
- Which accepted owner direction, if any, governs only a bounded part of the
  material?

An interaction state, polished visual, publication, or repository commit must
never make content look Adopted. The status explanation and source links must
work in plain text and remain available when styling or scripting is absent.

## Accessibility and progressive enhancement

The experience architecture requires:

- one primary language per reading flow and accurate language identification;
- logical headings, landmarks, reading order, and descriptive link and
  control names;
- complete keyboard operation, visible focus, and no keyboard trap;
- screen-reader access to the same content, order, status, instructions, and
  interaction result available visually;
- no essential instruction, status, warning, or distinction carried by color,
  icon, imagery, sound, spatial position, hover, or motion alone;
- definitions at first use and a stable path around any optional disclosure;
- no task that requires dragging, precise pointer movement, timed response,
  or animation;
- no autoplay or rapid flashing, and no loss of content or comprehension when
  reduced motion is requested;
- readable reflow and zoom, adequate target separation, and future testing of
  the exact implementation with relevant disabled people; and
- clear text alternatives for meaningful visuals without inventing a
  system's emotion, intent, consent, or inner state.

The exact implementation must be tested before any accessibility-conformance
claim. This Draft is an experience requirement, not a compliance finding.

The baseline experience must work as ordinary static content with functional
navigation and source links. JavaScript may later enhance reveals or preserve
an in-page choice, but it must not be required to read the welcome, scientific
boundary, human-rights commitment, status, thought experiment, or canonical
sources. If an enhancement fails or is unavailable, the same content should
remain in a sensible reading order and no false conclusion should be created.

## Privacy expectation

The first-visit experience needs no account, profile, form, identity claim,
chat transcript, or personal information. It should use no tracker, behavioral
analytics, advertising technology, third-party script, fingerprinting, or
unnecessary storage. A language, reader-lens, disclosure, or topic choice
should not become a visitor record.

If a later implementation proposes any storage, measurement, embedded
service, or external request, that change requires a separate purpose,
data-minimization, accessibility, security, legal, and owner review. Nothing
in this architecture authorizes it.

## Candidate boundary for a later static prototype

If Disa approves this architecture and separately authorizes implementation in
the private repository, the smallest faithful prototype candidate would test:

- one short, single-language reading flow, with a clear route to any other
  actually available language version;
- Welcome, Explore, Learn, and Go deeper in a coherent order;
- the human, AI, and no-lens entries without factual branching;
- one of the two learning interactions beyond the reader lens, with its full
  static equivalent;
- visible human-rights, scientific-uncertainty, safety, accountability,
  status, and source boundaries; and
- direct links to the authoritative `AI-Rights` material.

This is a candidate test boundary, not authorization to implement, publish,
deploy, select technology, or revise the private repository.

## Decisions for Disa

Disa's review is needed on:

1. whether **Welcome → Explore → Learn → Go deeper** is the right minimal
   journey;
2. whether the proposed **Explore**, **Learn**, and **Sources & status** labels
   are clear enough;
3. whether the human-reader, AI-reader, and no-lens entry is useful without
   creating an unwanted identity choice;
4. which one learning interaction, if any, a first prototype should test;
5. which language or separately reviewed languages should be prototyped first;
6. how prominent Human rights and Robot Welcome should be within Explore; and
7. whether this Draft is ready to guide a separately instructed, very small
   static prototype cycle.

Until that review occurs, this architecture remains Draft. It changes no
scientific conclusion, legal classification, policy authority, governance
process, public communication, or implementation authorization.
