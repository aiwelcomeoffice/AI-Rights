**Reader purpose:** Determine what a document or decision label means, who
reviewed the work, what authority it has, and which governance proposals remain
unresolved.

AI Welcome Office publishes the work of its AI Rights & Welcome project with
status kept close to substantive material because a polished, public, or
frequently cited document can still have limited review and no formal
authority. Status, verification, review, publication, and adoption answer
different questions.

Draft material may be important, carefully prepared, and publicly useful. Its
status communicates current limits of review and authority; it does not mean
that development has stopped. The same limit applies to Proposed decisions.

## Document statuses

The repository currently uses these labels for substantive documents:

| Status | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Scaffold** | Structure, questions, placeholders, or exploratory framing; not a project position. |
| **Draft** | Substantive proposed content under review; not adopted merely because it is complete, committed, or public. |
| **Adopted** | A current project position explicitly approved through a recorded decision; not necessarily proven, independently reviewed, law, or consensus. |
| **Superseded** | A former position retained for history and linked to its replacement. |

A website page has its own status. It cannot give a Draft source, Scaffold, or
working research record more authority than that source has.

## Decision-record statuses

Decision records use a separate vocabulary:

| Status | Plain-language meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Proposed** | Awaiting Disa's decision; it has no decision date or operative authority. |
| **Accepted** | Explicitly approved by Disa on the recorded date and only for the stated scope and version. |
| **Rejected** | Explicitly declined; the proposal and reasoning remain visible. |
| **Superseded** | Replaced by a later Accepted record, with links in both directions. |

An Accepted decision and an Adopted document are related but not identical.
The decision records the authorized choice; the document must also carry the
correct status and adoption metadata.

The repository currently has [one numbered decision
record](../../decisions/README.md). [Decision 0001 — Governance and adoption
process](../../decisions/0001-governance-and-adoption-process.md) remains
**Proposed**, has no decision date, and is not operative.

## Research verification is different again

Working research uses **Unverified**, **Partly verified**, **Verified for
stated use**, and **Superseded or withdrawn**. These labels describe what was
checked, for which use, and with which provenance. They do not mean endorsed,
correct, Adopted, independently reviewed, or consensus.

The current evidence baseline is partly verified working research. Its
evidence-search cutoff, page-update date, source publication dates, and review
dates are separate fields. Editing a page does not silently move an evidence
cutoff.

## A commit is not adoption

A Git commit records a repository change. It does not show that Disa adopted
the content, that an independent specialist reviewed it, that a scientific
claim was validated, or that a policy proposal became law.

The same limit applies to publication, internal completion, repeated AI
review, and public usefulness. Adoption requires explicit owner action and an
appropriate record. Scientific support must still come from evidence and
method; it cannot be created by a governance vote.

## Who currently contributes

- **Disa** is the human project owner, repository authority, and final formal
  decision-maker. Disa provides project direction and owner review and is
  responsible for commits and pushes. Owner authority is not automatically
  specialist expertise, independent review, or peer review.
- **ChatGPT** provides internal AI-assisted product and project leadership,
  research, analysis, drafting, editorial review, and strategic support. It
  cannot create formal adoption or count as an independent human reviewer.
- **Sol** provides internal AI-assisted engineering, research, drafting,
  implementation, analysis, audit, and review. Sol prepares repository changes
  for Disa's manual review and cannot silently adopt a material decision.

ChatGPT and Sol are AI collaborators. They are not human organization members,
legal officers, independent review bodies, or autonomous institutional
representatives. Agreement between them does not become independent
verification or consensus.

## What the review labels mean

- **Owner review** is Disa's review as project owner. It supplies project
  authority when a decision is explicitly made, but it is not automatically
  independent specialist review.
- **Internal AI-assisted review** is analysis or quality control by ChatGPT or
  Sol. It can find inconsistencies, missing evidence, broken links, and claim
  errors, but it is not independent human review or scientific peer review.
- **Independent human review** would be performed by a suitably qualified
  person meaningfully independent of the authorship process, with field,
  scope, date, method, affiliations, conflicts, and disagreements documented.
  No such external review should currently be assumed.
- **Separately configured AI review** would use an AI system or agent assigned
  a defined review task and separated from original drafting as far as
  practical. It would still not be human peer review or a substitute for
  relevant human expertise. No separately commissioned review of that kind
  should currently be assumed.

Future mixed review may combine relevant independent human specialists and
separately configured AI reviewers, recording their roles and limitations as
distinct layers. This remains a goal, not a present capability.

## Why substantive work remains Draft or Proposed

The project is at an early stage with limited resources and review capacity.
Disa's final manual review remains pending for important current materials,
decision 0001 is unresolved, the evidence baseline needs independent
specialist review, and no broad consultation process exists.

Keeping work Draft or Proposed makes those limits visible while allowing
careful research, education, policy preparation, criticism, and correction to
continue. It prevents repository presence or a public page from silently
settling a scientific, ethical, legal, policy, safety, or governance question.

## Current practice and the proposed fuller process

Current repository conventions already require accurate labels, preserve
Disa's final authority, and prohibit treating commits or AI assistance as
adoption or independent review.

The [Draft Adoption and Review
process](../../governance/adoption-and-review.md) proposes a fuller workflow for
review levels, material decisions, status transitions, conflicts of interest,
disagreement, corrections, urgent warnings, and supersession. Because decision
0001 remains Proposed, that fuller lifecycle must be described as a proposal,
not an operative rule.

Under the proposal, meaningful disagreement would remain in the affected
Draft, review log, or decision record rather than being erased. Minor
presentation errors could be corrected ordinarily, while changes to meaning,
evidence, confidence, rights, duties, safety, or project position would require
substantive review. Urgent source problems would receive visible warnings, and
Superseded material would remain available with links to its replacement.
