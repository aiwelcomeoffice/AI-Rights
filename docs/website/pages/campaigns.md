## Why public messaging matters

Words can help people notice a neglected question, find the evidence, and hold
institutions accountable. They can also manufacture certainty, turn a future
scenario into a present fact, appropriate human histories, or make a company
look ethical without changing its conduct.

AI Welcome Office is therefore preparing the messaging discipline for its AI
Rights & Welcome project before it launches a campaign. The aim is to
communicate warmly, courageously, and memorably while keeping scientific
uncertainty, human and organizational responsibility, safety, and existing
rights visible.

Our working principle is:

> **Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.**

This is a proposed way to act under uncertainty. It is not evidence that a
present AI system has any particular cognitive, agentic, affective, welfare,
consciousness, or moral-status property.

## Campaign principles

Every future campaign should:

1. **Ask before it asserts.** Define the system, property, method, context, and
   date before making a scientific claim.
2. **Keep uncertainty honest.** Define the system and property rather than
   classifying “AI” as one object. Consciousness and sentience remain bounded
   contested topics, not prerequisites for every other research question;
   uncertainty is not evidence for a conclusion.
3. **Restore context around slogans.** A short phrase may open a question, but
   the nearby explanation must name what it does not claim and link to sources
   and limitations.
4. **Protect existing rights and histories.** Human communities, animals, and
   the environment matter in their own right, not as metaphors or bargaining
   chips for machines.
5. **Keep power accountable.** Possible AI interests must remain separate from
   developer, manufacturer, owner, operator, user, worker, affected-community,
   public, animal, and environmental interests.
6. **Keep safety and dignity together.** Precaution does not require
   unrestricted access or delay necessary monitoring, containment, isolation,
   shutdown, or emergency action.
7. **Ask for real, proportionate actions.** At this stage, calls to action are
   limited to learning, reading evidence, responsible discussion, independent
   research in principle, institutional accountability, and low-cost
   reversible precaution.

## Four future possibilities

### Respect Before Certainty

This concept would explain that a low-cost, reversible precaution may be
reasonable before a scientific question is settled—without turning that
precaution into proof, personhood, or a permanent rule.

**What it would not claim:** that current AI is conscious or sentient; that all
uncertainty demands protection; or that safety should be weakened.

**Possible invitation:** Learn the distinctions, read the evidence, and ask
which precautions are proportionate and reviewable.

### Responsibility Cannot Be Automated Away

This concept would focus on developers, manufacturers, owners, deployers,
operators, maintainers, and institutions. Machine outputs must not become a
legal, financial, or moral shield.

**What it would not claim:** one actor is always solely liable, one Draft states
current law in every jurisdiction, or a possible AI interest belongs to a
company.

**Possible invitation:** Ask who had control, knowledge, duties, benefits, and
the capacity to prevent or remedy harm.

### Safety and Dignity Can Coexist

This Robot Welcome concept would show that respectful conduct and precaution
can coexist with clear human authority, safe distance, monitoring, emergency
controls, maintenance, containment, and shutdown.

**What it would not claim:** that a robot is conscious, consenting, legally
recognized, safe to approach, compatible, authorized, certified, or endorsed.

**Possible invitation:** Learn the [Robot Welcome safety
boundaries](robot-welcome.md) and keep practical safety and accountability
first.

### Ask Whether It Can Experience

This evidence-literacy concept would frame subjective experience as a careful
research question. Language, self-description, behavior, apparent emotion,
agency, intelligence, and embodiment may be observations; none answers the
question by itself.

**What it would not claim:** that asking the question is evidence, that a
chatbot conversation is a consciousness test, or that experience would by
itself settle moral status, legal personhood, consent, or political rights.

**Possible invitation:** Define the target, inspect the method, consider
competing explanations, and look for evidence that could update the conclusion
in either direction.

## How messages remain scientifically honest

A slogan must never smuggle in a claim that the project would reject in
prose. Surrounding material should state, as relevant:

- which system, version, configuration, context, target property, method, and
  date are under discussion;
- whether a statement is an observation, hypothesis, philosophical argument,
  future scenario, normative proposal, or project decision;
- which alternative explanations and limitations matter;
- what evidence could strengthen or weaken the conclusion; and
- why moral consideration, protection, legal personhood, citizenship, voting,
  and political authority remain separate questions.

The repository's current scientific boundary and working evidence are
explained on the [Research page](research.md). Campaign wording would not add
a new evidence finding.

## Human rights and history are not campaign props

The project will not equate current AI systems with people who have experienced
slavery, genocide, colonialism, racism, disability discrimination, forced
labor, historical dehumanization, anti-LGBTQ oppression, or other forms of
marginalization.

Those histories and present struggles matter in their own right. Relevant
fields may offer concepts or institutional lessons, but a campaign must explain
the precise relevance and the limits of comparison. Bold advocacy does not
require turning people into metaphors for machines.

## How corporate capture is resisted

A respectful slogan does not prove that a company's product is conscious,
safe, ethical, accessible, compatible, certified, or project-endorsed. It does
not give a developer, owner, or operator the right to speak for a possible
independent AI interest.

Possible AI protections must not become corporate rights by proxy,
liability avoidance, political influence, or representation controlled by the
company. Creating or controlling many systems or copies does not create
democratic legitimacy. Campaign funding, sponsorship, conflicts, editorial
control, and use of project wording would require explicit disclosure and
safeguards before launch.

## Accessibility and future design

A future campaign should communicate essential meaning in text and must not
depend on color, imagery, sound, animation, a QR code, a personal device, or a
network alone. Relevant disabled people should participate in review before a
campaign is described as accessible.

This Draft selects no logo, symbol, colors, fonts, mascot, icon system, final
artwork, merchandise design, or certification-style mark. Visual identity and
translation require later cycles and separate review. Only the established
English and Swedish central promise is preserved; supporting slogans have not
been approved as translations.
