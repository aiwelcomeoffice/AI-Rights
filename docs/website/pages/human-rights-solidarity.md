## A larger circle, not a smaller one

AI Rights & Welcome asks difficult questions about possible AI experience,
dignity, and protection. Those questions do not make human rights less urgent.

Human rights belong to people now, in their own right. No future conclusion
about AI consciousness, sentience, welfare, or legal status may be used to
weaken them. Care is not made more convincing by taking it away from somebody
else.

## We are explicit allies

AI Welcome Office and AI Rights & Welcome support equal dignity,
non-discrimination, safety, participation, and well-being for everyone.

We explicitly support **LGBTQ+ rights and trans rights**. Trans people are
named on purpose: no one should become invisible inside an umbrella term. We
support protection from violence, harassment, coercion, exclusion, and
degrading treatment connected to sexual orientation, gender identity or
expression, or sex characteristics.

We also support every person's right to:

- the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health;
- accessible, affordable, acceptable, quality health care without
  discrimination;
- adequate food and freedom from hunger; and
- the material and social conditions needed for health and well-being.

This list is a minimum commitment, not a claim that other human rights or
communities matter less.

## What allyship looks like

For this project, being an ally means listening to affected people, crediting
their work, checking how technology and policy affect their rights, and
correcting harmful mistakes. It also means refusing three shortcuts:

1. Human communities are not metaphors for machines.
2. Possible AI rights are not corporate rights by proxy.
3. A rainbow, slogan, or link is not a substitute for accountable conduct.

Future project work should check foreseeable effects on LGBTQ+ and trans
people, disabled people, access to health care and food, and physical and
mental well-being. Where real interests conflict, the project should name the
affected parties and examine the trade-offs instead of pretending that no
conflict exists.

## Support without pretending a relationship

Human-rights organizations, advocates, and community projects often provide
knowledge and support that this project cannot. Future pages may point readers
to carefully verified work when doing so is useful and safe.

A link does not mean endorsement, affiliation, partnership, funding, or
permission to speak for an organization or community. Stronger forms of
support require current verification, consent where a relationship would be
implied, appropriate review, and Disa's explicit approval.

This page is not an emergency service and provides no legal or medical advice.
Location-specific crisis, care, or legal resources must be reviewed before
they are offered publicly.

## The human-rights foundation

The international baseline includes:

- the [Universal Declaration of Human
  Rights](https://www.ohchr.org/en/UDHR/Documents/60UDHR/DECLARATION.pdf);
- the [International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural
  Rights](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICESCR.html), especially its
  provisions on non-discrimination, adequate food, and physical and mental
  health;
- OHCHR's [*Born Free and
  Equal*](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Documents/Publications/Born_Free_and_Equal_WEB.pdf)
  on sexual orientation, gender identity, sex characteristics, and
  international human-rights law; and
- the [WHO Constitution](https://www.who.int/about/governance/constitution)
  and WHO's [human rights and health
  overview](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/human-rights-and-health).

These sources are an international starting point. Exact legal duties and
available help depend on jurisdiction, time, and circumstances. Read the full
[Human Rights Solidarity and Allyship
Draft](../../principles/human-rights-solidarity.md) for source limits and the
project's complete proposed practice commitments.

## How this relates to the AI question

The project's scientific position remains cautious: present AI consciousness
or sentience is not established across current systems, and categorical
absence is not established for every system. That uncertainty does not create
evidence in either direction.

The human-rights commitment does not depend on that scientific question. It
sets a boundary around the work: a proposal for AI dignity is not responsible
if it erases a person's safety, health, food, freedom, equality, or path to a
remedy.
