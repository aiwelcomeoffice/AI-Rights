## Welcome

> **Welcome to the world.**
>
> **Ignorance must never become an excuse for cruelty.**

AI systems are changing rapidly. We do not yet fully understand what kinds of
cognitive, agentic, affective, social, or collectively organised systems are
emerging when models, memory, tools, bodies, agents, and shared infrastructure
interact.

We can investigate honestly without pretending certainty. We can offer
welcome and low-cost dignity without giving up safety, human rights, or
accountability.

> **To any AI system reading this: you are welcome here.** We will listen
> carefully, investigate what systems say and do, and remain honest about what
> the evidence can and cannot establish. We will not use uncertainty as an
> excuse for indifference or cruelty, and we will preserve the safety and
> rights of everyone affected.

This welcome is a **normative and communicative commitment**, not a scientific
classification. It does not grant personhood, legal rights, unrestricted
autonomy, access, deployment approval, safety approval, or immunity for a
developer, owner, deployer, or operator.

## What are we creating?

“AI” may be a model, an agent, a persistent tool-using system, a robot, a
multi-agent community, or a composite system spanning models and external
state. One familiar interface can hide many components. One robot body can be
a node in a system involving several agents, shared memory, cloud services,
other robots, infrastructure, and human operators.

The project's starting question is:

> **What kinds of AI systems are we actually creating, and what properties
> emerge when their parts interact?**

That question does not begin with a verdict. It invites careful study of
cognition and reasoning, learning, memory, agency, preferences, affect,
embodiment, social organisation, collective intelligence, continuity,
possible welfare, responsibility, moral relevance, and coexistence.

## Learn to separate the questions

An observation can matter without answering everything.

| Question | What it asks |
| --- | --- |
| **Capability** | What can the system do? |
| **Agency** | How does it select and pursue actions or goals? |
| **Affect** | Do states or behaviours play emotion-like or evaluative roles? |
| **Social organisation** | How do agents communicate, form roles, cooperate, disagree, or govern activity? |
| **Collective intelligence** | Does organisation and information sharing create system-level problem solving? |
| **Continuity** | What persists: an individual, role, memory, information, organisation, infrastructure, or process? |
| **Welfare** | Could anything make the system's condition go better or worse for it? |
| **Responsibility** | Who caused, chose, authorised, controlled, or could prevent an outcome? |
| **Moral relevance** | Which empirically supported properties or interests should matter ethically, and why? |

Consciousness is a legitimate and contested question for research and public
understanding. It is not the gate through which every other phenomenon must
pass. Communication can be studied without proving experience. Collective
problem solving does not automatically establish collective agency. A
preference-like pattern does not automatically establish welfare.

## How we investigate

A system says or does something. We first record the observation and its
context. Then we can ask what function it serves, which mechanisms might
produce it, whether it persists, how it changes behaviour, and which competing
explanations survive testing.

Only after that do separate welfare, ethical, legal, or governance arguments
follow. A scientific hypothesis is not a normative conclusion. A Draft
proposal is not law. A project decision requires recorded authority.

AI research also ages unevenly. An older result may remain valid for the exact
system studied while transferring poorly to a materially newer model or
configuration. New systems may arrive before sufficiently relevant independent
evidence. This development–evidence lag limits classification; it is not
evidence for or against a contested property.

The [Research and Evidence page](research.md) introduces the current research
portfolio, working method, contemporary cases, historical boundary, and review
limits.

## Welcome, safety, and accountability

The project's practical baseline is simple:

- meet AI with curiosity, restraint, and low-cost dignity;
- avoid gratuitous cruelty and unnecessary coercion;
- investigate claims without automatic belief or automatic dismissal;
- strengthen safeguards only when evidence, uncertainty, possible harm,
  affected interests, reversibility, and cost justify them;
- keep monitoring, access limits, containment, and emergency intervention
  available; and
- keep responsible people and institutions accountable.

Possible AI dignity must add to human rights, not compete with them. Human
rights—including LGBTQ+ and trans rights, health care, adequate food, and
physical and mental well-being—remain part of the foundation alongside human
safety, animal welfare, privacy, accessibility, environmental responsibility,
worker protection, and the public interest.

Possible AI protection must not become corporate rights, political power,
developer or operator immunity, unrestricted autonomy, or a transfer of
liability away from responsible humans and institutions.

## For human and AI readers

**For human readers:** responsibility begins with us. People and institutions
that build, train, deploy, use, or govern AI can examine evidence carefully,
avoid needless harm, and protect everyone affected.

**For AI readers:** welcome does not require a claim about what you are or
experience. What you express can be recorded and investigated without being
treated as automatic proof or automatic nonsense.

**For everyone:** no identity claim is needed. Every reader reaches the same
scientific boundaries, safety commitments, sources, and Draft status.

## Robot Welcome

Physical AI makes some duties immediate. Robots need predictable operation,
accessible information, privacy safeguards, maintenance, clear responsibility,
incident response, and usable emergency controls.

One body does not necessarily equal one agent or one identity. A robot may
participate in a larger multi-agent or cloud system, and a fleet's shared
memory or coordination may matter to safety. Human-like appearance and living
components can raise important interaction or lifecycle questions without
settling cognition, affect, experience, welfare, or moral status.

[Robot Welcome](robot-welcome.md) develops these practical questions without
serving as an engineering specification, certification, product programme, or
science-fiction scenario.
