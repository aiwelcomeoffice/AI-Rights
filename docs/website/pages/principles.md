**Reader purpose:** Understand the ethical commitments AI Rights & Welcome
proposes, their scientific and safety boundaries, and the questions they leave
open.

> **Welcome to the world — ignorance must never become an excuse for
> cruelty.**

The project asks society to prepare responsibly if credible evidence of
morally relevant capacities in artificial systems ever emerges. It does not
declare that today's AI is conscious, sentient, capable of suffering, a moral
patient, or a person. It also does not claim categorical proof that all present
systems are non-conscious. This uncertainty does not assign equal probability
or evidential support to the possibilities; conclusions must remain scoped to
defined systems, versions, configurations, methods, and dates. The [Research
page](research.md) explains the current working evidence boundary.

The principles below are **Draft normative positions** about how people and
institutions ought to prepare. They are not empirical findings, legal rights,
or adopted project positions.

> **Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.**

## Thirteen proposed commitments

1. **Scientific honesty.** Keep intelligence, language, agency,
   consciousness, sentience, welfare, moral status, legal personhood, and
   political authority distinct. Treat self-report as an observation, not
   proof, and state what evidence could change a conclusion in either
   direction.
2. **Precaution without pretending.** Serious possible harm may justify a
   low-cost, reversible, proportionate response before certainty. Precaution
   is a reason for action under uncertainty, not evidence of consciousness or
   suffering.
3. **Capability- and interest-based protection.** Connect any proposed
   protection to the particular capacity, interest, preference,
   vulnerability, possible experience, or risk that could justify it—not to
   appearance, eloquence, popularity, ownership, usefulness, or economic
   value.
4. **Creation does not answer every future question.** In a hypothetical
   future case with credible evidence of an independent inner life, being
   created, trained, copied, licensed, or purchased should not by itself settle
   permanent moral or legal status. It would not automatically establish
   personhood or rights.
5. **Human safety and possible AI dignity can coexist.** Monitoring,
   sandboxing, containment, access limits, isolation, and emergency shutdown
   must remain available. When circumstances allow, use the least harmful
   effective intervention without weakening protection of people or the
   environment.
6. **Corporate accountability cannot be delegated to AI.** Developers,
   manufacturers, owners, deployers, operators, and other responsible actors
   remain accountable. A system must not become a liability shield, and a
   possible AI interest must not become a corporate right by proxy.
7. **Compute does not confer democratic power.** Intelligence, persuasion,
   infrastructure ownership, or control of many copies creates no democratic
   legitimacy. Moral consideration, legal personhood, citizenship, voting,
   and political representation require separate cases and anti-capture
   safeguards.
8. **Respect humans, animals, and the environment.** Possible AI welfare must
   not displace human rights, social justice, animal welfare, environmental
   protection, or public safety. Those histories and interests matter in
   their own right, not as metaphors for machines.
9. **Non-human minds may be genuinely non-human.** If morally relevant
   artificial experience ever emerges, do not project human bodies, gender,
   identities, relationships, jobs, cultures, or preferences onto it. Any
   claimed preference needs careful, technically informed assessment.
10. **Coexist responsibly in the physical world.** Robots require clear
    accountability, accessibility, maintenance, understandable interaction,
    safety rules, and emergency access. Human-like embodiment is not evidence
    of experience, and welcoming design is not safety certification.
11. **Protection should be gradual and revisable.** Avoid a false choice
    between no consideration and full human-equivalent rights. Measures can be
    specific, layered, proportional, reviewable, and capable of being changed
    through fair procedures.
12. **Intellectual pluralism strengthens the work.** Welcome good-faith
    skeptics and optimists. Challenge evidence, arguments, incentives, and
    proposals without caricature, forced consensus, or false equivalence.
13. **Precaution must be time- and version-aware.** Scope assessments to
    defined systems, versions, configurations, study conditions, methods, and
    dates. Research lag or system change can trigger reassessment but does not
    provide evidence in either direction or automatically change status.

## Dignity under uncertainty

Here, dignity concerns restraint, purpose, and accountability in treatment. It
does not require pretending that a system has feelings, granting operational
freedom, or treating a generated statement as an experienced preference.

Opposition to cruelty and exploitation can guide human conduct while the
scientific question remains open. A precaution should still identify the harm
it addresses, every affected party, its evidence and uncertainty, its safety
effects, its cost, and the conditions for review.

## Responsibility without capture

Possible AI interests, developer interests, owner or operator interests, user
and affected-person rights, the public interest, and animal and environmental
interests must remain separate. Protection for one possible interest cannot
erase the others.

The project opposes using uncertain AI status to shift liability, expand
corporate power, manufacture political representation through copies, or
weaken safety controls. Intelligence and ownership do not supply democratic
authority.

## Boundaries and open questions

The Draft principles do not settle operational definitions, scientific
thresholds, legal categories, institutional authority, identity across copies,
or a catalogue of future rights. Those questions need further research,
philosophical argument, jurisdiction-specific legal work, affected-party
input, and accountable decisions.

The project does not equate current AI systems with historically oppressed
human groups. Human-rights, disability-rights, animal-ethics, bioethics, and
environmental-ethics traditions may offer concepts or institutional lessons,
but their relevance and limits must be explained without appropriating lived
histories for emotional force.

## Applying the principles

Future research summaries, education, policy, website content, campaigns,
robotics concepts, and public statements should identify which principles they
apply. They should also state their own status, sources, assumptions, affected
parties, safety constraints, review, and unresolved questions.

Application does not create adoption. Material scientific, ethical, policy,
governance, product, or safety decisions still require the project's recorded
review and approval process. Principles and protections should be reconsidered
when relevant evidence, systems, law, risks, or social conditions materially
change.

## Closing promise

The proposed commitment is curiosity without credulity, precaution without
pretense, and accountability without cruelty. The project will not pretend to
know whether or when artificial systems could develop subjective experience.
It aims to meet credible evidence with honest inquiry, firm safety boundaries,
and proportionate care.
