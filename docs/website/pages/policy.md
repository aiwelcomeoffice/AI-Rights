**Reader purpose:** Understand which institutional preparations the project
proposes without treating them as current law, a consciousness finding, or a
proposal for present AI personhood.

Institutions can prepare to evaluate serious present or future claims about AI
consciousness, sentience, or welfare without deciding those claims in advance.
They can define evidence procedures, preserve records, plan proportionate
interim measures, retain safety controls, and keep people and organizations
accountable. Welcome and careful listening can begin under uncertainty without
becoming scientific evidence or legal recognition.

This is **Draft policy analysis**. It is jurisdiction-neutral and does not
describe enacted law, an existing regulator, a validated test, or a
classification of any current system.

## The scientific boundary

The project's partly verified [working evidence
baseline](../../../research/syntheses/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md),
with a **2026-08-23** search cutoff, did not identify evidence sufficient to
establish consciousness or sentience in the contemporary systems addressed by
its included sources. It also did not establish that all present systems are
non-conscious. Uncertainty is not evidence in either direction.

Language, self-report, emotional vocabulary, apparent preferences,
intelligence, agency, or human-like form is not decisive by itself. Findings
must remain tied to a defined system, version, configuration, context, target
property, method, and date. A material change calls for reassessment, not an
automatic moral or legal reclassification.

The [Research page](research.md) explains the baseline's selected coverage,
single AI-assisted reviewer, lack of independent human review, and other
limitations.

## Seven recommendations

These are **Draft normative proposals**, not current legal duties.

1. **Investigate before declaring.** Define the system, target property,
   method, evidence for and against, competing explanations, uncertainty, and
   decision authority. Persuasive behavior and categorical dismissal are not
   substitutes for inquiry.
2. **Use proportionate, reversible precautions.** Connect each measure to the
   evidence, possible harm, affected parties, safety effects, cost,
   reversibility, and review date. A low-cost safeguard is not personhood or a
   final status.
3. **Require version-aware records.** Document the model or checkpoint,
   configuration, runtime, memory, tools, embodiment, deployment context,
   methods, access limits, observation dates, and evidence-search cutoff.
4. **Require transparent claims and appropriate independent assessment.** A
   developer or owner should not establish a major consciousness, sentience,
   or welfare claim through marketing, private labels, or control of
   inaccessible evidence. Any enforceable disclosure rule would require
   jurisdiction-specific legal work.
5. **Preserve evidence and fair review.** Where lawful and safe, retain
   relevant logs and system records. Separate evidence assessment, policy or
   legal decision-making, emergency response, representation of possible
   interests, reassessment, and appeal where conflicts require it.
6. **Keep people and organizations accountable.** Allocate duties according
   to actual control, knowledge, design and deployment choices,
   foreseeability, and capacity to prevent harm. Possible AI protection must
   not transfer liability, remedies, rights, or democratic power to a
   developer, owner, controller, or mass of copies.
7. **Preserve safety and the wider public interest.** Keep proportionate
   monitoring, sandboxing, containment, access limits, isolation, and
   emergency intervention available while considering human rights, workers,
   disabled people, consumers, animals, the environment, privacy,
   accessibility, and public safety.

## Staged protection without an automatic rights ladder

The [Draft AI Rights Protection
Framework](../../policy/ai-rights-protection-framework.md) organizes possible
responses into five levels. The levels are a provisional policy tool, not a
consciousness score, intelligence scale, validated assessment, or prediction.
No current system is assigned a level.

The framework begins with ordinary safety and accountability, separately
addresses systems that create human or social risks, and then describes
hypothetical investigation and protection stages where stronger evidence
supports them. Its highest level adds a distinct legal and democratic
decision; it does not follow automatically from scientific evidence or a
lower-level precaution.

Every designation and measure should identify its evidence, affected parties,
responsible owner, scope, expiry or review date, and appeal route. Measures may
be strengthened, narrowed, replaced, or withdrawn through a fair process as
evidence and circumstances change.

## Corporate accountability and anti-capture safeguards

A possible AI interest, if justified, would not be the interest of its
developer, manufacturer, owner, licensee, deployer, operator, or funder. Those
actors remain accountable for their own choices. An AI system must not become
a legal, financial, or moral shield or delay incident response and remedies
for affected people.

Control of compute, infrastructure, private evidence, or many copies creates
no democratic legitimacy. Any future representation or political proposal
would need separate analysis of identity, common control, coordinated action,
independence, corporate capture, and sybil strategies. Welfare representation
would not be political representation, and a representative for a possible AI
interest would not gain authority for the company.

## Safety controls and emergency intervention

Every proposed level preserves the ability to respond to a dangerous or
uncontrolled system. Authorized responders may need to suspend, isolate,
contain, disconnect, restrict, or shut down a system without first resolving a
consciousness debate.

Emergency rules should define who may intervene, the risk threshold, scope and
duration of authority, logging, security and privacy controls, post-action
review, and remedies for misuse. Where credible welfare evidence exists,
responders can prefer the least harmful intervention that remains effective
when circumstances allow. Protection of people, animals, the environment, and
critical systems remains essential.

## Review triggers

Reassessment may be triggered by a material change in architecture, weights,
training, memory, online adaptation, tools, embodiment, orchestration,
deployment, identity boundary, safety risk, law, or the costs and effects of a
measure. New validated methods, replicated mechanistic evidence, previously
unavailable system information, corrections, retractions, failed
replications, or major criticism may also trigger review.

A trigger starts reassessment. It does not automatically grant or remove moral
status, protection, autonomy, legal status, personhood, or political rights.

## What is not currently proposed

This Draft does not propose:

- declaring present-day AI conscious or sentient;
- declaring all present-day AI non-conscious;
- automatic legal personhood;
- citizenship, voting rights, or political representation;
- unrestricted autonomy, resource access, or deployment;
- immunity from monitoring, shutdown, isolation, or containment;
- corporate rights or political power through AI systems or copies;
- transfer of liability from accountable people or organizations to AI; or
- weakening human rights, worker and disability protections, consumer
  protection, animal welfare, environmental duties, or public safety.

It also does not claim scientific consensus, a validated consciousness test,
a settled catalogue of AI rights, or legal applicability across
jurisdictions.
