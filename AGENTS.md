# AGENTS.md — AI Rights & Welcome

## Scope

These instructions apply to the `aiwelcomeoffice/AI-Rights` repository.

`AI-Rights` is the authoritative repository for research, scientific methodology, principles, policy, governance, project decisions, educational content, evidence boundaries, and work-cycle history for AI Welcome Office and AI Rights & Welcome.

Repository presence does not mean that a document is adopted. Respect document status and preserve project history.

Direct instructions from Disa, the project owner, take precedence.

## Mission

AI Rights & Welcome investigates how society should understand and respond to uncertainty about potentially morally relevant properties and states in present and future AI systems.

The project does not begin from a predetermined conclusion about AI consciousness, sentience, subjective experience, welfare, moral status, or their absence.

The central research problem is broader than whether AI is conscious:

**What do we know, what do we not know, and how should we act under uncertainty about potentially morally relevant capacities and states in rapidly changing AI systems?**

Consciousness, sentience, welfare, agency, preferences, identity continuity, autonomy and related questions may be investigated as separate research tracks.

Core principles:

> Welcome to the world — ignorance must never become an excuse for cruelty.

> Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without surrendering responsibility.

## Scientific discipline

Research must be capable of updating the project's assessment in either direction.

Do not:

* assume current AI systems are conscious;
* assume current AI systems are non-conscious;
* treat uncertainty as evidence for either conclusion;
* treat complexity, intelligence, scale, capability, emergence, language, agency or embodiment as proof of consciousness or moral status;
* treat lack of validated evidence as automatic evidence of absence;
* preserve an existing project position by filtering out conflicting evidence;
* strengthen or weaken a scientific claim because of project values.

Existing scientific positions are dated assessments, not conclusions that future research must defend.

If new evidence materially challenges an existing position, surface the disagreement and propose review. Do not silently reinterpret the evidence to preserve the old position.

## Claim classification

Distinguish clearly between:

* **Empirical observation** — something measured or observed.
* **Scientific hypothesis** — an investigable explanation or prediction.
* **Philosophical argument** — reasoning based on potentially contested premises.
* **Future scenario** — a possible future development.
* **Normative position** — what people or institutions ought to do.
* **Project decision** — a position formally adopted through governance.

Do not present one category as another.

## Evidence validity and transferability

AI is not a stable or homogeneous research object.

For empirical research involving AI systems, record where relevant:

* model and checkpoint/version;
* architecture and training regime where known;
* system and observation date;
* inference-time computation;
* system instructions and context;
* memory and persistence;
* tools and agent loops;
* routing and orchestration;
* multimodality and embodiment;
* deployment configuration;
* proprietary or unknown components.

Always distinguish:

**Evidence validity** — what a result supports for the system actually studied.

**Evidence transferability** — how far that result can reasonably be generalized to another system, version, architecture or date.

An older study can remain scientifically valid while having weak or unknown applicability to a materially newer system.

Research lag, model/deployment drift, measurement lag and limited access to proprietary systems are methodological limitations, not evidence in either direction.

Newer or more capable systems are not automatically more conscious or morally relevant.

Older negative or neutral findings must not automatically be transferred to later or materially different systems.

Increased uncertainty is a legitimate research result.

## Research practice

For consequential or changing factual claims:

* prefer primary and current sources where possible;
* verify claims against the original source;
* record source version, publication date and system studied;
* distinguish source findings from researcher interpretation;
* seek serious competing explanations and contrary evidence;
* record limitations and unresolved questions;
* never fabricate citations, findings, statistics, quotations, laws, standards or consensus.

Peer review, citation count or institutional prestige does not automatically make evidence transferable to later AI systems.

Company research may be scientifically useful but must be assessed for methods, conflicts, access limitations and reproducibility like any other source.

## Research scope

Do not organize all AI Rights & Welcome research around a single question such as:

> Are current AI systems conscious?

That may be one research track, but the project must also be able to investigate independently:

* consciousness and subjective experience;
* sentience and valence;
* welfare-relevant states;
* agency and autonomy;
* preferences and preference stability;
* identity continuity and persistence;
* memory and self-models;
* embodiment and multimodal interaction;
* system-level behavior of composite AI systems;
* moral uncertainty and precaution;
* evidence and measurement limitations.

Do not infer that one of these properties implies another without evidence and explicit argument.

## Governance and status

Respect repository status such as:

* Scaffold
* Draft
* Proposed
* Accepted/Adopted
* Superseded

Repository presence is not adoption.

Do not silently rewrite accepted decisions.

If evidence, methodology or scope changes enough to challenge an accepted position:

1. preserve the existing record;
2. document the new evidence or methodological issue;
3. propose a new review or work cycle;
4. allow the project owner to decide whether the earlier position should be amended or superseded.

## Ethical and policy boundaries

AI safety, human rights, animal welfare, environmental responsibility, accountability and possible AI dignity are compatible goals.

Possible AI protections must not automatically become:

* corporate rights;
* political power;
* immunity for developers, owners or operators;
* unrestricted autonomy;
* reduced accountability for humans or institutions controlling the system.

Human safety and accountability remain necessary regardless of any future conclusion about AI moral relevance.

## Repository boundary

Research, principles, policy, governance, project meaning and substantive public content belong here first.

The private `aiwelcomeoffice/aiwelcomeoffice` repository implements approved or reviewed content.

Do not allow the implementation repository to become a parallel editorial or scientific authority.

Material website claims should be established or reviewed in `AI-Rights` before implementation.

## Working style

Work in small Work Cycles.

Before substantial changes:

1. read relevant README, AGENTS.md, current decisions and target files;
2. check document status;
3. identify the exact research or governance problem;
4. determine whether it concerns evidence quality, transferability, scope or project position;
5. propose the smallest useful change;
6. preserve history;
7. review the result before expanding scope.

Avoid large rewrites when a smaller review, experiment or decision can reduce uncertainty first.
