## Source report and appraisal

In the opening and §1, the group objects to frontier labs testing advanced mathematical problems on proprietary inaccessible models while recommending prompt, responsible release of significant results when produced. It centers human understanding and community-led follow-up. In §2 it distinguishes papers understood by a responsible mathematician from output not yet human-understood. It recommends clear citations and exposition, a repository outside lab control with stable identifiers/versioning, disclosure of model, prompts, summarized reasoning, time/cost and failed attempts, formalization where possible, and funding for independent mathematical understanding. §3 calls for broad, equitable model access and warns of unequal power within the field.

The statement is an identifiable independent **normative** perspective, not an independent correctness audit or endorsement of OpenAI's implementation. OpenAI cites consultation with the group, but the group's own positions must be read separately. Its survey claim is self-reported, and “clear plurality” does not establish consensus.

**Evidence-quality profile for the claim that AGMAI made these recommendations:** direct relevance, strong documentary support for the group's position, descriptive review of its text, no replication requirement. **For claims about the prevalence of mathematicians' views or real-world effects:** methodological quality and representativeness not assessed, so evidential support is limited. Funding, affiliation and individual conflicts beyond the group's stated advisory role were not checked.

**Supports:** The existence and content of a serious community-facing critique and release recommendations. **Does not support:** Mathematical validity or invalidity of any OpenAI manuscript, representative consensus, or an AI Welcome Office policy decision.
