## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The consortium preregistered contrasting predictions of GNWT and IIT and
tested them using complementary human-neuroscience methods.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Preregistered adversarial collaboration across
  multiple theory-neutral experimental teams and sites, with theory proponents
  participating in the collaboration
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** 256 participants recruited
  across arms (fMRI 120, MEG 102, iEEG 34); after exclusions, fMRI 108, MEG 97,
  and iEEG 32 were included, with analysis-specific sample sizes sometimes
  smaller (including iEEG n=29 for one analysis).
- **System boundary and version:** Human neural activity, not AI.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Spatial, temporal, and connectivity
  predictions assigned in advance to GNWT or IIT.
- **Comparators and controls:** Conscious stimulus conditions, duration, task,
  and theory-contrasting predictions.
- **Analysis:** Preregistered confirmatory tests plus reported exploratory work.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Publisher provides reporting,
  data/code availability and preregistration links; exact repository links
  TODO: extract.

### Main findings or conclusions

The study reports some results compatible with each theory and prominent
challenges to both. In particular, predicted sustained posterior synchronization
for IIT and an expected stimulus-offset ignition result for GNWT were not
observed as predicted. These are empirical results about human neural
predictions, not a direct test of experience or AI.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The central value of the study is mixed and disconfirming evidence: several
preregistered predictions were not supported, while other observations did not
uniquely distinguish the theories.

A later GNWT-proponent response by Naccache et al. argues that several findings
supported GNWT, that the clearly visible-stimulus design did not test its
central conscious-versus-non-conscious contrast, and that offset ignition was
not an unconditional core prediction in the tested conditions. This is a
published counterinterpretation of the same evidence, not a new finding of the
Cogitate article; see the related source record.

### Source-reported limitations

The tasks, recording modalities, spatial/temporal resolution, and auxiliary
assumptions limit how strongly any failed prediction bears on a whole theory.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

Adversarial tests can still depend on negotiated operationalizations. Human
neural findings cannot be transferred to artificial substrates without an
additional theory and validation argument.

### Competing explanations

A null prediction may reflect the wrong task, measure, time window, or theory
variant rather than the absence of the underlying mechanism; theory revision
can also accommodate results. Naccache et al. make this alternative concrete
for GNWT by contesting offset-awareness assumptions and the sensitivity of
prefrontal decoding. Because the response is retrospective, introduces no new
data, and includes a Cogitate coauthor and GNWT proponents, it must be retained
as serious disagreement without overriding the preregistered result.

### Evidence-profile summary

This is comparatively strong evidence that theory appraisal remains unsettled.
It does not refute either theory wholesale or directly weigh any AI system.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

It prevents indicator frameworks from treating their source theories as
settled and demonstrates the value of adversarial, preregistered tests.

### Claims this source supports

- Empirical observation: several preregistered predictions of both GNWT and
  IIT were challenged in the tested human paradigms.
- Empirical observation about the literature: a technically specific,
  theory-proponent counterinterpretation disputes whether some GNWT challenges
  tested central or properly conditioned predictions; it supplies no new data.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against

- Scientific hypothesis that current versions of either theory are already
  decisively confirmed enough to serve as an uncontested AI classifier.

### Claims this source does not support

- Consciousness or non-consciousness of any AI system.
