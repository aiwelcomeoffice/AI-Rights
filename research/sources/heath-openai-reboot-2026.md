## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

Heath reports on OpenAI's strategy, internal capability demonstrations,
response to the Hugging Face incident, safety slowdown, and leadership views
about AGI after interviews and on-site observation.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Journalistic reporting and attributed interview
  statements.
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** More than 20 OpenAI leaders,
  employees, investors, customers, and competitors; events observed during two
  weeks at OpenAI headquarters.
- **System boundary and version:** Mostly proprietary internal OpenAI systems,
  including an upcoming Astra family; independent technical access was not
  reported.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** OpenAI's charter definition of AGI
  is cited, but no quantitative “distance to AGI” measure is described.
- **Comparators and controls:** None suitable for scientific validation.
- **Analysis:** Reporting, interview synthesis, and organizational context.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Article only; interview notes,
  demonstrations, models, and underlying performance data are not public.

### Main findings or conclusions

TIME reports that OpenAI chief research officer Mark Chen estimated the
company was “80% of the way” to AGI. It also reports that Sam Altman said
OpenAI was not yet there but predicted an internal system he would call AGI by
the end of 2026, and that Greg Brockman offered a retrospective scenario in
which the present period might later be seen as AGI's creation (paragraphs
headed around the AGI discussion).

The article separately reports internal demonstrations and a claimed internal
“AI research intern” benchmark, but supplies no independent evaluation data.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The article says OpenAI leaders did not claim AGI had already been reached.
It describes safety failures, proprietary evidence, organizational incentives,
and reasons to be skeptical of OpenAI's attempted safety repositioning.

### Source-reported limitations

The underlying systems and performance evidence are not available for
independent inspection. The article discloses that TIME has a licensing and
technology agreement with OpenAI and that TIME owner Marc Benioff leads
Salesforce, an Anthropic investor.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

“80%” lacks a disclosed scale, baseline, aggregation rule, benchmark set,
uncertainty interval, or validation procedure. AGI definitions are contested,
and an executive estimate may reflect forecasting, strategy, advocacy,
fundraising, competition, or genuine private evidence in unknown proportions.

### Competing explanations

The statements may accurately reflect substantial proprietary progress, may
use an internal threshold unlike other AGI definitions, or may be optimistic
organizational forecasting. Public evidence in the article cannot discriminate
among these possibilities.

### Independence and evidence lineage

- **Overlapping authors or institutions:** The journalist is not an OpenAI
  employee.
- **Shared funding or access control:** OpenAI controlled access to internal
  systems and staff; TIME discloses a commercial agreement with OpenAI.
- **Shared data, sample, model, checkpoint, or benchmark:** Claims rely on
  proprietary OpenAI systems and demonstrations.
- **Replication category:** Not applicable for verifying that statements were
  made; no independent replication of the capability claims.

### Funding, conflicts, and incentives

OpenAI leaders have commercial, reputational, fundraising, policy, and
competitive incentives. TIME's disclosed OpenAI agreement and its owner's
Anthropic connection are relevant context. The article also includes critical
material rather than functioning solely as promotion.

### Evidence-profile summary

The source verifies that senior OpenAI leaders communicated unusually short
AGI timelines and an “80%” estimate. It provides no validated quantitative
evidence that OpenAI or the field is actually 80% of the way to AGI.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The reporting is a material horizon signal because it concerns proprietary
frontier systems and senior leadership expectations. It justifies timely
investigation and scenario review, not adoption of the estimate as fact.

### Claims this source supports

- Mark Chen made the reported “80%” estimate, and Sam Altman made the reported
  end-of-2026 internal-AGI prediction.

### Claims this source does not support

- That AGI is 80% complete under a validated or shared definition.
- That an internal AGI system will exist by the end of 2026.
- That the reported systems are conscious, sentient, moral patients, safe, or
  ready for deployment.
