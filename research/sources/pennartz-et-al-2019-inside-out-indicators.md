## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The authors ask how internal properties associated with experience can be
connected to externally observable indicators in animals and machines.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Neuroscience review and indicator construction
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** Animal/human neuroscience and
  intelligent-machine literature up to 2019.
- **System boundary and version:** Animals, 2019 deep-learning networks, and
  robots; not contemporary LLM deployments.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Six domains: goal-directed/model-
  based behavior; anatomical/physiological integration; psychometrics and
  metacognition; episodic memory; illusions/multistable perception; and
  visuospatial behavior.
- **Comparators and controls:** Cross-domain consistency rather than a single
  threshold.
- **Analysis:** Inside-out inference from proposed experiential functions and
  brain mechanisms to observable indicators.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** No new dataset.

### Main findings or conclusions

The authors propose gradual evidence accumulation across six indicators and
argue that no one measure is decisive. They judged the intelligent machines
available in 2019 not indicated to be conscious under their framework.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The paper rejects a brief behavioral/Turing-style result as sufficient and
emphasizes cross-examination. Its 2019 negative assessment is not evidence about
later systems.

### Source-reported limitations

Indicators do not define a hard threshold and require consistency across
evidence types; the approach is a proposal rather than a validated scale.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

The inside-out mapping begins with contested views about consciousness's
features and biological function. Several behavioral indicators could be
optimized without experience. The Glasgow Coma Scale analogy cannot supply
validity across substrates.

### Competing explanations

Flexible behavior, memory, metacognition, or susceptibility to engineered
illusions may reflect non-conscious computation; other theories might prioritize
different internal properties.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

It offers an independent methodological alternative and reinforces caution
about one-shot behavioral demonstrations.

### Claims this source supports

- Methodological claim: evidence should converge across multiple domains and
  time rather than depend on one output.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against

- A brief imitation or Turing-style test as decisive evidence.

### Claims this source does not support

- That the six indicators are validated for modern AI, or that their presence
  proves experience.
