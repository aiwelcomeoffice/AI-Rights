## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The authors ask which computations are associated with conscious processing
and whether machines could implement them.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Neuroscience review plus functional analysis
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** Selected human neuroscience
  and machine-learning literature.
- **System boundary and version:** AI systems available by 2017; not modern
  language-model deployments.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** C0 for unconscious specialized
  computation, C1 for global availability, and C2 for self-monitoring.
- **Comparators and controls:** Functional comparison between brains and
  machines.
- **Analysis:** Theory-guided synthesis.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Not applicable.

### Main findings or conclusions

The authors argue that then-current machines primarily implemented C0-like
processing and that machine consciousness would require architectures
supporting global availability and self-monitoring. This is a theory-dependent
interpretation, not an empirical measurement of subjective experience.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

High performance at specialized tasks does not by itself satisfy the authors'
C1/C2 criteria. The framework does not settle phenomenal consciousness beyond
its chosen functional analysis.

### Source-reported limitations

The mapping from computational functions to consciousness depends on the
global-workspace and metacognitive framework; exact caveat locators are TODO:
verify.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

The AI examples are historically dated. C1 and C2 may be measurable
functional capacities without resolving whether there is experience, and
current systems require configuration-specific assessment rather than analogy.

### Competing explanations

Global information access or metacognitive-looking output might arise through
non-conscious algorithms, learned reporting, or external scaffolding.

### Evidence-profile summary

The paper supports distinguishing task capability from theory-relevant global
availability and monitoring. It cannot establish phenomenal consciousness or
classify contemporary AI.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

It supplies architecture-linked hypotheses and a warning against equating
intelligence with consciousness.

  candidate functions under one influential family of theories.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against
