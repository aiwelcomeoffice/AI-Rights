## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The article profiles DroidUp and founder Li Qingdu, recounts a company and
product history, presents Moya as a move from laboratory work toward real
settings, and argues that humanlike robots could address social and elder-care
needs.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Narrative company profile using founder
  statements, company history, descriptive technical claims, and editorial
  interpretation.
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** DroidUp, Li Qingdu, Moya, and
  earlier DroidUp robots. Interview and documentary selection methods are not
  reported.
- **System boundary and version:** Moya at the product-name level. No unit,
  hardware revision, AI model, weights, training, runtime, memory, prompts,
  network dependencies, operator, or deployment configuration is identified.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Weight, degrees of freedom,
  endurance, temperature, facial mechanisms, motors, and sensors are reported,
  but measurement methods, tolerances, conditions, and test protocols are not.
  “Emotion,” “soul,” “understanding,” and “humanlike” are not operationalized.
- **Comparators and controls:** None disclosed.
- **Analysis:** Profile narrative; no statistical, engineering-validation, or
  user-study method.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** None linked for the Moya
  claims.

### Main findings or conclusions

**Reported events and article narrative:**

- The article says DroidUp entered Zhangjiang Robot Valley and formally
  released Moya in 2026. It calls Moya a global first; that superlative was not
  independently verified (paragraphs under “全球首款完全仿生机器人，从实验室走向真实场景”).
- The article describes Moya's screen appearance as very close to a person and
  says it can appear indistinguishable while standing still. This is an
  editorial or perceptual characterization, not a measured finding.

**Founder- or company-attributed technical claims:**

- The paragraph beginning “Moya整机约30公斤” reports approximately 30 kg
  total weight, more than 100 whole-body degrees of freedom, 8–10 hours of
  endurance, a 3D-printed lattice outer material, a PEEK skeleton, and silicone
  skin maintained at 32–36°C.
- The paragraph beginning “身体之外是情感层” reports mechanisms described as
  simulating 40 facial muscles and producing more than 60 microexpressions,
  three-dimensional eye movement, scanning, tracking, and image stabilization.
  It also relays Li Qingdu's preference for an amygdala analogy and “soul”
  language. Those are company interpretations, not evidence of emotion or
  experience.
- The paragraph beginning “再往下是交互层” reports 12 miniature motors and
  tactile sensors in a flexible hand, binocular vision for ranging and
  obstacle avoidance, and a proprietary DroidSoul engine said to combine
  vision, speech, touch, and posture.

**Normative framing and prediction reported by the source:**

- Li Qingdu frames robots as trusted partners rather than merely tools. That
  is his philosophical or commercial position, not a scientific conclusion.
- In the paragraph beginning “谈及未来,” he predicts that within five years a
  person may be unable to distinguish a robot seated before them from a human.
  This is a founder prediction without a disclosed forecast method.
- The article presents Moya-type systems as a possible response to home-based
  elder-care and companionship demand. It reports no Moya care deployment,
  user study, or outcome evidence.

The article reports no negative tests, failure rates, safety incidents,

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

The article is written in a strongly affirmative profile style and relies
heavily on founder and company representations. It gives no test procedures,
raw measurements, unit provenance, source list, independent evaluator, or
adverse results. Xinhua hosting does not show that technical claims were
independently tested. DroidUp's own news index labels the profile a repost,
which signals an evidence-lineage connection but does not establish the
article's original authoring or editorial process. Translation and the byline
identity remain unreviewed.

### Competing explanations

Reported behaviors could be implemented through different mixtures of
scripted animation, conventional control, perception models, generative AI,
retrieval, teleoperation, and supervision. A humanlike appearance may affect
observers without demonstrating any internal emotion or experience. Reported
specifications could be accurate under particular undisclosed conditions,
marketing approximations, or descriptions of a prototype rather than a stable
product.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The source makes clear why Moya is a valuable research case: embodiment,
humanlike appearance, facial mechanisms, gaze-like behavior, multimodal
interaction claims, and care framing converge in one product narrative. It
also shows why the project must separate observer response and company
language from evidence about system architecture or morally relevant
experience. The proposed care role raises human safety, consent, dependency,
privacy, manipulation, accountability, and accessibility questions whether or
not a robot is sentient.
