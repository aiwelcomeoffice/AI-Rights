## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The authors compare higher-order, global-workspace, re-entry and predictive
processing, and integrated-information approaches, asking how they explain
consciousness and how their predictions might be distinguished.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Comparative narrative review
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** Selected consciousness
  theories and human neuroscience findings; no reproducible search corpus
  reported in the accessible metadata.
- **System boundary and version:** Human consciousness science, not an AI
  system evaluation.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Theory-specific neural,
  functional, and phenomenological predictions.
- **Comparators and controls:** Cross-theory comparison; no experimental
  control group.
- **Analysis:** Conceptual and evidential comparison.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Not applicable to this review.

### Main findings or conclusions

The review reports substantial diversity in what theories explain and how they
relate consciousness to mechanism. The authors' synthesis is that the field
does not yet have a settled way to adjudicate among the major theories. This is
a review conclusion, not direct evidence about any AI system.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

Empirical results are not presented as cleanly selecting one theory. Some
apparent disagreements reflect different explanatory targets or auxiliary
assumptions rather than decisive tests.

### Source-reported limitations

Theories use overlapping concepts and predictions, and empirical
discrimination remains difficult. Exact passages are TODO: verify from the
version of record.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

The source is selective rather than systematic, centers human neural evidence,
and does not validate cross-substrate indicators for AI. Its value is as a map
of disputed theory, not a meter for consciousness.

### Competing explanations

Agreement of a system with one functional description might reflect ordinary
information processing without phenomenal experience; theory underdetermination
remains unresolved.
