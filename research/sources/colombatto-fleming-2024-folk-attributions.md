## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

The authors examine how people attribute consciousness to ChatGPT and which
individual differences correlate with those judgments.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Cross-sectional online survey; no preregistration
  was identified in the article or linked materials
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** 300 U.S. participants
  recruited through Prolific in July 2023 with age and gender quotas.
- **System boundary and version:** Participants judged ChatGPT in the survey;
  judgments, not system outputs, were the outcome.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** A single-item graded consciousness
  judgment plus measures of AI use, attitudes, and demographics.
- **Comparators and controls:** Individual-difference associations.
- **Analysis:** Descriptive frequencies and regression/association analyses.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Open Science Framework
  materials, data, and code: <https://osf.io/49w7m>

### Main findings or conclusions

The sample showed substantial above-minimum attribution: 67% chose above the
minimum response, described by the authors as assigning some possibility of
consciousness, and 23% answered above the scale midpoint. More frequent LLM use was associated with higher
attribution. These are observations about people's judgments; the association
does not establish causation or AI consciousness.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

Most participants did not place their judgment above the midpoint. Associations
did not make experience observable, and several proposed predictors were not
equivalent in strength; exact model table extraction is TODO: verify.

### Source-reported limitations

The authors note the sample/context and measurement limits and do not present
public attribution as evidence that ChatGPT is conscious.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

One U.S. online sample, a single main consciousness item, possible concept
priming, and cross-sectional associations limit generalization and causal
interpretation. Percentages depend on how the response scale is categorized.

### Competing explanations

Use may increase anthropomorphic familiarity, people already inclined to
attribute consciousness may use LLMs more, and third variables may influence
both.

provides no positive or negative measurement of LLM experience.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

It helps distinguish social reaction and anthropomorphism from scientific
evidence and supports public communication safeguards.

### Claims this source supports

- Empirical observation: substantial portions of this sample chose above the
  minimum response when judging ChatGPT consciousness.

### Claims this source challenges or weighs against

- The study documents graded and sometimes confident attributions but does not
  test whether confidence is calibrated to scientific evidence.

### Claims this source does not support

- That ChatGPT is conscious, that use causes attribution, or that the U.S. sample
  represents all publics.
