## What the source reports

### Research question or proposition

Seth asks which metaphysical and scientific assumptions make artificial
consciousness plausible and argues for biological naturalism: consciousness may
depend on being a living system, not only on computation.

### Methods

- **Design or argument form:** Target-article philosophical and scientific
  argument
- **Population, sample, corpus, or authorities:** Consciousness theory,
  neuroscience, AI, and biological organization literature.
- **System boundary and version:** Current AI trajectories in general; no
  specific model audit.
- **Measures and operational definitions:** Computational equivalence,
  biological organization, brain-like and life-like routes.
- **Comparators and controls:** Computational functionalism versus biological
  naturalism.
- **Analysis:** Premise-based inference and scenario comparison.
- **Data, code, materials, or preregistration:** Not applicable.

### Main findings or conclusions

Seth argues that computational sufficiency should not be assumed and that
current AI development trajectories are unlikely to yield consciousness if
biological naturalism is correct. Brain-like or life-like systems are presented
as more plausible routes under that view.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary findings

The article acknowledges that computational functionalism would support a
different conclusion. It offers no direct observation capable of detecting or
excluding experience in a current AI.

### Source-reported limitations

The conclusion depends on a contested metaphysical/scientific position and is
framed probabilistically rather than as proof.

## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

Biological naturalism is not empirically established as a necessary condition
for consciousness. Predictions about “current trajectories” are system- and
time-sensitive. The article's value is clarifying a serious alternative, not
settling it.

Open peer commentary had begun by the cutoff. [Mazor
2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X2510304X) argues that evidential
underdetermination combined with moral stakes can encourage rationalization in
consciousness science. That is one external critical perspective, not a summary
of the full commentary set; complete commentary and author-response synthesis
remains TODO.

### Competing explanations

Computational functionalism, organizational invariance, and substrate-neutral
mechanistic accounts permit artificial consciousness without living biology.
