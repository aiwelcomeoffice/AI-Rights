## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

This is a manufacturer-controlled, selectively curated index. Most items link
to separate pages, while this record assesses only the summaries visible on
the supplied URL. Event attendance and a temporary public role are not the
same as a sustained deployment. The summaries provide no architecture,
operator, error, safety, privacy, user-consent, or outcome details. Working
translations have not received bilingual review.

### Competing explanations

Moya may have carried out the reported roles autonomously, under scripted
control, through teleoperation, through close human supervision, or through a
combination of these. A production agreement may indicate real manufacturing
capacity, an intention, a framework agreement, or a promotional milestone;
the index does not distinguish among these possibilities.

### Independence and evidence lineage

- **Overlapping authors or institutions:** DroidUp publishes reports about its
  own systems and commercial activity.
- **Shared funding or access control:** DroidUp controls product access and the
  index's selection and framing.
- **Shared data, sample, model, checkpoint, or benchmark:** No assessable data
  or benchmark is provided.
- **Shared methods, code, measures, or evaluators:** Not disclosed.
- **Claims derived from an earlier source:** Several summaries link to

- **Replication category:** Not attempted; repeated promotional reports are

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The reports make Moya useful as a bounded social-robot case because they place
a humanlike embodied system in policy communication, presentation, and
companionship-adjacent narratives. Those settings raise human consent,
privacy, disclosure, attachment, manipulation, accessibility, safety, and
operator-accountability questions independently of robot sentience. The
production-scale summary also makes population-scale governance worth asking
about without assuming that the announced scale was achieved.
