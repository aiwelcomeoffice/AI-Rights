## Source report and limits

The Methods and Results distinguish technical profiling, conversational
dynamics, domain benchmarks, and prompted reasoning explanations. Methods,
“Technical profiling,” describes feedback from MINDapps users and a patient
advisory board; Discussion describes the NAMI partnership and invitation to
contribute. The benchmark format uses graded expert reference judgments,
including SIRI-2; additional clinical cases were undergoing validation.
It does not report a detailed lived-experience rating cohort or clinical
outcome validation of the platform. Generated reasoning explanations are
observable outputs, not direct internal-state measurements.

## Appraisal, independence, and conflicts

For the narrow claim that affected-party input is part of the design,
relevance is direct, method descriptive, causal strength absent, and
independent replication not applicable to establishing the authors' design
report. Effectiveness and representativeness remain untested here. Input
may improve coverage or may reflect a narrow advisory sample; this paper does
not discriminate their extent.

The platform developers authored the paper. Its institutional
[project description](https://www.digitalpsych.org/mindbench/) was checked
as context, not a separate empirical confirmation. Funding disclosures include
NIH/NIHR, Wellcome, and foundation support; competing-interest disclosures
include consulting relationships and Torous's journal editorial role
(Funding; Ethics declarations). These disclosures warrant attention, not
automatic dismissal. Source-specific sponsorship effects were not assessed.
