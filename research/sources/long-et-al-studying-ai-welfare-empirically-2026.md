## Source claim and method

The abstract and Part I §§1–3 distinguish whether an entity has a **welfare ground**, what might be in its **welfare interests if it does**, which entity is assessed (model, instance, persona), and behavioral, internal, and developmental evidence. The authors argue for probabilistic, pluralistic, targeted, ethical, transparent, partly independent research. Their report surveys early work and methods; it does not present a direct welfare test of Moya, Asimov 1, IRON, or another identified humanoid in the portions used here.

The authors discuss consciousness, sentience, intentional agency, and rational agency as different possible grounds under differing philosophical views (Part I §1.1). The existence of one ground would not identify a particular benefit or harm (Part I §1.2). The report's 2024 predecessor and related Butlin indicator work share authors and concepts; do not count citations among them as independent empirical corroboration.

## Claim-specific appraisal

**Claim assessed:** Humanoid-welfare research must separate subjecthood/grounds, conditional interests, and the assessed model/instance boundary. **Direction:** Conceptual support. Relevance is high; reasoning is inspectable; no completed humanoid experiment, replication, or validated measurement threshold follows. Causal strength and robustness for a robot are not applicable; discriminating value depends on future system-matched tests; conceptual uncertainty remains material. The report addresses both over- and under-attribution but its advocacy role and shared author lineage warrant separate appraisal of opposing arguments.
