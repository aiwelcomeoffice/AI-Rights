## Critical appraisal

### Reviewer-identified limitations

The footage is brief and edited; shot selection, rehearsals, failed takes,
remote control, scripts, off-camera operators, and post-production are not
disclosed. Visual inspection cannot verify warmth, touch, materials, internal
architecture, recognition, memory, dialogue, autonomy, safety, or care
competence. The English captions may themselves summarize or translate speech,
and the underlying audio was not independently transcribed. No raw footage or
unit provenance was available.

### Competing explanations

Visible orientation and movement could arise from autonomous perception and
control, scripted sequences, teleoperation, manual cueing, editing, or a
combination. Apparent eye contact or facial expression can be generated by
mechanisms without any corresponding emotion or experience. Humanlike design
may increase perceived warmth or may cause discomfort; no audience study is
included.

## Relevance to AI Rights & Welcome

The visible combination of a humanlike body, facial motion, gaze-like behavior,
and care or companionship framing illustrates why anthropomorphism and human
attachment require study. Human treatment of a humanlike robot can affect
people and social norms even if the robot has no morally relevant experience.
Conversely, the edited behavior is not a sound basis for ruling experience in
or out in this or any later architecture.
