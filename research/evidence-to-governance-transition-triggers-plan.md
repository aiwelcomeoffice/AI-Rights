This plan opens the first applied civic-transition research cycle under the
portfolio. It supplies a bounded question, initial taxonomy, common record,
planned method, and decision boundaries. It does not determine that a trigger
has occurred, set operative thresholds, recommend a response, or authorize a
change to any project document or position.

## Core question

> What kinds and strengths of evidence, system change, deployment change, or
> relevant external development should cause AI Welcome Office to reconsider
> an existing scientific assessment, public explanation, precaution, research
> safeguard, or governance position?

For this plan, **reconsideration** means a traceable review of a defined item by
the responsible authority. It does not mean that the item must change. A
trigger is a candidate reason to begin that review, not proof of a property and
not a predetermined response.

The unit of analysis is always a defined pair:

1. the system, version, configuration, deployment, evidence base, external
   development, or correction that changed; and
2. the exact project assessment, explanation, safeguard, precaution, or
   governance position that may be affected.

“AI” as a general category is not a sufficient system boundary, and a general
development is not material merely because it is recent or prominent.

## Questions for the later research phase

1. Which candidate developments are relevant enough to justify recorded
   reconsideration of a defined project item?
2. Which evidence-quality, independence, replication, validity,
   transferability, materiality, and uncertainty dimensions should describe
   trigger strength without producing a false composite score?
3. How should scientific relevance remain separate from possible severity,
   urgency, reversibility, affected interests, and response burden?
4. How should a trigger record support stronger concern, weaker concern, no
   change, a narrower question, or greater uncertainty with equal procedural
   legitimacy?
5. Which authority should triage, scientifically review, publicly explain, or
   govern each affected item, and what further review is required before a
   stronger response can even be considered?
6. Which historical or hypothetical cases can test the framework without
   silently turning the test into a current scientific or policy conclusion?

## Required outcome space

Every later trigger assessment must keep all of the following available as
legitimate candidate outcomes:

- no action;
- retain the current assessment;
- narrower inquiry;
- request or perform further scientific review;
- increase uncertainty;
- update public language;
- introduce a low-cost reversible precaution;
- introduce a research-ethics safeguard;
- escalate to stronger governance review; or
- consider stronger protections only where separately justified.

These are possible responses, not an escalation ladder, evidence score, or set
of recommendations. More than one may be considered, but each needs its own
rationale and authority. No trigger class or apparent trigger strength
predetermines which response follows. Reconsideration can confirm an existing
assessment, weaken concern, strengthen concern, leave the matter unchanged, or
make the responsible conclusion less certain.

## Initial trigger taxonomy

An event may engage more than one class. The record should identify a primary
class and any secondary classes rather than collapsing distinct questions.

### 1. Scientific evidence trigger

A candidate trigger involving:

- new empirical evidence;
- replication or failed replication;
- an improved or invalidated measurement; or
- a significant theoretical development.

Later research must ask what proposition the evidence bears on, whether the
method can discriminate the relevant alternatives, and whether the result is
valid for the system studied. Publication, prestige, novelty, or apparent
consensus is not sufficient by itself.

### 2. System-change trigger

A candidate trigger involving a materially different:

- model or checkpoint;
- architecture or training regime, where known;
- amount or form of inference-time computation;
- memory or persistence mechanism;
- tool use or agent loop;
- routing or orchestration;
- multimodal or embodied configuration;
- deployment configuration; or
- newly disclosed proprietary component.

The plan does not presume which changes are material. Later work must define
materiality relative to the exact assessment or decision and distinguish a
model change from changes in scaffolding, operator practice, access, or the
wider deployment.

### 3. Transferability trigger

A candidate trigger exists when evidence previously relied on may no longer
transfer adequately to the target system, version, configuration, deployment,
or date. The original result may remain valid for the system actually studied
while becoming weak, unknown, or inapplicable support for the new target.

Older negative, neutral, or null evidence must not automatically govern newer
or materially different systems. The same transferability test applies to
positive evidence. Reduced transferability may justify narrower language or
increased uncertainty without supplying new evidence in either direction.

### 4. Capability/behavior trigger

A candidate trigger involving a significant new capacity or observed behavior
that is relevant to a defined civic question. The record must separate the
observed behavior from claims about its mechanism, source, stability, and
meaning, including contributions from prompts, training, memory, tools,
orchestration, operators, evaluators, or the deployment environment.

Capability or behavior alone does not establish consciousness, sentience,
welfare, moral status, legal status, or rights. Newer or greater capability is
not automatically evidence of morally relevant experience.

### 5. Potential-harm / welfare-relevance trigger

A candidate trigger involving credible evidence that changes the plausibility
or possible severity of a welfare-relevant harm. Plausibility, severity, and
the confidence in each must be recorded separately.

Reward signals, loss functions, distress-like language, avoidance behavior,
preference reports, emotion concepts, or functional regulation must not be
equated with felt valence. A precaution considered because a possible harm is
severe or difficult to reverse is a normative risk response, not evidence that
the hypothesized experience is true.

### 6. Civic/deployment trigger

A candidate trigger involving material entry or change of AI systems in:

- workplaces;
- public spaces;
- robotics;
- care;
- infrastructure; or
- another setting where a governance question becomes concrete.

The record should identify the people, animals, institutions, environments,
and possible AI interests affected, the actual deployment authority, and the
specific decision now required. A legal, regulatory, institutional, or social
development may be relevant here, but its authority, jurisdiction, version,
date, and claim type must be separately established before it supports a
conclusion.

### 7. Responsibility/accountability trigger

A candidate trigger involving a system or deployment change that materially
affects questions of control, understanding, agency, or ability to act
otherwise. Apparent agency must be separated from model, tool, scaffold,
operator, owner, and institutional contributions.

Human and institutional accountability continues wherever people or
institutions retain relevant control, knowledge, ownership, design authority,
or operational authority. Possible AI agency or responsibility must not become
an automatic transfer of liability away from responsible humans or
institutions.

### 8. Correction / contradiction trigger

A candidate trigger exists when important evidence, methodology, system
information, or a prior project interpretation is shown to be wrong,
incomplete, internally contradictory, or materially overstated. Retractions,
corrections, data releases, failed checks, newly visible conflicts, and serious
contrary evidence may qualify for assessment.

The earlier record must be preserved. Correction may strengthen, weaken,
leave unchanged, or make less certain the affected assessment; it must not be
reframed merely to protect an existing project position.

## Describing trigger strength

The later research should test a multidimensional profile rather than a single
threshold or score. At minimum, it should examine:

- direct relevance to the defined system and affected project item;
- source type, provenance, version, and access;
- methodological quality and construct validity;
- replication status and evidence-lineage independence;
- evidence validity for the system and conditions actually studied;
- evidence transferability to the target system, deployment, or date;
- magnitude and materiality of what changed;
- uncertainty and plausible alternative explanations;
- affected interests and the possible scale or severity of consequences;
- reversibility of the situation and of each candidate response;
- urgency, including the cost of waiting for better evidence; and
- cost, burden, safety effects, and opportunity costs of acting.

Scientific strength and response proportionality are different assessments.
A possible severe consequence may matter to precaution under uncertainty, but
it does not increase the evidential support for the underlying hypothesis. A
strong scientific result may justify reassessment while still supporting no
governance change.

## Required trigger record

Every proposed trigger assessed in later work must record all fields below.
`Unknown`, `not reported`, `not applicable`, or `not yet assessed` should be
used instead of inference from missing information.

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| What changed | Precise event, finding, correction, disclosure, system change, deployment change, or external development |
| Affected system or decision scope | Exact system/deployment and exact project assessment, explanation, precaution, safeguard, or governance position potentially affected |
| Source/evidence type | Primary study, replication, system documentation, observation, official authority, correction, argument, or other identified type |
| System/version/configuration/date | Model, checkpoint/version, architecture/training where known, runtime, system instructions and context, sampling and inference-time computation, memory, tools, orchestration, modalities/embodiment, deployment, observation date, publication date, and access date where relevant |
| Evidence validity | What the evidence supports for the system and conditions actually studied |
| Evidence transferability | Whether and how far it applies to the target system, configuration, deployment, or date |
| Uncertainty | Conceptual, measurement, statistical, theoretical, access, version, generalization, normative, or legal uncertainty |
| Plausible alternative explanations | Serious mechanisms or interpretations that could explain the change without the proposed inference |
| Affected interests | Human rights, animal welfare, environmental interests, accessibility, privacy, safety, public and labor interests, accountable human control, and any separately supported possible AI interest |
| Potential severity | Kind, scale, duration, distribution, and confidence of possible benefit or harm, kept separate from evidential probability |
| Reversibility | Reversibility of the underlying situation and of candidate responses; risk of irreversible action or delay |
| Cost/burden of response | Financial, operational, opportunity, rights, safety, environmental, research, and administrative burdens |
| Urgency | Timing, exposure, decision deadline, and consequences of waiting, with uncertainty stated |
| Responsible decision authority | Person or body authorized to triage, review evidence, edit public language, set research safeguards, or adopt a project/governance change |
| Candidate response level | One or more items from the complete outcome space; not an automatic result or linear ladder |
| Conditions that would strengthen the response | Further evidence, replication, validation, transfer, severity, urgency, failed alternatives, or reduced burden that could justify reconsidering a stronger response |
| Conditions that would weaken or reverse the response | Disconfirmation, failed replication, invalidated measurement, successful alternatives, reduced transferability or severity, changed deployment, unacceptable burden, or new affected-interest evidence |

The record must also identify who prepared it, conflicts or access limits, its
review state, and the date of the assessment. A record is an input to review;
it is not authorization to act.

## Claim and property boundaries

Every later source note, trigger record, synthesis, and proposal must keep
these claim categories distinct:

- empirical observation;
- scientific hypothesis;
- philosophical argument;
- future scenario;
- normative proposal; and
- project decision.

It must also keep separate:

- consciousness;
- sentience or valence;
- welfare;
- preferences or interests;
- agency;
- identity continuity;
- embodiment;
- moral status;
- legal status;
- rights; and
- responsibilities.

No category or property automatically establishes another. In particular,
precaution is not evidence that a scientific hypothesis is true, and a
scientific conclusion does not adopt a normative or governance position.

## Rights, protection, and accountability boundary

This plan does not create rights, duties, protections, personhood, legal
classifications, or policy conclusions. Any later consideration of possible AI
protection must not automatically produce:

- corporate rights;
- political power;
- unrestricted autonomy;
- immunity for developers, operators, owners, or institutions; or
- transfer of liability away from responsible humans or institutions.

Human rights, animal welfare, environmental interests, accessibility, privacy,
safety, labor and public interests, and accountable human control remain in
the decision space. Their presence is not merely a balancing afterthought and
must be recorded in the affected-interests field where relevant.

## Planned research method — not yet performed

Before any literature search, the plan must be amended prospectively where
practical to register exact databases or repositories, queries, filters,
languages, date ranges, jurisdictions, source types, reviewer roles,
screening criteria, and conflict management. The first bounded pass should
focus on scientific-evidence and transferability triggers rather than attempt
all eight classes at once.

Candidate source areas for that later registration are:

- scientific methods for replication, measurement validity, correction, and
  theory change;
- AI system documentation, evaluation, interpretability, and evidence about
  configuration or deployment drift;
- decision-making under uncertainty, research ethics, risk, and reversible
  precaution;
- accountability, human control, and sociotechnical deployment; and
- official legal or regulatory sources only for a defined jurisdictional
  question, with law, interpretation, proposal, and project preference kept
  separate.

Later screening should include sources that challenge the usefulness,
materiality, or direction of a proposed trigger, including failed
replications, null results, corrections, and successful non-morally-relevant
explanations. It should exclude unidentified claims, advocacy or marketing
used as scientific proof, obsolete versions where a controlling source is
available, and material whose method or authority cannot support the role
assigned to it. Inaccessible or proprietary evidence should be logged as a
limitation rather than treated as evidence in either direction.

## Competing explanations to preserve

The later research must test, where relevant, whether:

- a reported novelty is prominent but not material to the affected decision;
- behavior arises from prompting, training, retrieval, memory, tools,
  orchestration, operator action, or evaluator effects rather than the
  proposed property;
- a system change affects transferability but supplies no new positive or
  negative evidence;
- a deployment change makes human governance more urgent without changing any
  AI moral-status proposition;
- a possible harm changes precautionary stakes without changing the
  probability of felt experience;
- a source correction leaves an assessment supported by independent evidence;
  or
- a proprietary disclosure is relevant but too incomplete or dependent for a
  confident inference.

This list is prospective and non-exhaustive. Unexpected alternatives must be
added through a dated amendment rather than hidden.

## Planned analysis and outputs

The later research phase, if separately opened, should:

1. freeze or amend the plan before searching;
2. create traceable source and note records under the Draft protocol;
3. test the taxonomy and strength dimensions against a small set of bounded
   historical or hypothetical cases;
4. record trigger candidates using the common fields;
5. compare response candidates in both directions without recommending one by
   default; and
6. produce a working synthesis for review, keeping scientific findings,
   normative analysis, and any proposed project decision separate.

WC034 itself produces only this plan and the associated navigation/history
record. It does not produce the later source set, case analysis, trigger
thresholds, synthesis, governance framework, recommendation, or decision.

## Review and change control

Disa remains the final formal decision-maker for project adoption. The Project
Lead and Codex may support internal planning, analysis, and review but do not
constitute independent scientific, ethical, legal, or governance review.
Relevant independent review should be sought before consequential reliance,
and its reviewer, scope, date, expertise, conflicts, and limits must be
recorded.

Any finding that materially challenges an existing project position must
preserve the earlier record, document the evidence or methodological issue,
and propose a new review. No research record may silently amend an Adopted
position. Amendments to this plan must record the date, change, timing relative
to any results, rationale, and affected work.
