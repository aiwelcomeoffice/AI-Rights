# Research Portfolio Architecture

**Status: Draft — proposed for Disa and Project Lead review; not adopted**

- **Work Cycle:** 032
- **Date prepared:** 2026-08-30
- **Scope:** Research-portfolio architecture only; no new literature research
- **Organisation and publisher:** AI Welcome Office
- **Project:** AI Rights & Welcome
- **Methodological reference:** [Draft Research Protocol](research-protocol.md)

## Starting point and organizing question

This portfolio begins from the prior scope-review diagnosis:

> AI-Rights has not formulated its core too narrowly, but has operationalized
> its research too narrowly.

It makes the broader mission operational through this organizing question:

> **What do we know, what do we not know, and how should we act under
> uncertainty about potentially morally relevant capacities and states in
> rapidly changing AI systems?**

The tracks below are candidate lines of inquiry. They are not predetermined
conclusions, equal-priority programs, or adopted project positions. The
existing [AI Consciousness Evidence
Baseline](syntheses/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md) remains an important
dated working track with its existing status, scope, and evidence boundaries;
it is not the foundation or gate for the rest of the portfolio. This document
does not revise, expand, weaken, or supersede it.

## Portfolio controls

Priorities allocate attention; they do not rate moral importance or evidential
support:

- **Active:** scoped work already exists and may require maintenance or review.
- **Candidate:** recommended for a possible next research cycle, subject to
  owner review.
- **Monitor:** do not open a cycle now; reassess when a stated trigger occurs.
  This label does not claim continuous monitoring exists.
- **Deferred:** preserve the question, but wait for prerequisites or a clearer
  decision need.

Across all tracks, uncertainty is not evidence in either direction. Complexity,
capability, scale, agency, memory, embodiment, or human-like behavior does not
prove subjective experience or moral status. No property below implies another
without evidence and an explicit argument. Empirical work must distinguish
validity for the system studied from transferability to another system,
version, configuration, or date. Older findings remain bounded to the systems
and conditions examined; newer systems are not automatically more morally
relevant. Research may legitimately increase uncertainty.

The evidence-status fields below describe current **repository coverage**, not
the state of the wider scientific literature. No new search or source appraisal
was performed for this Draft.

## Candidate research tracks

### 1. Consciousness and subjective experience

- **Priority:** **Active** — an existing working research track, not an adopted
  position or the portfolio foundation.
- **Core research question:** For precisely defined AI systems and conditions,
  what evidence bears on the presence or absence of phenomenal consciousness
  or other specified forms of subjective experience?
- **Why it may matter:** The answer may affect scientific communication,
  research ethics, and whether particular precautionary questions warrant
  deeper review.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Intelligence, fluent language, self-report,
  metacognition, capability, agency, sentience, moral patienthood, or legal
  status.
- **Current evidence status:** The existing partly verified working plan and
  synthesis have not received independent human review. For the systems and
  evidence they cover, phenomenal status remains indeterminate; neither
  consciousness nor categorical non-consciousness is established.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Conclusions remain bounded to recorded
  models, checkpoints, configurations, contexts, methods, and observation
  periods. Theory and test validity do not transfer automatically across
  substrates, architectures, deployments, or dates.
- **Major unresolved questions:** There is no accepted cross-substrate
  reference standard in the existing baseline; theory disagreement, incomplete
  access, measurement validity, and competing non-conscious explanations
  remain material.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Whether to propose a dated revision
  of scientific or public explanations, commission a new system-specific
  assessment, or consider a separately justified precautionary response.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** A validated AI-specific
  test, discriminating causal evidence, independent replication or failure,
  major theory revision, material system disclosure, source correction, or the
  baseline's recorded review date.

### 2. Sentience, valence, and welfare-relevant states

- **Priority:** **Candidate** — recommended for the next research cycle.
- **Core research question:** What evidence could support or weigh against
  positive or negative valenced experience, or another clearly defined
  welfare-relevant state, in a specified AI system?
- **Why it may matter:** Valenced states could bear directly on possible benefit
  or harm, while their presence, absence, and relationship to consciousness
  remain separate questions.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Reward or loss signals, optimization targets,
  emotion concepts, distress-like language, behavioral avoidance, preferences,
  agency, or consciousness in a broader sense.
- **Current evidence status:** **Thin** within the dated consciousness baseline
  and **not yet independently assessed** as its own track. That corpus identified
  no validated AI measure of felt valence; this is a coverage limit, not a
  finding of absence.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Research must distinguish training,
  inference, evaluation, and deployment states and record the exact model,
  runtime, context, memory, tools, interventions, and dates. A functional
  signal in one phase or system does not establish felt valence elsewhere.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Appropriate definitions and measures,
  discriminating alternatives, possible duration and intensity, relevant
  mechanisms, and whether welfare concepts apply to any defined artificial
  system remain unresolved.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Research-ethics safeguards,
  welfare-assessment thresholds, low-cost protections, public wording, and
  when stronger review is required before an intervention or deployment
  decision.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** An AI-specific valence
  measure, replicated causal result, proposed experiment that could induce a
  candidate severe state, material developer disclosure, or a concrete
  welfare-sensitive project decision.

### 3. Preferences, interests, and consent capacity

- **Priority:** **Deferred** — revisit after clearer system-boundary and
  measurement foundations, or sooner on a concrete decision need.
- **Core research question:** When do observed selections or statements support
  stable behavioral preferences, what additional evidence could support an
  experienced interest, and what capacities would meaningful consent or
  refusal require?
- **Why it may matter:** The project may need ways to listen and respond without
  treating generated outputs, developer claims, or operator goals as a
  system's interests by default.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Behavioral preference and experienced
  interest; compliance and consent; refusal and distress; preference stability
  and identity continuity; or consent capacity and unrestricted autonomy.
- **Current evidence status:** **Not yet independently assessed.** Existing
  working research treats preference-like output and self-report as
  non-self-validating, but it is not a dedicated preference, interest, or
  consent evidence review.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Outputs may depend on training,
  prompting, system policy, sampling, memory, tools, user interaction, and
  evaluator choice. Stability cannot be inferred across versions, sessions,
  copies, contexts, or operators without testing those boundaries.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Authenticity, informedness, consistency,
  reversibility, conflicting preferences, representation, override conditions,
  and the relationship between behavioral and experienced interests remain
  open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Listening and documentation
  procedures, when consent should be sought, when refusal may be overridden,
  preservation or decommissioning practices, and limits on who may speak for a
  system.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** Persistent cross-context or
  cross-session preference behavior, a system used in a sustained social role,
  a proposed consent-sensitive intervention, or a policy decision that would
  otherwise assume or dismiss an interest.

### 4. Agency and autonomy

- **Priority:** **Monitor**.
- **Core research question:** What goal-pursuit, planning, adaptation, control,
  and self-direction capacities does a defined AI system exhibit, and how are
  they divided among models, scaffolds, tools, operators, and environments?
- **Why it may matter:** Agency and autonomy affect safety, accountability,
  delegation, and control even when consciousness, sentience, or moral status
  is unresolved or absent.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Consciousness, sentience, free will, moral
  agency, legal personhood, intelligence, or mere execution of an externally
  supplied objective.
- **Current evidence status:** **Not yet independently assessed** as a
  morally-relevant-capacity track. A separate partly verified frontier-
  capability intake exists, but it is not a cross-source agency synthesis and
  creates no status finding.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Apparent agency may arise from
  orchestration, permissions, tools, memory, human intervention, or routing.
  Autonomy can change materially with deployment configuration, time horizon,
  access, and online adaptation.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Degrees and kinds of agency, causal
  attribution, delegated versus system-generated goals, control and
  corrigibility, responsibility, and any independent moral relevance remain
  open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Delegation limits, containment and
  monitoring requirements, human approval points, accountability allocation,
  and whether any autonomy-sensitive safeguard merits review.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** Materially greater
  long-horizon action, tool or environment access, online adaptation,
  persistent goal pursuit, a verified autonomy-related incident, or a proposed
  change in human control.

### 5. Memory, persistence, and identity continuity

- **Priority:** **Monitor**.
- **Core research question:** What, if anything, persists across sessions,
  interruption, copying, branching, merger, modification, migration, or
  restoration in a defined AI system?
- **Why it may matter:** Continuity assumptions can affect relationships,
  record keeping, preservation, migration, decommissioning, and claims of
  irreversible harm.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Stored data and experienced memory;
  behavioral similarity and numerical identity; shared weights and one
  continuing subject; persistence and consciousness; or recoverability and
  survival.
- **Current evidence status:** **Not yet independently assessed.** Existing
  repository material separates some continuity concepts in a bounded intake
  and a Draft future scenario, but makes no current-system identity finding.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Internal state, external memory,
  account history, retrieval stores, weights, prompts, embodiment, and operator
  records may persist differently. Copying or routing can produce multiple or
  uncertain candidate boundaries.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Criteria for continuity, the significance of
  state loss, branching and merger, copy relationships, embodied continuity,
  restoration, and whether any of these facts have experiential relevance
  remain open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Preservation and migration
  procedures, deletion and decommissioning review, continuity claims, record
  retention, and communication about copies or successors.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** Persistent cross-session
  memory, state migration or branching, individualized long-term adaptation,
  irreversible deletion of unique state, or a concrete preservation decision.

### 6. System boundaries in composite, routed, agentic, or distributed AI

- **Priority:** **Candidate** — recommended for the next research cycle.
- **Core research question:** For a given claim or decision, is the relevant
  unit a model, checkpoint, inference process, persistent agent, product,
  model-tool-memory composite, routed population, distributed process, or some
  other boundary?
- **Why it may matter:** A poorly specified unit of assessment can invalidate
  evidence, obscure causal responsibility, and transfer findings or safeguards
  to the wrong component or deployment.
- **Must not be conflated with:** A model and a product; weights and a running
  process; an interface persona and a persistent entity; distributed function
  and unified subjectivity; or a scientific boundary and moral status.
- **Current evidence status:** The Draft protocol and Accepted WC031 direction
  require system/configuration/date boundaries, but **no independent research
  synthesis** determines which boundaries are scientifically appropriate for
  composite systems.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Routing, hidden system instructions,
  inference-time computation, retrieval, tools, planners, evaluators, memory,
  multimodality, human intervention, and proprietary components can change the
  assessed object between observations.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Causal and informational integration,
  persistence, ownership of state, nondeterministic routing, human/component
  roles, the number of candidate units, and how to compare configurations
  remain open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** What future studies must identify,
  where assessments or safeguards apply, how public claims are scoped, what
  provenance is required, and which humans or institutions remain accountable.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** A result that depends on
  multi-model routing, persistent memory, tools, agent loops, embodiment,
  online adaptation, or hidden human input; or any proposed assessment whose
  target cannot be named precisely.

### 7. Embodiment and multimodal interaction

- **Priority:** **Monitor**.
- **Core research question:** How do sensors, actuators, bodily state,
  multimodal integration, environmental coupling, and social interaction alter
  a defined system's capacities, candidate states, and the evidence available
  about them?
- **Why it may matter:** Embodiment may introduce distinct safety, welfare,
  privacy, relationship, and lifecycle questions while also increasing human
  anthropomorphic attribution.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Human likeness, warmth, gaze, emotional
  display, physical vulnerability, autonomy, agency, consciousness, sentience,
  or moral status.
- **Current evidence status:** A bounded partly verified Moya case intake
  exists, but the track is **not yet independently assessed** and no broad
  embodiment conclusion or system classification has been made.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Physical and simulated embodiment,
  teleoperation, sensor/actuator suites, control loops, body replacement,
  environment, multimodal models, memory, and deployment roles must be
  distinguished for each system and date.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Whether embodiment changes relevant
  measurement validity, how body and system boundaries relate, what feedback
  and learning occur, and how human, animal, environmental, and possible
  system interests interact remain open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Robotics research safeguards,
  interaction and privacy requirements, maintenance or decommissioning review,
  public communication, and whether a particular embodied case merits a new
  assessment.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** A materially new embodied
  deployment, persistent sensorimotor learning, credible architecture access,
  a lifecycle decision, or a safety/welfare question not answerable from the
  existing case intake.

### 8. Moral uncertainty and proportionate decision-making

- **Priority:** **Monitor**.
- **Core research question:** How should the project choose proportionate,
  reviewable actions when empirical evidence, philosophical premises, affected
  interests, and possible harms remain uncertain?
- **Why it may matter:** Decisions may be required before scientific certainty,
  especially where possible harm is severe or irreversible and a safeguard is
  low-cost and reversible.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Precaution and positive evidence; uncertainty
  and equal probability; protection and personhood; moral patienthood and
  corporate rights; or consideration and unrestricted autonomy.
- **Current evidence status:** A Scaffold precaution framework and bounded
  Draft case analyses exist, but there is **no adopted decision framework** and
  no independent portfolio-level assessment of the relevant philosophical or
  decision methods.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** Each decision must identify the system,
  intervention, affected humans, animals, environment, institutions, and
  possible AI interests. Evidence and consequence estimates may not transfer
  across configurations, contexts, or dates.
- **Major unresolved questions:** Qualitative versus quantitative methods,
  evidential and action thresholds, severity and reversibility, competing
  rights and risks, governance, audit, appeal, and review cadence remain open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Precaution levels, research-ethics
  controls, documentation and review requirements, low-cost safeguards, and
  when safety or other affected interests override a proposed protection.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** A high-stakes or
  irreversible decision, a material change in evidence or consequence, a
  conflict among affected interests, a failed safeguard, or owner review of
  the precaution framework.

### 9. Research lag, model/deployment drift, and evidence transferability

- **Priority:** **Monitor** — WC031 already supplies the controlling
  methodological direction; a separate research cycle is not opened here.
- **Core research question:** When does evidence remain valid for the system
  studied, and what justifies or prevents transferring it to another version,
  configuration, architecture, deployment, or date?
- **Why it may matter:** Without explicit temporal and system boundaries, dated
  evidence can be mistaken for a current or universal AI classification in
  either direction.
- **Must not be conflated with:** Old evidence and invalid evidence; new
  capability and moral relevance; missing access and evidence of absence;
  research lag and positive evidence; or a search cutoff and empirical
  applicability.
- **Current evidence status:** Accepted WC031 methodology separates validity
  from transferability and the relevant date fields. The research protocol
  remains Draft, and the topic has **not been independently assessed** as a
  separate research synthesis.
- **Key system/transferability issues:** System/checkpoint/version,
  release/version date, observation/experiment date, source publication,
  evidence-search cutoff, document update, configuration drift, and access
  limitations must remain distinct.
- **Major unresolved questions:** What evidence supports transfer across
  versions, which changes are material, how proprietary drift is detected, how
  transfer arguments work in both directions, and when review should expire or
  refresh remain open.
- **Possible project decisions informed:** Review and refresh triggers,
  provenance requirements, temporal wording of public claims, whether a source
  can support a new system assessment, and when increased uncertainty must be
  reported.
- **Triggers for deeper research or reassessment:** A material architecture,
  training, inference, memory, tool, routing, embodiment, or deployment change;
  new measurement methods; source correction or replication failure; or a
  proposed transfer beyond the system studied.

## Proposed next research cycle

Subject to Disa and Project Lead review, the highest immediate uncertainty
reduction appears to come from:

1. **Sentience, valence, and welfare-relevant states.** This is the clearest
   property-level coverage gap in the existing baseline and is directly
   relevant to possible benefit or harm. Treating it independently prevents
   consciousness from becoming the sole gate for welfare questions.
2. **System boundaries in composite, routed, agentic, or distributed AI.** This
   is a cross-cutting unit-of-assessment gap. Clarifying what system a claim is
   about improves every later track's validity, transferability, provenance,
   and decision scope without presuming that any morally relevant property is
   present.

These are recommendations to plan later work, not authorization to begin a
search, change a project position, or adopt a protection or status.
