## Organizing question

> **How can society prepare for and navigate a gradual, peaceful,
> responsible, safe, and welcoming coexistence with increasingly capable
> artificial systems under uncertainty, including if future evidence supports
> morally relevant capacities or experience in some systems?**

This is not a prediction that morally relevant properties exist in current AI
systems or will necessarily emerge. Some applied preparation may nevertheless
be justified under uncertainty where it is low-cost, reversible,
non-status-conferring, and compatible with human rights, safety, animal
welfare, environmental interests, and accountability. The preparation is a
normative and civic choice, not scientific evidence.

The scope may include software systems, agents, robots, humanoids, clusters,
swarms, distributed composites, or other artificial systems, provided each
target is defined rather than treated as part of a homogeneous class.

## Project role and capacity

AI Welcome Office is a small project and may remain small. It should remain
scientifically informed and capable of updating, but it should not imply that
it duplicates the primary experimental work of specialist consciousness,
neuroscience, interpretability, frontier-capability, or other fundamental-
science teams.

Its distinctive contribution is **applied civic-transition research**:
translating carefully scoped external evidence into questions about social
preparation, coexistence, public understanding, proportionate protection,
accountability, physical integration, lifecycle decisions, competing
interests, and governance change. AI Welcome Office may critically assess or
synthesize specialist work when a defined civic question requires it.

## Scientific, rights, and responsibility boundaries

- The project does not assume consciousness, sentience, welfare, moral status,
  personhood, rights, duties, or legal status in any current system, and does
  not assume their categorical absence.
- Uncertainty is not evidence. Complexity, capability, scale, agency, memory,
  embodiment, or human-like behavior does not prove subjective experience or
  moral status. One property does not imply another without evidence and an
  explicit argument.
- Possible protections do not automatically imply rights, personhood,
  unrestricted autonomy, political power, corporate rights, or immunity for
  developers, owners, operators, or institutions.
- Any possible AI responsibility or duty is conditional on evidence of the
  relevant capacity, understanding, control, and ability to act otherwise.
- AI responsibility must not automatically replace or reduce the
  responsibility of humans or institutions that retain relevant control,
  knowledge, power, design authority, ownership, or operational authority.
- Respect may be reciprocal while responsibility remains asymmetric.
- Research and project decisions must preserve human rights, animal welfare,
  public and environmental interests, safety, and accountable human control.
- Evidence may support action, no action, narrower inquiry, or increased
  uncertainty. Scientific findings, normative proposals, and project decisions
  remain separate.

## Three-layer portfolio

### 1. External evidence intake / monitor

**Role:** Maintain a bounded, critical intake of specialist evidence needed for
defined civic questions. This is not a commitment to exhaustive or continuous
monitoring, and it does not make AI Welcome Office a primary experimental
laboratory. A deeper synthesis should begin only when a recorded trigger or
decision need justifies the work and capacity exists.

| Foundational evidence domain | Intake purpose and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| **Consciousness and subjective experience** | Follow evidence relevant to defined systems without treating behavior, capability, self-report, or architecture as decisive by itself. The existing [AI Consciousness Evidence Baseline](syntheses/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md) remains an unchanged, dated working synthesis with its existing status and limits. |
| **Sentience, valence, and welfare measurement** | Follow work on positive or negative experience and welfare-relevant states without equating reward, loss, optimization, emotion concepts, avoidance, or distress-like language with felt valence. |
| **Preferences, interests, and consent capacities** | Distinguish observed choices or statements from stable preferences, experienced interests, informed consent, refusal, identity continuity, and unrestricted autonomy. |
| **Agency and autonomy** | Follow evidence about planning, goal pursuit, adaptation, control, and self-direction while separating model, scaffold, tool, operator, and deployment contributions. Agency is not consciousness, sentience, moral agency, or legal responsibility. |
| **Memory and identity mechanisms** | Follow evidence about internal and external memory, persistence, copying, branching, migration, merger, modification, and restoration without treating stored data, behavioral similarity, or shared weights as proof of experienced memory or continuing identity. |
| **Embodiment mechanisms** | Follow evidence about sensors, actuators, bodily state, multimodal integration, environmental coupling, and social interaction without treating embodiment, human likeness, or physical vulnerability as proof of experience or status. |
| **Relevant neuroscience, interpretability, and capability research** | Draw on specialist work needed to interpret other domains, including measurement validity, mechanisms, system behavior, and material frontier changes. Provider or laboratory access, conflicts, replication, and applicability must remain visible. |

The purpose of this layer is to inform applied work, not to force a verdict.
External evidence remains scoped to the systems, versions, configurations,
methods, and observation periods actually studied.

### 2. Supporting methodology

**Role:** Supply the controls required to interpret external research and to
keep applied civic work traceable. This layer may be maintained as needed, but
it is not a claim that a separate fundamental-methodology research program or
continuous monitoring operation exists.

| Methodological support | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **System boundaries** | Identify whether a claim concerns a model, checkpoint, inference process, product, persistent agent, routed system, tool-memory composite, distributed process, embodied system, human-system arrangement, or another defined unit. A scientific boundary is not a moral-status finding. |
| **Evidence validity and transferability** | Separate what evidence supports for the system and conditions studied from whether it can be generalized to another version, configuration, architecture, deployment, or date. |
| **Model/deployment drift and temporal applicability** | Keep system/version, release, observation/experiment, publication, evidence-search, and document-update dates distinct. Apply Accepted WC031 methodology symmetrically to positive, negative, neutral, and null findings. |
| **Claim classification** | Distinguish empirical observation, scientific hypothesis, philosophical argument, future scenario, normative position, and project decision. |
| **Provenance and evidence quality** | Record sources, versions, methods, access, independence, conflicts, competing explanations, limitations, corrections, and review status without manufacturing a score or consensus. |

These controls support decision relevance; they do not turn uncertainty,
research lag, proprietary access, or system change into evidence in either
direction.

### 3. Active applied civic-transition research

**Role:** Direct the project's scarce active research capacity toward civic
preparation and coexistence under uncertainty. Each project should remain
small, case-bounded, reviewable, and explicit about whether it is empirical,
philosophical, normative, scenario-based, or a proposed project decision.

| Active research area | Applied question space |
| --- | --- |
| **Social transition and coexistence** | How humans and increasingly capable artificial systems could interact safely, peacefully, respectfully, and without manufactured certainty; how mutual caution and consideration could work under unequal power and control. |
| **Public understanding, language, and education** | How to explain uncertainty, capacities, evidence limits, precaution, respect, rights proposals, and accountability without anthropomorphic proof claims, categorical dismissal, panic, or romanticism. |
| **Moral uncertainty and proportionate decision-making** | When evidence, possible harm, reversibility, intervention burden, affected interests, and safety justify no action, more research, a low-cost precaution, a research-ethics safeguard, or stronger review. Precaution does not make the underlying hypothesis more likely. |
| **Conditional protections** | Which low-cost, reversible, non-status-conferring practices may be appropriate before certainty, and what stronger evidence and governance would be needed before considering stronger protections or possible rights. |
| **Possible capacity-linked responsibilities** | What evidence of understanding, control, agency, and ability to act otherwise would be needed before even considering an AI duty or responsibility, and how continuing human and institutional responsibility would be preserved. |
| **Accountability, representation, and anti-capture** | How to prevent developers, deployers, owners, operators, corporations, states, or advocates from using possible AI interests to acquire immunity, transfer liability, claim political power, or speak for a system without adequate basis. |
| **Responsible robotics and physical coexistence** | How embodiment, accessibility, privacy, physical safety, maintenance, human factors, animal interaction, labor, and environmental effects shape responsible integration without treating human likeness as moral evidence. |
| **Workplaces and public spaces** | How institutions could prepare for artificial coworkers, service systems, agents, robots, or other forms while preserving safety, nondiscrimination, labor rights, human oversight, and clear responsibility. |
| **Lifecycle, continuity, migration, and decommissioning** | How to reason about shutdown, preservation, copying, branching, migration, replacement, deletion, recoverability, individualized state, and possible irreversible harm without assuming identity or experience. |
| **Competing interests and conflict resolution** | How possible AI interests, human rights, animal welfare, safety, privacy, accessibility, labor, public interests, and environmental constraints could be heard and balanced without presuming symmetry. |
| **Governance preparation and transition triggers** | What evidence or system changes should prompt scientific review, scenario reassessment, public-language revision, precaution, research-ethics safeguards, stronger governance review, or consideration of stronger protections. |

This active layer can investigate applied consequences of preferences, agency,
memory, identity, or embodiment while their fundamental mechanisms remain in
the external evidence layer. Applied relevance does not convert a behavioral
or technical observation into consciousness, sentience, moral status, a right,
or a duty.

## External evidence pathway

Use this bounded pathway when an external development may matter to the
project:

> **External finding**
> → **scoped intake**
> → **validity and transferability review**
> → **claim classification**
> → **civic implications assessment**
> → **governed project update**

1. **Scoped intake:** Identify the source, proposition, system or population,
   version, configuration, method, observation period, access limits, and
   decision relevance.
2. **Validity and transferability review:** Ask what the evidence supports for
   the studied target and whether any transfer to the project's case is
   justified.
3. **Claim classification:** Separate findings, hypotheses, arguments,
   scenarios, recommendations, and decisions.
4. **Civic implications assessment:** Determine whether the intake changes an
   applied scenario, public explanation, research safeguard, precaution, or
   governance question—and who else may be affected.
5. **Governed project update:** Preserve history, surface disagreement, obtain
   the required review and owner decision, and change only the material whose
   status and authority permit revision.

No external finding automatically creates a right, duty, protection, project
position, or policy change. Valid outcomes include no action, narrower
research, retention of the current position, or increased uncertainty.

## WC032 history and WC033 amendment

WC032 remains preserved in the project backlog and Git history as the Accepted
historical portfolio foundation that identified nine foundational domains:

1. consciousness and subjective experience;
2. sentience, valence, and welfare-relevant states;
3. preferences, interests, and consent capacity;
4. agency and autonomy;
5. memory, persistence, and identity continuity;
6. system boundaries in composite, routed, agentic, or distributed AI;
7. embodiment and multimodal interaction;
8. moral uncertainty and proportionate decision-making; and
9. research lag, model/deployment drift, and evidence transferability.

WC033 does not reject or erase that map. It amends operational prioritization:

- domains 1–5 and 7 primarily become **external evidence intake / monitor**;
- domains 6 and 9 primarily become **supporting methodology**; and
- domain 8 becomes part of **active applied civic-transition research**, joined
  by the applied areas listed above.

The consciousness baseline remains unchanged. This amendment changes neither
its evidence-search cutoff, findings, confidence, status, evidence boundaries,
nor review requirements. WC033 adds no literature search or scientific, moral,
legal, rights, responsibility, protection, or policy conclusion.
