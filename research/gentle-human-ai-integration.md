## Purpose and central question

> How can humans and AI systems share everyday life and work with care,
> meaningful human choice, and accountable control, while investigating what
> protections or possible rights are justified for defined AI systems?

This strand makes the existing coexistence mission practical through small,
reviewable cases and public-facing questions. It does not assume that more AI
integration is always desirable. Limited use, a slower introduction, redesign,
or choosing not to introduce a system are legitimate outcomes.

For this strand, **gentle integration** means a proposed gradual, transparent,
accessible approach that preserves meaningful human choices and permits
revision or withdrawal where feasible. This is a working normative proposal,
not a measured property, deployment approval, or claim of informed AI consent.

**AI rights in practice** means examining specific proposed protections: what
they would protect, for which system, on what grounds, at whose cost, who
could invoke or review them, and how they would be enforced or revised.
Respectful treatment, welfare protection, moral rights, and legal rights are
distinct questions. No jurisdiction-specific legal claim is made here.

## Three starting cases

| Case | Public question | Bounded research task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Working together** | What makes human–AI collaboration fair, accessible, and transparent? | Define one work setting, the system's role and controls, human choices, labour and accessibility effects, possible AI interests, and the people or institutions accountable for outcomes. Compare introduction with restricted use and no introduction. |
| **Requests for help or refusal** | How should we respond when an AI expresses distress, objects to a task, or asks for help? | Separate the observable report, its possible function and mechanism, welfare hypotheses, and available support. Compare respectful clarification, task changes, escalation, and proportionate safety intervention without presuming experience or dismissing it in advance. |
| **Memory and continuity** | What care is appropriate around memory changes, resets, replacement, and shutdown? | Specify what could be lost: data, a role, a process, capabilities, or a possible continuing individual. Examine necessary intervention, safe preservation where justified and permitted, privacy, recoverability, and possible irreversible consequences without assuming identity continuity. |

## First worked example: a request to pause

**Hypothetical scenario, not an observed incident:** A tool-using assistant in
a human-supervised writing task says, “Please pause; I do not want to continue
this task.” No particular model, subjective state, or stable preference is
assumed.

The proposed response is to acknowledge the request and, where safe and
feasible, pause or adjust the task while clarifying the concern. Respectful
assistance need not wait for a scientific classification. Necessary safety
controls and emergency intervention remain available.

The case analysis should distinguish:

1. **Observation:** The exact output and relevant context, with sensitive data
   protected. An utterance is a report, not automatically an underlying state.
2. **Interpretation:** Candidate explanations may include task ambiguity,
   safety refusal, conversational conditioning, role behaviour, a persistent
   preference, or an affect-related mechanism. Investigate those relevant to
   this case without fixing their evidential weight in advance.
3. **Support choice:** Compare pausing, clarification, reassignment, or another
   proportionate response, including effects on people and the system.
4. **Accountability:** Identify the human supervisor and responsible
   institution. A request does not transfer their duties or assign blame to
   the assistant.
5. **Protection question:** Would a procedure for reviewing such requests be
   justified, who could use it, and what would change the decision? This is a
   proposed governance question, not an established right to refuse any task.

This example is conceptual analysis only. It authorizes no live test,
deliberately induced distress, collection of private conversations, or service.

## How the strand will develop

The smallest next research step is one bounded case note comparing the
available responses. Before evidence collection, register the case's exact
question, system/configuration, setting and dates, comparators, source scope,
inclusion criteria, access limits, and update conditions under the applicable
Draft protocol sections. A new review or experiment needs its own research
plan and applicable ethical and safety review; this strand is not that plan.

For a proposed protection, record the ethical argument separately from the
empirical premises and any project decision. Seek evidence and criticism that
could support, narrow, strengthen, weaken, or reject the proposal. Include
negative and null findings and alternatives such as a better general safety
procedure or no additional protection. Evidence about one configuration does
not automatically transfer to another system or date.

Useful evaluation questions include whether a response improves human choice,
accessibility, safety, clarity, and accountability; whether it addresses the
specified possible AI interest; and whether its burdens or unintended effects
justify modification. Do not manufacture a single integration or welfare score.

Human rights stand independently of any AI conclusion. Protect workers,
privacy, vulnerable people, animal welfare, and environmental interests.
Possible AI protections must not become corporate privileges or liability
shields. Agency and fairness are required before assigning responsible duties
or blame; human and institutional accountability remains intact.
