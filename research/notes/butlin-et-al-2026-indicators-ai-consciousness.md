## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

The authors propose a rigorous method for assessing AI consciousness despite
uncertainty in consciousness science (abstract; full section locators TODO:
verify).

### Methods or reasoning

The method derives functional or architectural indicators from neuroscientific
theories, especially theories with computational-functionalist implications,
and uses observed implementation evidence to inform credences.

### Reported findings

The methodological conclusion is that theory-derived indicators allow
structured empirical progress. It does not report a validated binary test or a
new system-level experiment.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

The article foregrounds significant scientific uncertainty. Indicator validity,
theory choice, functionalism, implementation depth, and evidential weighting
remain disputed.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

Indicators are described as informing credences, not as demonstrating
consciousness. Exact full-text caveats are TODO: verify.

## Researcher's interpretation

The method is best treated as a transparent hypothesis generator. An indicator
inherits uncertainty from both the source theory and its operational mapping to
AI; multiple indicators from one theory are not independent confirmations.

## Criticisms and methodological concerns

- Theory truth and indicator validity can become circular if assessed together.
- Public architectural descriptions may not establish implemented runtime
  properties.
- Shared authors and method with the 2023 report preclude counting the two as
  independent confirmation.
- The accessible record did not permit full evaluation of the detailed
  indicator operationalizations.

## Competing explanations

| Explanation | Evidence consistent with it | Evidence that discriminates against it | Status |

## Unanswered questions

- How are indicator likelihood ratios or error rates validated?
- Which theory variants survived preregistered adversarial testing?
- What counts as implementing, rather than simulating, an indicator?

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen it:** Preregistered AI-specific validation,
  independent coding, causal interventions, and convergence across genuinely
  independent theories.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** Indicators track task design or prompting,
  fail theory predictions, or lack inter-rater reliability.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve it:** A single impressive transcript.
- **Re-review triggers:** Full Pennartz 2026 critique, independent validation,
  erratum, or a new version of the method.
