## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

The paper asks how consciousness tests should be evaluated in typical humans
and in populations where ordinary report is unavailable or unreliable
(abstract and framework, pp. 3–5).

### Methods or reasoning

It evaluates four dimensions of a consciousness test: target population,
specificity, sensitivity, and rational confidence. The argument is
methodological: validation evidence is conditional on the population and the
link between measure and target; rationale and feasibility are related
considerations rather than replacements for the four dimensions.

### Reported findings

The authors conclude that tests valid in one setting cannot automatically be
exported to another. For AI, this means a human neural correlate or report
marker needs an additional cross-substrate validation argument.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

No universal C-test or accepted gold standard is identified. A plausible
theoretical rationale can coexist with unknown sensitivity or specificity.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

Tests should be compared transparently and matched to a defined population;
uncertainty about consciousness and population differences remains.

## Researcher's interpretation

The framework is a gate on inference, not a negative result. It makes the lack
of AI-specific validation a reason for indeterminate classification, while
leaving open the possibility of future validation.

## Criticisms and methodological concerns

- The paper proposes appraisal criteria but does not validate an AI test.
- Its broad framework needs operational thresholds and error costs for each use.
- Several authors overlap the later Butlin indicator paper, reducing lineage
  independence.

## Unanswered questions

- What would establish AI-specific sensitivity when true status is unknown?
- Can convergent validity be obtained without assuming one contested theory?
- How should false-positive and false-negative costs affect use?

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen it:** Successful cross-system validation and
  preregistered dissociations from report/task capacity.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** A theory-independent measurement with
  demonstrated cross-substrate validity.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve it:** More fluent self-report alone.
- **Re-review triggers:** New AI-specific C-test validation, correction, or
  independent methodological review.
