This is a retrospective, bounded forecast-evidence review. For this note **AI-assisted research** means AI contributions inside a human-directed process; **autonomous engineering** means specified technical work completed under delegated goals; **recursive improvement** requires changes to an AI R&D process feeding into a successor process; **full RSI** follows Clark's stronger end-to-end successor-training sense. These definitions are not a field-wide taxonomy. Search/disposition: direct October URL, followed its TasteVal and Import AI 455 links; arXiv v1 and P-Zero overview; queries `P-Zero TasteVal paper 2026 experimental research taste human baseline 24 experts results` and `TasteVal 2610.06824 critique replication October 2026`. Dependent summaries and unrelated trend claims were excluded as proof of TasteVal or RSI. No model run or forecast backtest occurred.

## Temporal and system applicability

Clark's May essay is dated **2026-05-04**; October item has a **2026-10-12 dated URL**, pending independent feed/archive date check. TasteVal v1 was submitted **2026-10-05**. TasteVal tested twenty 2023–2026 releases, notably **Opus 5.5 max reasoning as Researcher with a fixed Opus 4.8 Coder**, but exact run dates and checkpoints were not established in this intake. The study's eight task configurations, tool access, Coder, monitors and one-H100 budget define the empirical target. Its publication date does not make every result current to October 17 or transferable to later systems.

The TasteVal authors explicitly report that tasks are fast to score, use one H100, have clean validation signals and no multi-agent coordination; they did not recruit the world's top researchers or measure problem selection. They report limited model elicitation and much lower cost per AI run, which could understate potential model performance. They also say strongest submissions involved shallow or moderate optimization rather than novel ideas (§4). These are mixed limitations, not a reason to zero out the observed task result.

## Researcher interpretation and evidence quality

**Moderate confidence in a narrow empirical claim:** In TasteVal's fixed Researcher/Coder setup, at least one named frontier model achieved high experimental efficiency against its recruited expert baseline. The prospective experiment loop and same Coder for humans/models are relevant strengths. Independence is limited: P-Zero designed tasks, controls withheld items, implements scoring and publishes the result; independent replication was not found. The bootstrapped interval addresses variation in sampled tasks/runs but not generalization to frontier-lab conditions. **Low confidence for extrapolating a 3-month trend** beyond the studied model releases: model-release timing, benchmark design, small task set and alternate final-performance trend matter. **Indeterminate for full RSI by 2027:** neither the newsletter nor TasteVal observed autonomous successor design, training, evaluation and deployment with sustained gains. Clark's change is a legitimate expert update, but the evidential bridge to calendar-year success is not quantified in October.

| Mechanism/alternative | Current evidence | Discriminating evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Improved experimental judgment raises AI R&D productivity | TasteVal measures better serial compute use on fixed tasks. | New private tasks with different validation/noise/scale; matched prospective human+AI teams and full cost accounting. |
| Faster benchmark optimization without broad research taste | Fixed clean tasks, missing problem choice and no final-performance trend break fit this. | Tests of valuable problem selection, ambiguous outcomes, deep novelty and long-horizon coordination. |
| Human-mediated acceleration rather than autonomous feedback | Clark's May examples and current lab claims often include human direction; TasteVal fixes the task and Coder. | Trace successive AI-proposed improvements through model-building, promotion and fresh successor evaluations, separating human contributions. |
| Self-sustaining recursive process | Existing [Weco AIDE² note](weco-aide2-recursive-research-agent-improvement-2026.md) provides bounded harness-lineage evidence, not open-ended frontier-weight improvement. | Multiple independently checked generations, positive net capability gain at matched resources, fresh tasks, failure accounting and governance control. |

The recent [TheoPhysEval note](theophyseval-astra-scientific-reasoning-2026.md) is relevant to AI scientific calculation but tests physics problem solving, not AI R&D self-improvement. The [OpenAI mathematics note](openai-mathematics-release-2026.md) records candidate outputs and corrections, not a validated RSI loop. These are distinct evidence lines; combining them as if independent tests of one mechanism would inflate support.

There is an apparent difference in expert appraisal: TheoPhysEval's physicist authors say, from unquantified experience, that current agents struggle to choose interesting problems or redirect a research agenda, whereas TasteVal reports a quantified advantage at **experiment design after a problem is supplied**. The claims concern different constructs and systems, so neither refutes the other. A shared prospective evaluation of both problem selection and experiment execution would help narrow the disagreement.

## Unresolved work and review triggers

The smallest next study is a **prospective task set** where agents and matched expert teams choose consequential AI research directions, work through ambiguous outcomes and are scored on fresh successor-system gains with complete human, compute and validation costs. Repeated gains across generations would strengthen RSI mechanisms; failure to generalize from fixed tasks, persistent human bottlenecks or regression after grader repairs would weaken the timetable inference. Additional commentary on the same TasteVal score would not discriminate. Audit task-level trajectories, Coder errors, expert recruitment, model/run dates, paper version and independent replications; document Clark's future numerical forecast if he publishes one. This note may prompt a review of the [September RSI preparedness assessment](ai-assisted-research-rsi-preparedness-2026.md) if direct evidence of a sustained loop appears; it does not itself amend that working assessment or any governance decision.
