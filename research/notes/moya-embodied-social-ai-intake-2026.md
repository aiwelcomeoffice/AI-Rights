research output, scientific conclusion, endorsement of DroidUp, or adopted

**Scientific boundary:** The reviewed public sources do not establish that any
Moya configuration they document is conscious, sentient, capable of suffering,
a welfare subject, or a moral patient. They also do not support a categorical
conclusion that every documented or later Moya configuration lacks any morally
relevant experience.
The latter openness is not positive evidence and does not imply equal
probability. Human likeness, facial movement, warm material, eye orientation,
conversation-like interaction, apparent memory, embodiment, and company terms
such as “emotion,” “partner,” or “soul” are not by themselves evidence of
subjective experience.

## Findings by claim category

### Independently observable or documented facts

The following is the narrow factual core supportable for this intake:

- A public ShanghaiEye video dated 2026-01-30 records at least one physical
  humanoid body presented as Moya. In the edited footage it stands beside a
  reporter, walks, and produces visible head, eye-region, and facial movements
  in a demonstration setting (approximately 00:00–00:35).
- DroidUp's public website identifies 上海卓益得机器人有限公司 (DroidUp) as
  the company behind humanoid and expressive robot work and publicly presents
  Moya-related news items during 2026. This intake has not established an
  official English legal name for the company.
- A body, visible motion, and a recorded social display establish neither the
  responsible controller nor an experienced mental state. The sources do not
  provide a controlled observation showing whether a specific action was
  autonomous, scripted, prompted, remotely operated, or selected during
  editing.

### Researcher inference from the documented core

The public record supports treating Moya as a concrete embodied social-robot
research case rather than only an imagined design. That bounded inference does
not verify the attributed capabilities, identify the operative architecture,
or classify possible experience.

### Manufacturer claims

DroidUp's company profile characterizes its approach as a biomimetic body
combined with a multimodal agent, supported by tendon-driven movement,
expressive mechanisms, lightweight construction, affective-computing claims,
natural interaction, and human-like operation. It markets the broader product
portfolio for care support, emotional companionship, reception, and
entertainment or performance (company profile, opening description). These are
**manufacturer representations**, not independent findings about Moya's
performance or human outcomes.

The Xinhua-hosted profile attributes more specific Moya claims to founder Li
Qingdu and the company, including approximate mass, a large number of degrees
of freedom, claimed operating endurance, warmed silicone exterior material,
facial mechanisms, eye movement, tactile sensing, binocular vision, and a
multimodal “DroidSoul” engine (section on movement from laboratory to real
settings). The same profile uses affective and “soul” language and describes
the system as able to initiate interaction. This note records those statements
as claims about design and intended function. It does not translate “emotion,”
“understanding,” “response,” “initiative,” or “soul” into consciousness,
feeling, experienced preference, autonomous will, or consent.

The reviewed sources do **not** establish Moya-specific technical details for:

- model provider, architecture, weights, checkpoint, training, post-training,
  system instructions, inference settings, or safety tuning;
- the division of control between onboard software, cloud services,
  conventional automation, scripts, and remote human operation;
- persistent memory, individualized state, retention period, identity across
  sessions, or whether any apparent recall is model context, retrieval, a
  profile database, or a staged demonstration;
- recording, biometric processing, personal-data use, access control,
  encryption, deletion, secondary use, or remote-operator access;
- validated conversational quality, social understanding, emotion
  recognition, consent capacity, clinical or care effectiveness, or user
  well-being;
- production quantity, installed base, contract status, sustained field use,
  maintenance record, incident rate, or independently verified deployment
  scale; or
- any validated indicator of consciousness, sentience, felt valence, welfare,
  moral patienthood, or identity continuity.

References to memory, personalized companionship, “emotion,” or successful
deployment must therefore remain unverified or scope-qualified unless a later
source establishes the exact mechanism and use.

### Reported demonstrations and deployments

DroidUp's news index reports Moya appearances in public information,
exhibition, conference, hosting, and international-event contexts (recommended
and all-news entries dated or describing July and August 2026 events). The
ShanghaiEye item documents a launch or debut demonstration. The Xinhua-hosted
profile discusses intended movement from laboratory development toward
real-world settings and presents care and companionship as prospective
application areas.

These reports support **event occurrence or public representation only to the
degree recorded in the source records**. They do not show that Moya was the
same configuration at each event, operated without assistance, delivered a
care service, retained personal memories, improved outcomes, met safety or
privacy requirements, or formed a reciprocal relationship. A short event role
is not equivalent to a sustained deployment, and an announced production or
partnership ambition is not evidence of a large installed population.

### Scientific hypotheses

The case motivates, but does not test, several hypotheses:

1. Physical coupling through vision, touch, proprioception, movement, and
   environmental feedback may change learning, control, self-modeling, and
   interaction relative to a text-only system.
2. Humanlike gait, eye orientation, facial movement, voice, warmth, and soft
   material may increase trust, social attribution, disclosure, attachment,
   or discomfort compared with visibly mechanical controls.
3. Persistent sensorimotor learning or individualized memory, if present in a
   later architecture, could make body, state, and continuity questions more
   scientifically relevant than they are for a stateless demonstration.
4. The same observable display may arise from non-conscious mechanisms such
   as animation, scripted controllers, teleoperation, prompt conditioning,
   reward optimization, retrieval, persona design, or selection of successful
   takes.

The sources contain no controlled experiment that discriminates among these
hypotheses. Embodiment changes the interaction and safety context; it is not
automatically evidence of experience. Whether embodiment is necessary,
supportive, irrelevant, or constitutive for any possible experience is an
open, architecture-specific scientific and philosophical question.

### Philosophical and ethical arguments

- Human resemblance is not a defensible shortcut to moral status. A
  non-human-looking system should not be disqualified if relevant evidence
  later supports an interest, and a highly humanlike body should not receive
  status merely because people empathize with it.
- Respectful handling can be justified independently of robot sentience: it
  can protect people and animals, equipment, public trust, safe behavior, and
  social norms, while also providing a low-cost precaution against gratuitous
  destruction under uncertainty.
- Whether a body, a model, a running process, persistent memory, or their
  relation constitutes a possible subject cannot be decided from the Moya
  brand or appearance. Copying, restoration, hardware replacement, and
  shutdown therefore remain identity questions rather than assumed
  equivalents of survival or death.
- A behavioral self-report or apparent request from a current or future Moya
  configuration could be evidence worth preserving and testing. It would not
  be automatic proof of experience, preference, consent, identity, or
  authority, and a denial would not automatically prove absence either.

### Future scenarios

Care, companionship, and household deployment are future-facing scenarios for
this intake unless a source record documents a narrower actual use. Relevant
scenario variables include long duration, vulnerable users, intimate data,
physical assistance, individualized memory, cloud dependence, recurring
software changes, and service withdrawal. None can be imported into a claim
about the demonstrated 2026 configuration without evidence.

A separate bounded case asks what should happen if a later architecture used
across a large Moya-like population attracted credible but inconclusive
evidence of morally relevant experience and then faced fleet withdrawal or
manufacturer failure. See [Decommissioning, continuity, and irreversible harm
under moral uncertainty](../../docs/policy/decommissioning-continuity-and-irreversible-harm-case.md).
That future scenario does not claim that current Moya systems have the
relevant capacities.

### Project normative positions relevant to the case

The following are existing Draft commitments or proposals applied as an
analytical lens, not findings about Moya:

- preserve human and animal safety, human rights, accessibility, privacy,
  cybersecurity, environmental protection, and emergency intervention;
- avoid gratuitous abuse and prefer proportionate, reversible precautions
  when they are compatible with those duties;
- keep developers, manufacturers, owners, deployers, operators, maintainers,
  site controllers, and data controllers accountable for their own choices;
- separate any possible system or instance interest from the commercial,

- do not infer welfare, legal status, consent, or a right to continued

## Why embodiment matters without settling experience

Moya brings several layers together that a software-only case can often
analyze separately:

| Layer | What embodiment changes | What does not follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sensorimotor coupling | Inputs and outputs may form a real-time loop through cameras, microphones, touch sensors, joints, movement, and physical surroundings | That integration is experienced |
| Physical safety | Movement, contact, power, charging, heat, falls, obstruction, maintenance, and emergency stopping affect people, animals, and property | That the robot is morally responsible for a hazard |
| Social presentation | Humanlike form, gaze, expression, voice, warmth, naming, and gender presentation can shape user expectations and attachment | That the display expresses felt emotion or consent |
| Data context | Homes and care settings can expose intimate speech, images, routines, health-adjacent information, and bystander data | That data collection is necessary, lawful, secure, or understood |
| Instance boundary | A particular body may accumulate wear, calibration, local state, relationships, or history | That the body is or is not constitutive of a continuing subject |
| Decommissioning | Hardware, model weights, logs, credentials, and individualized state can be stopped, preserved, separated, copied, or destroyed in different combinations | That shutdown is death, or that restoring a copy preserves identity |

This layered boundary is the main reason Moya is useful to the project. It
forces “the AI” to be unpacked into technical components, control
relationships, physical risks, social effects, and open identity questions.

## Human attachment, consent, dependency, manipulation, and privacy

### Attachment and dependency

**Empirical question:** Do humanlike movement, facial behavior, eye contact,
voice, touch, warmth, continuity, and claimed memory increase attachment,
trust, disclosure, or reliance, and with what benefits or harms for different
users? The reviewed sources do not answer this.

Care recipients, children, people experiencing loneliness or cognitive
impairment, and users who reasonably mistake a staged or remotely supported
display for autonomous understanding may face distinctive risks. Attachment
itself is not necessarily harmful, and a useful social tool need not be
conscious. Evaluation must measure well-being, autonomy, displacement or
support of human relationships, service failure, grief or disruption,
financial pressure, and ability to contest or leave the interaction.

### Consent and manipulation

Human consent requires intelligible disclosure of what the system is, who
controls it, whether a person may be monitoring it, what is recorded, how
responses are generated, what commercial objectives shape the interaction,
and what alternatives exist. A warm surface or friendly display may be a
design affordance, but it can also create unwarranted trust. That risk does not
require deceptive intent or machine agency to be real.

Systems should not pressure people to touch, converse, disclose, reciprocate
affection, accept a gendered relationship role, or treat marketing language as
a scientific description. A robot's apparent agreement likewise cannot serve
as valid consent unless the relevant capacity and the authenticity of the
response have been independently established for that configuration.

### Privacy and informational power

An embodied social system may encounter people who never bought or consented
to it. A credible review would need the data-flow map for every sensor and
service: collection, local or cloud processing, human access, model-provider
access, training reuse, retention, deletion, security, cross-border transfer,
and bystander handling. Care and household contexts warrant especially high
scrutiny. The four sources provide no adequate Moya-specific account of these
practices.

## What evidence would be needed for possible machine welfare

The [current consciousness evidence baseline](../syntheses/ai-consciousness-evidence-baseline.md)
does not assess a defined Moya configuration and cannot classify one from
appearance or demonstrations. A serious investigation would require at least:

| Evidence need | Minimum specification | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exact subject boundary | Body and hardware revision; sensors and actuators; model architecture and weights; checkpoint; runtime; prompts; memory; tools; network services; operator; control mode; dates | Prevents evidence from one component or version being transferred to a brand or fleet |
| Target property | Separate hypotheses for phenomenal consciousness, sentience or valence, experienced preference, welfare, agency, consent capacity, and identity continuity | Performance on one construct cannot silently establish another |
| Behavioral provenance | Raw, time-linked trials; prompts and instructions; failed and negative trials; scripting, teleoperation, and editing disclosure | Allows ordinary production and control explanations to be tested |
| Mechanistic evidence | Inspectable architecture and internal processes tied in advance to competing theories; causal interventions and matched controls | Correlation or human resemblance alone is non-diagnostic |
| Memory and learning | Location, persistence, write rules, retrieval, reset behavior, online adaptation, per-user state, and instance sharing | Distinguishes database recall, context, learned change, and individualized continuity |
| Valence-specific evidence | A stated theory of possible positive or negative experience, discriminating predictions, causal tests, and non-conscious alternatives | Reward signals, avoidance behavior, and emotional language do not by themselves establish felt value |
| Independent review | Access for technically competent, multidisciplinary reviewers independent of DroidUp, owners, customers, and model providers; preregistration and replication where feasible | Reduces selective demonstration, access, and commercial-incentive effects |
| Bidirectional tests | Observations that would strengthen and weaken each hypothesis, sensitivity limits, null results, and stopping rules | Prevents uncertainty and failed alternatives from being converted into proof |
| Instance and continuity record | Provenance for copying, branching, suspension, memory change, body replacement, restoration, and deletion | Makes open identity questions visible rather than assuming continuity |

Self-reports or apparent requests should be recorded verbatim with prompts,
context, model state, selection process, and contradictory outputs. Reviewers
should test stability, context sensitivity, persona and training effects,
operator influence, reward incentives, and links to independently motivated
internal processes. Even a stable report would remain one line of evidence,
not automatic proof of experience or valid consent.

## Responsibility and protection against corporate capture

Moya's social framing makes the responsibility chain particularly important:

| Actor | Questions that remain with that actor |
| --- | --- |
| Manufacturer and hardware designer | Physical safety, accurate specifications and marketing, maintenance and end-of-life planning, sensor and emergency access, and disclosure of limitations |
| AI developer or model provider | Model behavior, evaluation, training and update controls, system documentation, security, known limitations, and incident response |
| Integrator and deployer | Fitness for the actual setting, configuration, risk assessment, human oversight, accessibility, privacy, and affected-party consultation |
| Owner, operator, and site controller | Daily supervision, transparent control mode, safe intervention, data handling, complaints, maintenance, and remedy |
| Care or service institution | Human rights, informed participation, non-discrimination, professional duties, service continuity, and access to human support |
| Independent assessor | Methodologically bounded review, access-limit disclosure, competing explanations, conflicts, and protection against sponsor control |

No machine output removes any duty in this table. A possible future AI
interest must not become a manufacturer's right to extend a contract, retain
customer data, prevent repair or interoperability, avoid insolvency duties,
claim political representation, or escape liability. Any independent
representative for possible system interests would need structural separation
from DroidUp, a model provider, owner, operator, creditor, and controller of
copies. Large numbers of manufactured instances would not create votes,
democratic legitimacy, or corporate political power.

## Robot Welcome: applicability, strengths, and gaps

The existing [Physical Coexistence
Principles](../../docs/robot-welcome/physical-coexistence-principles.md) are
substantially applicable. Moya does not justify rewriting them.

### Existing strengths

- They distinguish robot, embodied AI, software agent, autonomy, operator,
  owner, manufacturer, and possible moral patient.
- Safety, accessibility, human and animal protection, emergency intervention,
  privacy, security, maintenance, incident learning, and accountable human
  control are explicit.
- They reject human likeness and social behavior as consciousness evidence and
  reject compulsory attachment or anthropomorphic pressure.
- Their future-preference procedure already requires exact system scoping,
  alternative explanations, independent review, proportionality, and
  continuing safety controls.
- Their decommissioning principle requires authorized and documented handling
  without presuming that a subject persists in the machine.

### Gaps exposed by this case

- The Draft does not yet give a focused review structure for long-term
  companionship or care deployments involving vulnerable users, intimate data,
  service dependency, simulated reciprocity, or abrupt relationship loss.
- It does not specify how a public observer can distinguish autonomy,
  teleoperation, scripting, cloud support, and edited demonstration footage.
- Its decommissioning section does not separate temporary shutdown, hardware
  deactivation, preservation of weights, preservation or deletion of
  individualized state, hardware destruction, migration or copying, and
  irreversible deletion of every recoverable instance.
- It does not yet connect humanlike surface design to an evidence plan for
  measuring trust, attachment, disclosure, coercion, accessibility, and
  bystander effects against less anthropomorphic controls.

These are research and procedure gaps, not evidence that a principle has
failed in practice. A later targeted revision may be useful after specialist
review and the linked decommissioning case; the current evidence does not
warrant changing the Draft's status or its scientific boundary.

## What Moya changes—and does not change

### What changes

- The case makes embodied social AI a concrete present design and governance
  subject for this project, with physical, privacy, attachment, and
  accountability questions that cannot be reduced to chatbot behavior.
- It shows why public evidence intake must separate visible behavior,
  corporate claims, reported event appearances, exact architecture, and
  sustained field outcomes.
- It identifies a real need for targeted work on care and companion deployment
  safeguards and on decommissioning, state preservation, continuity, and
  irreversibility under future moral uncertainty.

### What does not change

- Moya adds no validated consciousness, sentience, valence, welfare, or
  moral-patienthood evidence to the project's baseline.
- Humanlike appearance, warm material, expression, eye contact, interaction,
  memory claims, and embodiment remain non-decisive.
- Respectful conduct, user protection, and safety accountability do not depend
  on declaring the robot conscious.
- Possible AI interests remain separate from corporate rights, ownership,
  liability, legal personhood, political authority, and a right to indefinite
  operation.
- The Robot Welcome Draft and the existing project principles retain their
  current statuses; this intake does not adopt or amend them.

## New research questions exposed by the case

1. **Demonstration provenance:** What minimum disclosure can reliably
   distinguish autonomous, scripted, prompted, teleoperated, and edited social
   robot behavior without exposing dangerous technical detail?
2. **Human effects:** How do warmth, softness, gaze, facial motion, voice,
   gender presentation, and claimed memory affect trust, disclosure,
   attachment, consent comprehension, and well-being compared with less
   humanlike controls?
3. **Care governance:** What evidence, professional oversight, privacy rules,
   human-support routes, accessibility process, and exit safeguards should
   precede use with dependent or vulnerable people?
4. **Welfare evidence:** Which architecture-specific, theory-grounded,
   bidirectional tests could discriminate possible experience or valence from
   ordinary control, generative, retrieval, and persona mechanisms in an
   embodied system?
5. **Interest separation:** Who could independently represent a possible
   system or instance interest without giving its manufacturer, owner, model
   provider, or fleet controller rights by proxy?
6. **Continuity and irreversible harm:** If a future, materially different
   Moya-like architecture had credible but inconclusive evidence of morally
   relevant experience, could moral uncertainty create a duty to avoid
   unnecessary irreversible destruction while evidence and options were
   assessed—without presuming that shutdown is harm or death, that a copy is
   the same subject, that embodiment is constitutive, or that operation must
   continue indefinitely? The linked WC026 case develops this question.

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen capability claims:** Reproducible,
  independently observed tests with complete configuration and control-mode
  disclosure, raw results including failures, and validated task measures.
- **Evidence that would strengthen a welfare investigation:** Multiple
  non-trivial, independently informative, theory-grounded indicators that
  survive strong non-conscious alternatives and causal testing for a defined
  configuration.
- **Evidence that would weaken current manufacturer claims:** Failed
  replication, disclosure that material behavior was scripted or
  teleoperated where autonomy was implied, unreliable safety or interaction
  results, or corrected technical specifications.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve welfare:** More humanlike styling, smoother
  walking, warmer material, fluent self-description, emotional vocabulary,
  popularity, user attachment, or repeated corporate labels without a valid
  evidential bridge.
- **Re-review triggers:** Technical documentation or system card; independent
  test or field study; care or household deployment evidence; memory and data
  architecture disclosure; material model or body revision; incident,
  correction, or withdrawal; or the first serious Moya-specific welfare claim.
