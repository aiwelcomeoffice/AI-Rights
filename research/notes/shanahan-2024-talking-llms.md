## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

Shanahan asks how researchers and the public can speak about LLMs without
confusing generated language with literal psychological states (article body).

### Methods or reasoning

The article explains autoregressive language generation, prompt-prefix
conditioning, and pattern completion, then uses that mechanism to assess when
words such as “knows,” “believes,” or “wants” are literal, metaphorical, or
merely convenient.

### Reported findings

Its conclusion is conceptual: an LLM can produce coherent language through
learned sequence continuation. That supplies a mechanistic alternative for
psychologically interpreted output without requiring a matching phenomenal
state.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

Pragmatic intentional vocabulary can still be useful for prediction. The paper
does not show that every artificial system lacks internal states or that
language could never contribute to a broader evidence package.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

Psychological language should be used with explicit qualifications tied to
mechanism; generated statements should not be naively read as testimony.

## Researcher's interpretation

For the baseline, this source directly lowers the specificity of fluent
language and intentional-stance vocabulary because the known generation
process predicts them without requiring phenomenal experience. Extending that
analysis to self-report, emotion words, or preferences is a reviewer inference,
not a claim extracted from this article. Such outputs could contribute to
evidence if coupled to independently validated mechanisms and robust
interventions.

## Unanswered questions

- Which controlled language behaviors cannot be explained by sequence
  continuation and prompt conditioning?
- How do persistent memory and tool use change the analysis?
- Can intervention on internal mechanisms create a validated report link?

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen it:** Systematic demonstrations that language
  tracks prompt prefixes and decoding rather than stable internal variables.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** Predefined, robust report–mechanism links
  with independent cross-substrate validity.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve it:** A more moving or insistent self-report.
- **Re-review triggers:** New empirical report-validity research, major
  architecture changes, correction, or disclosure update.
