## What the source actually says

### Question or proposition

The consortium preregistered contrasting empirical predictions of GNWT and IIT
for human conscious visual perception (abstract, main text, and methods).

### Methods or reasoning

Theory proponents and experimenters agreed predictions before data collection.
Multiple sites recruited 256 participants across fMRI (120), MEG (102), and
intracranial EEG (34); post-exclusion samples were 108, 97, and 32 respectively,
with some analysis-specific samples smaller. Controlled visual stimuli and
confirmatory analyses were used. Analysis development
used one-third of the data and final tests used an independent held-out
two-thirds; per-method allocation and exclusions remain TODO: extract. This is
internal validation, not an independent replication.

### Reported findings

Results were mixed. Some observations fit parts of each theory, but predicted
sustained posterior synchronization for IIT and an expected stimulus-offset
ignition result for GNWT were not found as specified.

### Negative, null, mixed, or contrary material

The failed preregistered predictions are central to the paper's adversarial
result, but their centrality to each theory is disputed and they do not
automatically falsify an entire theory because measurement choices and
auxiliary hypotheses matter. Other findings did not cleanly discriminate the
theories.

Naccache et al.'s later [GNWT response](../sources/naccache-et-al-2025-gnwt-response.md)
argues that some findings supported GNWT, that the design did not include the
central conscious-versus-non-conscious contrast, and that offset ignition was
not an unconditional core prediction in the tested conditions. This is a
retrospective theory-proponent interpretation of the same record, not new data
or an independent replication.

### Authors' conclusions and caveats

The paper reports challenges to key tenets of both accounts and emphasizes
theory testing. It does not extrapolate a direct result about AI consciousness.

## Researcher's interpretation

This is indirect but important evidence: if AI indicators are derived from
GNWT or IIT, uncertainty and disconfirmation in the originating human theories
must propagate into the AI inference.

## Criticisms and methodological concerns

- Negotiated theory predictions may not represent every variant.
- Neural modalities impose resolution limits and null-result sensitivity issues.
- The adversarial program shares paradigms and funding even though it uses
  multiple sites/methods.
- Human findings are not AI validation data.

## Competing explanations

## Independence and source conflicts

- **Evidence-lineage dependencies:** Theory proponents participated; consortium
  includes many researchers but shares one coordinated program. Dehaene
  coauthored both Cogitate and the later GNWT response; the response's other
  authors include GNWT developers or proponents.
- **Funding and affiliations:** Templeton World Charity Foundation grants
  TWCF0389 and TWCF0486 and Max Planck Society support are disclosed. Koch and
  Tononi disclosed Intrinsic Powers board/financial interests; Koch a Tiny Blue
  Dot Foundation role; Tononi a patent; and Dehaene a patent and NeuroMeters
  association. See the source record for identifiers; the effect of these
  interests and the funder role remains independently unassessed.
- **Control of data, system access, and publication:** Consortium controls the
  human datasets; availability statements are public.
- **Effect on interpretation:** Adversarial design strengthens the result, but
  no single experiment ends the theory dispute.

## Unanswered questions

- Which predictions replicate independently outside Cogitate?
- How should partial disconfirmation alter theory-derived AI indicators?
- Which measurements were sufficiently sensitive to predicted effects?

## What would change this interpretation

- **Evidence that would strengthen it:** Independent exact/conceptual
  replications and successful cross-modal disconfirmation.
- **Evidence that would weaken it:** Demonstrated analysis/measurement failure
  or preregistered recovery of central predictions.
- **Evidence unlikely to resolve it:** Post hoc accommodation without new tests.
- **Re-review triggers:** Replication, correction, data reanalysis, or formal
  theory revisions.
