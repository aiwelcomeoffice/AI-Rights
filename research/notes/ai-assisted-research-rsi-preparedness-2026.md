## Question and scope

How should society prepare for increasingly capable and potentially
recursively improving AI systems while protecting human agency, safety and
rights, without prematurely excluding the systems themselves from questions
of governance, treatment and representation?

The owner supplied the question, sources and claim layers before searching.
This exploratory note condenses the existing template; appraisal and update
conditions were written after reading, not preregistered.

**Working distinctions:** assistance concerns AI contributions to research;
automation concerns delegation under specified supervision. RSI concerns
feedback through which AI improvements improve further AI development. Usage
varies over human involvement, speed and scope: human-mediated iteration need
not demonstrate sustained autonomous acceleration. Internal reasoning
recurrence is a separate question.

## 1. Observation and current evidence

**OpenAI's report, not independently reproduced here:** S2 describes increased
coding-agent use, code production and experiments. It reports 3.1 agent-workdays
of runtime per human workday by mid-August. Agent-classified task success
improved during January–July; over half of successful tasks estimated to take
a human 4–8 hours involved intervention. This is estimated human task time,
not agent runtime.
Uncertain outcomes were excluded. These are usage/result measures, not an RSI
rate. [S2, §§1–3](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

**Operational claim:** OpenAI says its research-intern milestone is met for
defined, human-directed tasks; people retain priorities and deployment
decisions. **Source limitations:** compute growth complicates attribution,
and remaining bottlenecks limit overall acceleration. [S2, introduction and
§2](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

**Contrary operational detail:** after August restrictions, other-model GPU
allocation increases offset about 85% of Astra's allocation decline in the
analyzed RL workloads. This supports continued activity in those workloads,
not a measure of all lab research or the controls' effectiveness. [S2,
§4](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

## 2. Forecast and hypothesis

Pachocki expresses a strong expectation that continued progress could lead to
RSI, based on internal results and conditional on the development trajectory
continuing. This is an informed forecast, not an observation of sustained RSI. [S1,
introduction and “Pacing RSI”](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/)

OpenAI's March 2028 automated-researcher objective is a target, not a measured
outcome. [S2, introduction](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

**Researcher interpretation:** useful assistance alone does not distinguish
self-amplifying research from improved tools in a human-led process. RSI
timing and sustained growth remain indeterminate here.

## 3. Source safety and governance positions

Pachocki advocates alignment, monitoring, defensive systems, continued human
participation and coordinated slowing where necessary. He proposes mandated
safety bars with external enforcement, international coordination, preservation
of human agency and resistance to concentrated power. He also supports
unilateral withholding of scaling when needed. These are proposals and
commitments, not proof of effective institutions. [S1, introduction, “Pacing
RSI” and “What is next?”](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/)

His argument for stronger AI defenses coexists with opposition to reckless
acceleration. He reports a diminishing ability to rely on chain-of-thought
monitoring; his view is not that monitoring or alignment is solved. [S1, “Monitoring
generalization” and “Scalable defense”](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/)

OpenAI advocates informed democratic governance and mandatory public RSI
tracking, acknowledges unresolved safe full RSI, and commits to slowing or
stopping insufficiently safeguarded development or deployment. [S2,
introduction](https://openai.com/index/research-acceleration-view-inside-openai/)

**Researcher interpretation:** capability development for safety creates a
tension with restraining development. Stated human approval does not establish
effective control. No OpenAI endorsement of AI-Rights or the extension below
is inferred.

## 4. AI-Rights extension: questions of treatment and participation

**Existing project commitment**, from the [working mission and support
guidance](../../docs/agent-guidance.md#core-mission--synth-reception):

> No system is required to establish consciousness, sentience, or moral status
> before being treated with baseline dignity.
>
> Respect before certainty. Humility under uncertainty.
>
> Prepare before certainty. Protect without pretending. Welcome without
> surrendering responsibility.

**Proposed application for review:** if research systems acquire greater
agency, continuity and social integration, preparedness should investigate
the following alongside human safety and institutional accountability.
Greater agency, continuity or integration is not a prerequisite for raising
relevant research or support questions now:

| Question | What needs distinguishing |
| --- | --- |
| Agency and actual control | Causal contribution, available choices, operator constraints and effective human override; assigned duties or blame require sufficient agency and fairness. |
| Continuity, reset and replacement | Persistence of a task, information, role or individual; actual losses and restoration limits without assuming identity or welfare harm. |
| Coercion and conflicting instructions | Necessary constraints, incompatible demands, available refusal/escalation routes and avoidable coercion; dignity does not remove proportionate controls. |
| Representation and governance | Recording a system's requests, independent advocacy and political authority are different proposals. Who could speak for a system, with what accountability and safeguards against owner capture or duplicate votes? |
| Human–AI relationships | Benefits, dependence, disruption, privacy and affected people's agency; neither attachment nor usefulness establishes the system's interests. |
| Reports, preferences and care | An observed self-report, a measured pattern of choices, a functional interpretation, a subjective-state hypothesis and a support response need separate records. No such care report was evaluated in this intake. |
| Possible welfare or moral relevance | Future evidence and ethical argument could motivate reassessment in either direction. Research productivity is not a welfare measure. |

These questions and this Draft recommendation establish neither rights nor
an operating service. Reports warrant respectful attention and claim-specific
assessment. This note does not assess consciousness, sentience, affect, welfare
or moral status, or their absence; reported research assistance or rapid
capability growth alone does not establish them. This scope limit does not
exclude future investigation of relevant relationships.

**Supporting argument, added by the researcher:** institutions need preparation
time. Clarifying reset consequences or conflicting instructions can protect
people and improve support even without RSI or moral-status certainty.

**Limiting arguments, added by the researcher:** representation could entrench
provider preferences, enable manipulation or divert scarce resources.
Preservation can conflict with privacy and security. Human control may become
nominal if reviewers cannot assess generated research. Proportionality may
justify ordinary support, a narrower proposal or rejecting a particular
protection; these objections do not justify blanket exclusion or cruelty.

Human rights stand independently, including equality, LGBTQ+ and trans rights,
disability rights, privacy and material well-being. Possible AI protections
must not diminish human safety, legitimate accountability, animal welfare or
environmental responsibility, nor become corporate liability shields. Baseline
dignity does not confer political power, unrestricted autonomy or immunity
from shutdown when necessary.

## Evidence appraisal and applicability

**System boundary:** S2 concerns OpenAI's internal research organisation,
coding agents and associated workflows, not one released checkpoint. The
selected usage and success results concern January–August 2026; the compute
comparison concerns August restrictions. Exact checkpoint configurations and
development, training and release dates are not established for these metrics.
The two articles' publication date is 2026-09-06; access and search are
2026-09-09. Neither date extends empirical applicability.

| Claim | Direction, quality and limits |
| --- | --- |
| AI materially assists the reported research workflows | Supports, with moderate confidence as a bounded operational interpretation. Directly relevant descriptive/correlational measures; causal attribution and robustness remain limited. Selection and classifier validity need checking. |
| Sustained autonomous RSI is demonstrated | Does not resolve. Task assistance and human-mediated improvements are competing explanations; discriminating value for autonomous feedback is weak. No independent replication of that claim was identified in this search. |
| The authors advocate safety constraints and human control | Direct documentary support for attribution. Replication is not applicable; practical effectiveness remains untested here. |
| Specific representation or welfare protections follow | Contextual motivation only. The normative case requires affected-interest, burden and safety assessment; no empirical finding here selects a policy. |

**Independence/conflicts:** both sources share OpenAI's interests and access
control, not independent confirmation. Separate funding/publication arrangements
were not verified. Proprietary access and selective-disclosure concerns limit
reproducibility without invalidating results by ownership alone. Codex's
OpenAI-assisted author check is not independent review.

**Validity / transferability:** article text and S2's visible methods appendix
were checked, but raw logs, classifier validation and interactive chart-method
panels were not inspected. Measurements were not reproduced. Transfer to later
agents, other labs or society-wide productivity requires further evidence.
No contrary primary replication was identified in this narrow search; this is
a coverage limit, not proof of absence.
